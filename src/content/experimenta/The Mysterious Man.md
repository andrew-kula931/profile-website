_Markus threw a dart with relatively little strength towards a large, red balloon against a wall of other similarly sized balloons. This game was supposed to be rigged somehow to make the vendor money, but Markus couldn't seem to figure out how. With each stroke of his arm, another balloon popped, and his prize pool got larger and larger. Sure the darts were extremely dull, and the balloons were particularly tenacious in material, but Wilbur's throwing techniques worked a little too well._

_"Okay!" The man in striped yellow and purple pants shouted as he blocked the way of Markus throwing another dart, "You won the top prize, take it and get out before you win everything else."_

_The man promptly pulled down a pink hippopotamus plushy about the size of a small child. He then pushed it over the counter and into Markus' arms._

_"Now go bother someone else."_

_Markus went along with the vendor's directions and began to walk down the dirt road with countless games on either side and even more people participating in them. He then handed the soft stuffed animal over to Monica._

_"Here you go," he said, trying to sound chivalrous but not too cringe._

_"Thank you, you didn't have to piss off that guy by hitting every single time," Monica said with a smile as she hugged the oversized plushy._

_"He just has a business to uphold, I get it," Markus reasoned, "I don't see how doing my best should upset him so much, but it's whatever."_

_Monica giggled, "You always make things like that look so easy. I'm honestly jealous."_

_Markus shrugged his shoulders with a smile to match hers. That's when her eyes fixated on another attraction of the carnival. A massive circular machine with two man carriages littering the circumference of the circle. The Ferris wheel had a very short line which only added to its intrigue as Monica started to linger towards it. Before too long, they were sitting together inside one of the little carriages—a pink hippo seated on Monica's lap. Their carriage moved quickly to the top before slowly drawing to a halt._

_"The green team helped clean up the side of the highway the other day," Monica explained rather enthusiastically, "I'm really glad I joined the club. Everyone there is so supportive and driven."_

_"Oh yeah?" Markus prompted as he split his attention between her and the immaculate view of the city._

_"Chelsey went down into this ravine to get some plastic bottles but then all of a sudden a rabbit sprinted out of a little hole. It scared her so bad she slipped and fell into the mud. The first thing she said when she got up was 'That rabbit got out of there faster than I did when I found out my boyfriend was cheating'," Monica giggled as Markus stared at her dumbfounded. She quickly noticed this trend and set it right, "Oh no, her boyfriend was super toxic so it's a good thing she left."_

_Markus glanced out of the carriage overlooking the carnival. People looked like ants walking around the colorful booths buying food or trying their luck at a game. Beyond the carnival were the pretty lights of the city. Each individual light looked like a speckle of glitter upon a mosaic canvas. It was utterly breathtaking and made Markus strangely sentimental. It sort of reminded him of the view from the Burj Khalifa._

_Monica slowly rapped her arm around Markus' and leans her head against his shoulder, "So many of my friends have gotten boyfriends and broke up within a few months," Markus' attention to perked up as her tone somehow matched his emotions perfectly, "After watching them, I was so afraid of starting a relationship. I was worried that I would invest all this time and effort into someone who would just up and leave me when they got bored. Not to mention what would happen if I couldn't bring myself to break the relationship if I discovered it was toxic," Markus stares at her as she looks into his eyes, "Well, I'm glad I gave you a chance."_

_They stared into each other's eyes for a moment, simply enjoying the other's company. No word needed to be said, yet an entire soap opera of dialogue was exchanged. The warmness within his heart told him that this is what being in love felt like._

_"What makes our relationship different from everyone else at school?" Monica asked after a while as she returned her head to his shoulder._

_Markus took a moment to think about a response before answering, "Before I moved back to America, I knew this one guy. In some ways he was my father away from home, in others, just a simple leader. Either way, he told me words that I will never forget. He said: 'Not everyone needs to like you. Some never will no matter how hard you try. That being said, when you find someone you trust who trusts you too. Hold them close and protect that, because trust is not easily gained nor replaced.'"_

_"Those are wise words," Monica commented._

_"Yeah," Markus agreed, remembering the face of the man who said them. A face he will likely never see again. It's almost enough to make him shed a tear… almost._

_If only he knew that this one, beautiful scene would be the last chance he ever got to see Monica's smile pointed in his direction. He would soon be reminded that a solider can leave a war, but the war will never leave the soldier._

Markus expected his hands to bound or his equipment to be stripped when he wakes up, but when he finds his pistol in its holster, he finds himself ready to draw it and fire. Then the pain comes in, and suddenly every single movement is incredibly excruciating. In light of this, he decides to simply remain still and inspect the surroundings with his eyes alone.

Surrounding him is the dark walls of a utility shed. Rakes, hoes, shovels, and a plethora of other various tools litter shelves all around him. As his eyes verge further towards the ground he notices a large wolf spider peeking out from underneath a bag of mulch. The tiny creature bolts out of sight as a voice speaks in the distance.

"I'm trying," the voice says.

Markus looks down towards his feet to find the cracked doorway of the shed. Outside he spots the back of a black leather jacket. Who the man is talking to, Markus can't tell, but he doesn't hear another voice speak—only the man's.

After a lengthy pause, the man eventually says, "No."

Another pause ensues, then he says, with a bit of annoyance, "I'm not doing it in spite of you! It's _because_ of you. Why don't you see that?"

No one responds to his outburst.

"I'm sorry Blake," he says softly, almost inaudibly. 

It's at that moment that the man shifts and a rogue glance pierces through the slit in the door. Pivoting out of his conversation, he steps up to the door and swings it open. Only the man appears on the other side.

"So you're awake," he comments coldly, any emotion he may have had suddenly vanishes at the opening of a door.

"Right," Markus grunts, finding it exceedingly hard to speak.

The man steps over and crouches down next to him. Underneath his clean, black jacket is a band with his oddly shaped knife. The entirety of the man's body is covered in clothing save the hands and face but from what he can see, he notices ample small scars on his hands and a single mark across the bottom of his left cheek.

"Who... are you?" Markus says slowly, each word requiring a great deal of care to avoid his ribs from making his entire consciousness to shake.

The man's eyes narrow, "You... you can call me," he looks out the door at nothing in particular, after a moment he returns his gaze to Markus, "You can call me Experiment T."

Laying on the floor is nice and all but Markus can't help but feel pathetic as the man literally crouches overtop of him. To save his pride, he accepts the onset of pain as he forces himself into a seated position and leans against the nearby bag of mulch. The cool surface makes him shiver as his bear back comes into contact with it. The tiny prickles of wood shavings makes him gasp slightly as they press into this burnt back.

"So you're... an Experiment?" Markus wonders.

The man stands up fully before leaning against the opposite wall with his arms crossed, "Yes."

Markus looks around at the arbitrary shed. He hopes to gain some understanding of the situation, but he assumes that he must not have been passed out for long. After all, he's still shirtless, the burns on his back are still fresh, and the cut across his shoulder still looks like it'll start bleeding profusely if he moves too quickly.

"Didn't expect you to... save me," Markus comments with a half-hearted smile.

The man, whose attention had drifted towards the door, glances back, "I wanted to know what happened to you. You're not an Experiment... and it seems like you're not going to be."

Markus takes a deep breath, "How do you... know that?"

Experiment T instinctively reaches for his pistol but consciously decides against it and lets his hand rest at his side, "You failed both criteria."

"Criteria?" Markus repeats.

The man dodges the question by simply staring at him without saying a word. The uncomfortable silence is only ended when Experiment T decides what topic he wants to return to.

"What happened to you?"

Markus closes his eyes for a moment, envisioning the events that just transpired. Does he tell this stranger everything—the same man who nearly killed him multiple times—or does he keep things as vague as possible? If he's not careful, this guy might call him out for lying, and he's in no position to argue nor defend himself. Perhaps the only available option is to incorporate a reserved truth.

"Experiment A was inside of me," Markus begins before taking another slow breath, "Doctor Crane removed it and... you saw what happened."

Experiment T slides down the wall until he's seated just as Markus is. He glances out the door in thought.

"What did that regenerating white mutant call itself?"

Markus offers a quizzical look before he makes the connection, "That was Harvey. He's just a cultist."

Experiment T nods in thought.

Arriving at yet another moment of silence, Markus checks his personal inventory. His pistol and knife are still where they should be along his belt, he still has a utility knife on his side pocket, and his phone is still in his back pocket. That's all he had on him before passing out, so it stands to reason that this man hasn't even touched his belongings.

For his next move, Markus decides to approach it with a bit of blunt honesty. If he can articulate his thoughts correctly, he might prompt this man into clarifying things for him. 

"Well, it seems I'm in no position to do much," Markus articulates carefully as he stares directly at the man, "So what now?"

Experiment T doesn't move as he simply bites the inside of his cheek and take notice of something below Markus. With a quick glance, Markus finds the wolf spider dashing across the floor towards another corner of the shed.

"I'm not sure..." Experiment T trails off, "I thought you'd be dead by now."

Markus rests a hand at his side as he slowly adjusts himself in place. He has to be very careful not to shift too quickly lest his lungs cry out in pain.

"Pardon me for being blunt, but I still don't trust you."

Experiment T looks up sharply at the comment. To Markus' surprise, the look isn't aggressive nor assertive: it's genuine shock. This abnormality is swiftly rectified as Experiment T closes his eyes and falls back into his indifferent demeanor. 

"Right," Experiment T comments to himself.

"So I have to know," Markus says, "You label yourself as an Experiment and Harvey as a cultist—I would too. But I still don't understand, how dangerous are these Experiments? And is the city in danger?"

Opening his eyes slowly, Experiment T sighs, "Experiments are..." he looks away, "Drew told me once that you either kill a cultist or become one. Experiments are just a more extreme form."

Markus' eyes widen, "Drew? As in Drew Wilbur! You said his name before, do you know him?"

Experiment T looks up again, this time with a particularly cold look in his eyes, "Did. He died three years ago."

Markus leans back. It was honestly too good to be true. Sure Jenks came back despite all odds, but to think that another member of his former squad has survived that ordeal. If only they could have seen one another before the apocalypse started—before things went south. He would have liked to thank Wilbur for all the skills he learned from him. It's a damn shame that will never happen.

"I see," Markus comments quietly as he tries to take a deep breath but promptly regrets it, "So is the city in danger?"

Experiment T goes silent for a moment, clearly thinking the question through, "Probably."

Clenching his fit a bit, Markus forces himself to stand up. The pain all across his body rages and makes his head spin, but he knows he has to move and pain alone isn't enough to stop him. So long as his rip doesn't puncture his lung, he'll be alright.

"Are you going to try and save it?" Experiment T wonders as he watches the injured man stand on his own two feet, "...the city?"

It's uncertain whether he'll be able to even make it home, but Markus isn't about to let his life mission to exterminate the Cult just end here. It'll take him time to heal, it'll be a long, difficult process, but he'll get back to his feet and take out Doctor Crane, Harvey, and every other cultist. Even Experiment T, who seems to be more helpful than he lets on, will likely get in his way. When the time comes, Markus will think of some way to defeat him, no matter what it costs. He simply doesn't have the knowledge to win yet.

"I do want to know something," Markus asks she leans against the doorway cradling his rib cage and looking back at the seated Experiment, "How exactly do I defeat an Experiment?"

Experiment T closes his eyes and turns away, "Why do you want to know?"

Without a second thought, Markus tightens his grip on the door frame, "I'm going to destroy everything the Cult built."

A long moment of silence elapses before the man looks back with an unyielding gaze, his eyes honing in and the weakened soldier before him, "You can't."

"What do you mean I can't," Markus interjects before groaning in pain.

Experiment T promptly rises to his feet, walks straight past Markus into the yard, and stands a few paces away with his back turned, "You can kill a cultist, destroy a facility, but an Experiment... they're a force of nature."

Markus narrows his eyes at the "Experiment". Sure this man has demonstrated uncanny skill, and maybe that's understandable given everything he knows about these Experiments, but words still feel conceited. No one is perfect. Markus has faced countless overwhelming obstacles, both before the apocalypse and after, but he's always found a way to win. The path to victory has always been grueling and difficult, but he's certain that a solution always exists—it just depends on how much you're willing to give up.

"I don't believe that," Markus counters as he stands up a bit straighter, "I'll find a solution."

With a cold, calculated expression, Experiment T glances back, "Sure."

After that, the man begins to walk away. Perhaps this could have been the end of their little conversation. Perhaps Markus could have let him go without asking about the casino, but then he notices where the man is headed: towards Markus' bike. 

Frustration flares up alongside the pain as he starts limping forward. He debates calling out the man as he picks up the toppled over bike, but the chest injury prevents any more than a soft whisper while stumbling forward. After all that just happened, is this man really about to steal his only form of transportation?

Then Experiment T stops and looks back to Markus expectantly. With both hands holding the bike up, he seems to be waiting for Markus to get closer.

"What are you doing?" Markus wheezes as he finally arrives besides the bike.

Experiment T looks down at the motorcycle, "Did you plan to drive?"

Markus offers an incredulous look. Sure he's in a compromised position, but why in the hell would he continue to put his life into this Experiment's hands. Even if it does cost him a bit of his blood, he's not going to let this guy drive his motorcycle.

"I've modified it," Markus begins rather spitefully, "you wouldn't know how to drive it, even if you tried."

Experiment T overlooks the bike quizzically for a moment. Then after a brief, and honestly rather shallow inspection, the man reaches over to the ignition and twists the keys three times—exactly as he rigged it to work. The next step of the process is the least intuitive so he'll never know—he does it perfectly. Markus' jaw nearly drops as the man look back over expectantly.

"How'd you..." Markus stutters. He's never told anyone how to start up his bike, and he's careful not to telegraph the process either so eavesdroppers can't figure it out. Despite that, this Experiment manages to figure it out on the first try.

"Get on," the man commands as he slides as far forward on the motorcycle as he possibly can, "I'll bring you home."

Every survival instinct scream for Markus to run, but then he looks off towards the distant city skyline, and suddenly he doesn't want to walk anymore. Well... it seems he'll either need to entrust his very life to this man or die walking. 

Markus grits his teeth, "Fine. Let's go."

The motorcycle is a particularly difficult vehicle to operate as its balance is a little off and the acceleration is faster than most other bikes of its class. Not only that, he's never been good at maintenance, so the bike has a bad habit of stalling if it drives too slowly for too long. These factors alone cost Markus two weeks of efficiency as he struggled to control the motorcycle well enough to get from point A to B without worry of a parasite catching up.

All of these difficulties that Markus thankfully overcame, don't even come up as the man effortlessly turns corners and accelerates without issue. He even manages to weave between broken-down cars at high speed—which nearly gives Markus a heart attack. Before too long they arrive at their destination: a hospital.

Just outside the downtown area is the city's largest medical center with a campus of three distinct buildings, the largest of them being twenty stories tall. The place was overrun with the injured and sick at the onset of the Fall and, as such, was quickly overrun with parasites. The front doors have been boarded up for years and no one dare enter for the same reasons that the Mall was labeled as a lost cause.

Despite the shattered windows on the first and second floors, a crashed car in the entryway, and obvious preludes to a hive, Experiment T decides it's a good idea to stroll right inside. Markus hesitates to follow, but it becomes an inevitability as the man waits inside the lobby waving him in. 

"What are we doing?" Markus comments, "This place is a hive."

Experiment T glances over his shoulder, "That didn't stop you from going to that Mall."

Markus recoils defensively, but promptly wheezes as the gesture causes his chest to throb, "That was... different."

The man doesn't offer a reaction as he continues deeper into the dimly lit building. 

They traverse the first hallway for about ten seconds before the first parasites reveals itself. An Infected class with no arms steps out of a random room and spots them immediately. It breaks into a violent charge it tramples over an empty wheelchair and nearly falls over entirely. 

Experiment T pays no attention to it as he nonchalantly slices his knife through its head splitting the skull in two pieces. He then resheaths his knife in one fluid motion before checking on Markus to ensure he's still behind him.

"What do you hope to gain here?" Markus asks, suppressing the urge to groan or wheeze despite every single movement causing immense pain. 

With a quick glance, the man continues on without answering the question.

Two Slender class parasites appear in the hallway and attempt to assault the two strangers, but they too fail to get close as Experiment T cleans them up. Both parasites try to use their speed to avoid the inevitable slash, but the man seems to predict their movements and leave the blade waiting for them when they eventually get too close. This process continues for another ten parasites of miscellaneous classes. 

Eventually they arrive at their true destination.

Experiment T tries to open a door labeled storage but it doesn't budge. The handle twists, but that's the end of it. The man doesn't contemplate his next decision as he lifts his foot into the air and slams it into the door. The attack shakes the door in place, but the stubborn barrier refuses to give. Experiment T tries again to no avail, but after a third attempt the door finally gives and a pile of boxes fall on the other side. He proceeds in without a second thought.

Inside the storage room is the familiar sight of white shelves with boxes, bins, and labels of a plentiful variety. In all honest, it's quite like the pharmacy Markus looted in the Mall.

On the floor beside the toppled pile of boxes is a mostly decayed skeleton. The skull seems human, but everything below that is an amalgamation of half-baked ideas. An elongated arm, a leg bent backwards, and the beginnings of a structured tail. It's not every day that he witnesses the skeletal remains of a parasite, but he guesses there has to be a first for everything.

"What exactly are we searching for?" Markus asks while grimacing.

Experiment T walks the shelves for a moment before stopping and reaching into a large plastic bin. Markus can't quite tell what's inside, that is, until the man pulls a small vial out and tosses it over. The side of the vial quickly reveals itself to be morphine.

"We came all the way out here for this?" Markus asks incredulous. Sure a pain suppressant will make his life considerably more pleasant, but what could this man gain from something like this.

Markus never receives an answer to his inquiry as Experiment T promptly walks over to another section of the storage room and scrounges through another bin. He reads them over quite carefully, offering a confused and speculative look here and there. Eventually he decides on something and tosses it over to him.

On this particular vial is the label: "Cefuroxime".

"What is this?" Markus possess.

The man glances back, "Antibiotics."

Studying the peculiar vial one more time, Markus pockets it, "I've never heard of it, how do I know that it'll actually help?"

Experiment T looks back sharply before continuing to another section of the storage room and rummaging through yet another bin. 

The man eventually sighs, "There's a log in the other room," he says, almost to himself, "It said that stuff was used on someone with a severe infection."

Markus looks around at the cramped storage room. With only one door leading in and out, there is no 'other room' to speak of. Not only that, but he's been with this guy for the entire time and never saw him look at any records or logs. His explanation only puts Markus further on edge.

The man eventually finds what he was looking for and tosses a pouch of fluid over to him. Luckily, this medical crème is something Markus is very familiar with: aloe vera. 

"So we really came out here just for medical supplies?" Markus surmises as he reorganizes the two vials in his pocket.

Experiment T stands up and walks right past Markus towards the hallway. Once outside he promptly throws his knife towards the ceiling causing something to crash to the floor. As Markus pokes his head outside, he finds a crab looking parasite with its arms curled upwards and a knife sticking straight through its exoskeleton.

The man retrieves his knife by kicks the parasite's carcass over and ripping the blade out with a fluid motion. He then places the knife back into it's place under his jacket and start off down the hall. 

Not wanting to find out what happens if he's left alone, Markus hustles out of the storage room with the drugs in hand.

"There were so many supplies back there," Markus almost grunts as he struggles to keep up, "We could have taken some."

Experiment T doesn't look back.

They exit out the way they came in while only encountering a single parasite. When they eventually do escape the building, the man takes a seat on the motorcycle and awaits Markus without a word. Markus, however, doesn't go along with the stunt any longer. Instead of compliance, he stands his ground a few steps from the bike.

"You need to answer me," he asserts directly, "What are you trying to achieve?"

Experiment T looks around at the tall buildings around them. His gaze dwells on one of the distant skyscrapers for an uncomfortable amount of time.

"We're not enemies," he responds simply.

Markus shakes his head, "That doesn't answer my question."

The man looks up to the sky, for some reason, Markus gets the feeling that he's studying the clouds. For a moment he almost appears nostalgic—that sentiment fades quickly.

"I guess..." the man begins slowly, "I was relieved... that I was wrong. You're not an Experiment candidate."

Markus takes a deep breath. With his injures and the subsequent pain, his patience is running dangerously thin. Despite that fact, he knows he can't grow impatient lest this Experiment lose his own temper. Having to suck up to someone pisses Markus off to no end—especially when he has zero respect for said individual—but then again, he wouldn't have these drugs if it weren't for this guy. The conflicting evidence makes Markus' headache grow into a minor migraine.

"I still don't understand," Markus reiterates, "But at this point, it seems like you won't tell me. I'll just heal up, and then I'll be after that Doctor Crane again. That's my plan."

Experiment T turns away from the sky briefly, "Sure."

"And what's your plan?"

The man reaches forward and rests his hands on the motorcycle handles, "I'm not sure."

Markus waits for some sort of clarification, yet it unsurprisingly never comes. Every future attempt at a question will invariably return this same result, so he gives up on that. It might be better to just accept the help for now—at least until he injects this morphine.
