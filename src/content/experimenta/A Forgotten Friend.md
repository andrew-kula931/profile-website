The location Markus had a hunch about is in, what used to be, the slums of Pearson. The region south of downtown was the epicenter of crime before the Fall. Now it was but a forlorn ghost town with nothing of interest and more Infected than necessary for one place.

Markus is only able to get six blocks from his destination before hordes of Infecteds cover the streets and make them impassable. On a standard day, parasites wouldn't be loitering out on the street, but by the look of things, some unfortunate animals got surrounded in the middle of the street. What those animals were exactly, it's impossible to say with how strung out their remains are.

His workaround for this roadblock is simply to parkour across the rooftops of the buildings lining the streets. It is a fairly tight stretch so getting from one rooftop to the next is relatively easy. As he runs and jumps, however, he finds himself stumbling a lot more than usual as his movements are strained by his sore body. The spinning of his head and the tightening of his muscles only seem to get increasingly severe the further he travels, which forces him to take a short break.

He stops just shy of his destination. Further ahead, he finds the roadblock as annoying as ever. Scattered on the street below is no least than two hundred Infected. Searching the area is not going to be easy, nor possible for that matter, without a serious distraction. 

He takes a seat away from the edge of the roof and checks his things. The loudest thing he has is a frag grenade, but it isn't worth wasting one of his few grenades for a time like this. He will have to be more creative. He pulls out his phone and checks a map of the city that he screenshotted a while back and checks for any location he can use to his advantage. He could… 

His train of thought is abruptly interrupted by a sudden pain shooting through his torso and his migraine exploding in intensity. It's as if something is actively crawling around inside of him. The pain grows and grows, all the while Markus has to use every last bit of willpower to avoid screaming. 

Slamming his fist into the roof of the building, he rolls over onto his hands and knees—every part of him growing dull. He can feel a void creeping towards him as his vision tunnels. The advanced breathing technique does nothing as the pain only pushes further and further into his consciousness. 

What the hell is happening? The pain doesn't cease, it only expands. His vision became blurry.

Is this it?

Everything goes dark.

Markus awakens with his face in the gravel of the same roof he passed out on. The pain is thankfully gone and the sun shines a warm evening light only an hour or two before dusk. In front of his face is a splatter of blood and saliva which he can only conclude is his own.

To think that awful memory had to resurface. He was beginning to think that his memories of that stage of his life had been completely erased... it seems that's not the case. Alas, there's nothing he can do but get up and take another step—at least that's what Grant would have said.

He lifts himself up slowly and looks around only to be faced with the grotesque figure of a parasite. He immediately draws his pistol and leaps back ready to fight. The parasite growls slowly before turning away dismissively. With a spider-like figure, no head, and seven oddly legs, Markus struggles to classify it. He waits for any sign of attack, but it proceeds to completely disregard him and walks away. Dumbfounded, Markus holsters his pistol and watches it jump from the roof. 

Admittedly, the parasite had no head but why did it not see him? That doesn't make sense, but then again, nothing makes sense these days. 

Checking in on himself, he finds the soreness all across his body completely gone, his migraine has all but subsided, and every subconscious function has seemingly returned to normal. The entire situation is incredibly peculiar, yet he doesn't have the first idea of what could be happening.

With no actionable ideas, he advances to the edge of the roof and rests his foot on the old sign on the building. The Infected below are still on the street which makes Markus sigh. Then as his amazing luck would have it, the sign he is leaning on gives way and he plummets off the building. Ordinarily he'd have the reflexes to catch himself, but this time his reflexes are just a bit too delayed. 

He lands from the one story fall with a roll and prepares for whatever comes next. 

The first thing he sees is the face of a humanoid Infected staring at him. There is no anger in its expression, no pain, simply a void of emotion. This Infected simply existed in time and space. The Infected then turns and begins pacing the street once again. 

Markus looks around himself many times, the horde of Infected are all staring at the fallen sign and a few even run to it, but other than that there is no sign of hostility towards him. It was as if he was camouflaged. 

Another Infected limps up to him and snarls. Its eyes show a sort of primitive intimidation to say the least. It acts like an animal trying to passively maintain its spot in the pecking order. Saying it was an over analyzation would be understating what he was thinking, but it becomes evident that Markus isn't invisible, rather he is being seen as an Infected himself.

Markus' head drops until he's starring down at his hands. 

It couldn't be. It's never resurfaced before.

As weird as this scenario is, he makes the deliberate decision to avoid contemplating it. Whether his suspicion is correct or not doesn't matter. There's no guarantee this condition is permanent so he should probably use it to his advantage while it lasts.

He scans the street for any conspicuous buildings; a long abandoned pub here, a sketchy nail salon there. Nothing stands out or at least until he stops right in front of a very familiar logo. Night Fox, a one story, beat up, barely serviceable before the Fall, video shop. The place lost a lot of business with the invention of smartphones, but it still had enough money to scrape by in this untamed neighborhood. 

Ordinarily he would pass this building as it has no significance to him, but given his suspicion, he recalls what his comrade in arms wanted to show him. Jenks was a bit of a collector back in the day and rustic movies and games is right up his alley. If only he could be here to see it—whoever sent that message must know about Markus and Jenks... probably about the entirety of Allegiance One.

Upon entering into the Night Fox, Markus realizes that this store is almost completely untouched. Movies line the walls of the store with very few missing. For the part of town this shop was in, it looks half-way decent. Magazine racks sit on each end of the shelves three movie shelves holding an assortment of magazines including Sports, Nature, and Markus' least favorite for very particular reasons: playboy. A counter stands in the back of the store straight from the entrance door. He approaches the counter and takes a glance over it. On the desk behind, a piece of white paper sits with a pencil on it; no dust was anywhere near which means that the message on it was made recently.

The words inscribed on the paper read: "My favorite place to meet at 8:00. Don't be late. PS. I thought about writing this in binary but imagined Carter yelling at me for it?"

Markus' arms tremble as he breaths, "It can't be… but… he's dead."

He sets the paper down and takes a step back, his hand running through his hair. This can't possibly be.

"Damn it Jenks, I actually wanted to study up on binary," Markus says with a genuine smile, a smile that caused a tear to run down his rough cheek.

--- 

The tallest building in Pearson is a 96 story skyscraper owned by the company Geomacht. The second tallest tower is a 80 story skyscraper owned by Ocram Inc. The companies were long gone but their buildings were now a symbol of Pearson's previous success. Both buildings are littered with Infected so clearing it out has never been on anyone's to-do list. The elevator shafts however made getting to the roof a possible task. His rendezvous would take place on the top of the second tallest building as the tallest is too overrated.

He climbs the entirely of the elevator shaft and one flight of stairs. From the roof, he finds the setting sun clearly visible as it sets the whole sky into a beautiful amber and tropical orange color. The entire city of Pearson is visible from the edge of the tower. The sight is awe inspiring and honestly takes Markus by surprise: it's been a while since he's been this high up.

"Beautiful isn't it?"

Markus snaps to the voice with a pistol raised. He'd like to say his guard is lowered, but it seems you can't teach an old dog new tricks.

When he looks back, he finds that the question came from a man in all black, concealing his head with a hood and his face with a scarf. Ski goggles protect his eyes and identity, but Markus knows that voice anywhere. 

He lowers his gun, "But…".

"But, what? That's no way to greet a brother."

The two long-lost brothers embrace in a reunion both thought only death would bring. Their embrace fills Markus with a barrage of powerful emotions of which he can only suppress in a silence. He never thought he'd live to see the day that a fellow member of Allegiance One came back from the grave.

"How did you survive?" Markus asks, taking a step back.

The man promptly removes his goggles and scarf revealing a slightly older and much more mature Jenks. His eyes radiate a sense of wisdom that he's never had before. The sides of his hairline harbor that familiar indention of glasses he seldom wore outside. Lastly, his smile—a sort of unique smirk that always said that he was up to something—rests on his face like the day everything went down. Jenks narrows his eyes, showing off his signature smirk with a light hearted chuckle.

"I was gonna ask you the same thing, but knowing you, it doesn't matter. I don't bother to ask the roaches in a basement how they got there—I just accept that it's inevitable." 

"I barely escaped…" Markus trails off.

"I figured, as for me, I hacked into some cult super armor which shielded me from the blast. It's a real shame that it was damaged beyond repair afterwards… or at least that's what I assume. I didn't wake up there," Jenks suppresses a chuckle as he thinks back to what all happened.

"What about everyone else?!" Markus says excitedly.

"I don't know, we got separated before the impact. I woke up about six months ago in a medical bay of a government facility. They labeled me as 'permanently comatose' but I showed them. I actually woke up with no one around. Turns out the staff in that facility abandoned it a while before I woke up. To them I was a lost cause, but they left the life support going. I woke up right before it expired."

"So what did you do after you woke up?"

Jenks shifts to look off across the cityscape dozens of stories below, "Well, I was confused to say the least. The world went to shit and I didn't even get to see it go down. After finally escaping the abandoned hospital, I made my way to a settlement nearby and adjusted to life there. About a month ago, I found information on the nature of the parasite and that a city in America was harboring a very important cult official. I assume you know where he is?"

Markus' smile turns to a more serious, determined one. As much as he'd like to keep catching up with his dear comrade, he knows as well as any how grave the task at hand is, "There's two actually. I can't find one and the other is in a bunker somewhere underneath this city. I've been tracking him for a while but I can't find information on where the bunker is located. Harvey's House is the best lead I got so far."

Jenks puts his hand on Markus' shoulder and gives a friendly sigh. "We can figure that out later, but in the meantime we have a more pressing matter." Jenks purses his lips, keeping his serious stature at full effect. "My you've really matured haven't you…" He smiles, putting Markus' nerves at ease even further, "but I digress. The cult is after us, you in particular."

"I know," Markus says firmly.

"We found out too much that night for our head not to be on a wall. Have you made contact with Volker yet?"

"About that…"

Jenks takes a seat at the edge of the roof and lets his feet dangle off. He gestures for Markus to join him, "Let's hear it."

Markus sits down and watches out to the descending sun, "Just yesterday I was fighting a parasite–"

"So that's what you guys call them?" Jenks butts in.

"Yeah… anyways, after taking the parasite down, a horde of Infecteds surrounded me. Amongst their ranks was a single leader who spoke. I believe that was Volker."

Jenks purses his lips and looks straight down the side of the building as he thinks. Allegiance One was initially formed to target the Cult and Volker just so happens to be a prime creation of that despicable place. Throughout their time fighting in the war, they gained more information on the Cult than anyone else. If it isn't already blatantly apparent, their mission is far from over... the end of the world didn't change that.

"How did you know that Volker was targeting me specifically?" Markus wonders.

Jenks gives him a puzzled expression as he shifts in his spot, "Do you not know? Everyone is talking about you—people related to the Cult, that is. I don't frankly know why, but you have a bounty on your head."

"Where'd you hear about all this," Markus inquires.

Jenks shrugs, "You know how my information network operates. It's here and there, but it's never fair... For them."

Markus smiles, "Yeah, sounds about right."

They both share a brief laugh before calming and enjoying the other's company in silence.

"Also," Jenks adds, "I got an anonymous tip—the same one that led me here. It was too specific to truly be anonymous."

"Who was it?" Markus wonders.

Jenks shrugs, "I can't say for sure, but it was definitely a cultist. I have a sinking suspicion that something may be coming, though I'm not sure what."

Markus takes the pause as a moment to ask his own question, "Why did you send such a coded message to me? You could have just come and found me."

Jenks, who had started looking around at the scenery, turns back to him and grins, "I hacked into that beacon downtown and I found someone snooping around looking for signals. Initially I was just messing with them, reading metadata, checking network stuff, you get the point. Eventually I realized that their network of information was fairly sophisticated. I didn't know where you were at the time so I sent that little bugger a message in hopes that it would find its way to you. I then went somewhere only a gifted spy could get and left a message only you would understand."

Markus nods to his comrade affirmingly giving him a subtle fist bump, "It's not every day that someone prefers the second tallest building as opposed to the first."

"Well the tallest always gets the glory and the second is the underdog. I'm just not a fan of the fan favorite. Call me special, but it's just what I have come to associate my tastes with… On a serious note, getting all the way to this city was no easy task. The Cult is most definitely on my tail and I've got to play it safe too."

"Right… oh, Jenks. There's something I've got to tell you—" Markus hesitates trying to draw out the right words only to be interrupted by a very faint noise coming from the stairs of the roof. 

The two soldiers instantly were on their feet with weapons drawn.

"Speak of the devil and he shall come," Jenks whispers.

Both men only have pistols to defend themselves, but that doesn't waiver their focus on the closed, dormant door. A minute passes as they wait for anything to happen. At the two-minute mark, Markus elects to creep up to the door. Upon arriving, he nods to Jenks and swings the door open with both pistols aiming down the stairs. 

Nothing hides behind it.

"It must've been a spy then. I knew the Cult wouldn't let me do whatever I wanted without a bit of pressure," Jenks reasons, turning around, and running a hand through his hair. "Now they know we've met... and after all that work."

Markus turns back to the stairs for one last look and is met with the black bulging eyes of a sickly man: an Infected if he's ever seen one.

He nearly falls backward in surprise as the Infected slowly walks around the roof—completely blind. Jenks responds with his pistol targeting the head of the Infected but is stopped by Markus pushing the gun down and putting one finger on his lips.

The Infected walks up to the edge of the building and stops right before the sheer drop. It coughs out black blood and makes a groaning noise deep within its throat. Jenks gives Markus a look and waves his gun in the air; Markus just shakes his head. The Infected, still unaware of the men's presence, begins a long and ugly groan. It makes all sorts of weird noises: gasps, clicking with the teeth, and more coughs. Finally after a minute or so of grotesque noises it stops, turns around, and faces Markus. It opens its mouth and breathes once, then again, then lets out "i…t…hur…t…s…". Once it finishes the final syllable, it extends its legs and thrusts itself off the building. 

"What the fuck was that?!" Jenks yells out.

"The hell if I know!" Markus responds with just as much force.

"Since when can an Infected talk… there has to be some kind of explanation right? Volker maybe?"

Markus stops himself, "Yeah it has to be. I don't know what else it could be."

"But that doesn't explain why it didn't attack you."

Jenks looks off the edge of the building. The corpse of the Infected below can't even be distinguished from the pavement at their height. "This shit keeps getting more complicated."

"I was going to tell you," Markus begins trying to backtrack to the thought path he held earlier, "On my way here I got this terrible feeling inside my body. It knocked me out. When I came too, the parasites… they didn't attack me."

Jenks stares at him incredulously, "You're pulling my leg, right?"

Markus shakes his head, "I'm serious, it's like I have some sort of camouflage against them."

"That's good and well, but it begs the question: why didn't it attack me?"

Both men stare at one another in silence.

Jenks strokes his chin a few times mulling over the new information over. He pauses a few times acting like he found something to say but ultimately reverses and goes back to thinking. When nothing comes to mind, he pulls a piece of paper out of his pocket and hands it to Markus. "I'd love to catch up more and talk about life, but for now let's just keep in touch and try to find more information on this bunker. You've given me a lot to process and we both know that our strengths lie in different areas. You search for clues in every nook and cranny in the city, and I'll look in the invisible places. Once we find the bunker and get in there we can finally get information on the Cult and figure out if any of our other comrades are still alive." Markus reads the paper to find a phone number on it. "With the beacon up we can be in contact and don't worry I'll handle the encryption."

Markus folds the paper up and stuffs it into his own pocket before saying, "Alright, I'd expect nothing less from the top computer nerd this side of the pacific."

The two men embrace one last time before Jenks takes his leave first through the stairwell. He waves on his way out hesitating to leave the field of view of his friend. Markus shares the feeling as he too doesn't want to leave Jenks only to believe he's dead once more. The night isn't getting any younger and they both come to terms with this as Jenks departs, leaving Markus alone on the roof. 

After brushing the somber mood out of his forethoughts, he looks up to the young night sky. The stars above twinkle at him like the sun reflecting off of rolling waves. This view reminds him of the sky in the desert: there never was a city nearby to block out the stars. Markus hates war almost as much as he hates the Cult, but he can't help but miss all that time he spent with his team... the people who welcomed him after he finally escaped that... place. 

If only, one day, they could all share some drinks together and get to look up into the sky like they used to. They'd sing songs even though half of them were tone deaf and they'd cheer to another day well spent. If only he could have just that, maybe he could forgive himself for everything that transpired.
