_There exist two schools of thought regarding existence. There is surviving and living; the distinction is purpose._

“It’s time to get up…”

The voice echoes through his thoughts like a distant memory forcefully dragged to the surface. The voice is familiar, yet his unconscious mind can't pinpoint to whom it belongs.

"Come on, Markus. I'm not going to remind you again."

Markus inhales deeply as he lifts himself off his back. The muscles running down his spine ache as he refuses their pleas for five more minutes. He turns and presses the top button on his alarm—the same one he does every morning—even though the real alarm never goes off.

He glances around the empty room and sighs when he determines the voice must truly be in his own head.

His legs slide off the edge of the bed as he presses his hands together, studying the familiar pattern of the carpet. Its repetitive pattern puts his mind at ease as he recalls the events of the day prior. Change was not something he appreciated, so finding any bit of consistency was a welcome experience.

Standing up, his leg nearly buckles as the strained quad receives a hair too much weight. He spends the next few milliseconds in a heated internal debate before ultimately acquiescing to cease the entitled complaining of his legs. He slowly trudges out of his room and sits down in front of a bowl of cereal and the morning news.

“If you haven't been outside yet, bring an umbrella—it's raining. Though it should clear up later today,” the familiar, well-dressed weatherman reports.

Markus looks out the window and finds only golden rays of sunlight leaking through the curtains. Wrong channel today.

He finishes his nearly stale cornflakes and grabs his bag, which he set by the door the day before. He looks back across the empty living room in search of something—he's not really sure what. Maybe someone would turn the corner and say hi, or maybe there was something he forgot to pack. With only a quick glance around, he concludes that the voice must still be in his own head.

"I'll be back later," he calls out through the house.

Only ambient creaking and groaning answer his call.

---

Markus’ motorcycle finally comes to a halt in front of the most intimidating building on the block: Pearson High School. He finds himself here often, even though his age group checked out of these doors a considerable amount of time ago. The statue of the school's mascot, a penguin, remains in front of the school, covered in a variety of vibrant colors—graffiti colors. Sometimes Markus likes to think that the prank to cover the mascot was so good that no one ever wanted to change it, three years later. The best part was that he had already graduated by that point. This prank was commissioned by his juniors.

As he enters through the front doors, he doesn't forget to wave to the front office, even though there are no secretaries there. He makes his way down the main hallway and arrives at his true destination: the weight room.

_“Seven… eight… nine…” Markus struggled._

_“One more, come on, one more,” Caden, Markus’ friend, cheered._

_Markus was a regular at the weight room as a matter of principle. He wasn’t on the football team like his friend, but he still found it tremendously important to stay physically fit. Caden, seconding this notion, quickly became a gym buddy and friend after a few months of acquaintance. Now, Markus could confidently say that Caden was his best friend._

_Right before the muscles in his chest and arms gave out, he finished his last rep._

_“Dang, man, I don't get how you jocks can do this all the time AND lift such impressive numbers,” Markus commented as he sprayed a stream of water from his water bottle into his dry mouth._

_“Dunno, it just kind of happens when you wanna make the team,” Caden looked back at some other team members at another rack and nodded as they made eye contact. “Maybe if you weren’t such an introvert all the time, you’d hang out with the jocks more often. You’ve got the build, confidence, and reputation for it—you just need the motivation.”_

_Markus gave a half-hearted laugh and shook his head with a grin. “Nah, man, I just value my alone time, that’s all.”_

_Caden rolled his eyes as he mumbled, “Wasted potential, if you ask me.”_

_Markus, hearing the statement clearly, feigned ignorance as a courtesy. “What was that?”_

_“Nothing important,” Caden commented, as conspicuous as ever.

_Markus and his friend switched places on the rack after another twenty-five-pound plate was placed on either end of the bar. Caden placed his hands on the bar when another guy approached them._

_“Hey Seth,” Caden greeted with a typical head nod, despite actively being on his back._

_“Whatsup, dude,” Seth responded. “I wanted to ask if you were coming to the party?”_

_Caden promptly sat up and pondered the offer. “Which one?” he eventually responded with a sly smile._

_Seth chuckled. “For real, it’s homecoming after all. I’m talking about the one at Mike’s house. He’s got a pool, a massive yard, and I guarantee all the hot chicks will be there.”_

_“Bet,” Caden commented enthusiastically.

_Seth smiled as he noticed Markus standing behind the bar. “Markus, you’ll be there too, right?”_

_Markus leaned his head from side to side in a contemplative fashion, already knowing what his response would be. “I don’t really know yet,” he replied politely._

_“Oh, come on,” Caden interjected, “You can’t hold up in your room every Friday night!”_

_Markus hesitated. Sure, spending time with Caden was always enjoyable, but parties just weren’t his thing. He preferred small, intimate gatherings over giant raves with who knows what going on._

_Seth laughed as he stepped up and patted Markus on the shoulder. “Take your time, no pressure… though you really should come. I’ll catch you guys later.”_

_Caden waved as his comrade in pads headed back to a group of other guys all cheering on a powerlifter. It wasn’t even a second later that Caden was already back on his case._

_“Please,” he pleaded, kneeling on the bench with his elbows resting on the bar, “This party will be lit. You have to indulge me this one time.”_

_Markus rubbed the back of his head. “I don’t know, man, I just feel like this thing will be a total bust.”_

_“Mike’s party?!” Caden exclaimed. “Hell no, that guy knows how to throw a party. It’s in his rich, high-class blood. So please, do this for me.”_

_Markus sighed as he leaned against the metal pillar of the lifting rack. “I’m telling you, man, I really need to be more assertive. Somehow, you always seem to convince me to do things I really don’t want to do.”_

_“Just call it a gift.”_

_“I wouldn’t go that far.”_

_Caden waved his hand as he lay back below the bar, stretching his chest out. “I’m just playin’. I would never try to convince you of something I wouldn’t do myself. Anyway, you might find a girl you like there.”_

_Markus prepared to spot him. “You have experience with that, don’t you? How many girlfriends have you had before? If I counted correctly, I think the total divided by ten is still zero.”_

_Caden laughed. “Tis the joys of being a dedicated athlete. I’d say I regret it, but my fair maiden wrapped in pigskin will always be waiting for me on Friday nights.”_

_He continued to laugh a bit at his own corny joke right as the weight of the bar kicked in. He was just lucky that Markus was a competent spotter._

Forty minutes of weights passed, followed by thirty of cardio. Markus, breathing steadily but not heavily, takes a chug of water and looks around the room. The empty room is lit solely by the large glass windows lining one of the walls. There was a time that this place meant something to various people. It’s a shame that he’s the only one who uses it nowadays.

The hallway is similarly forlorn, branded with remnants of a much happier time. At either end of the corridor is a miniscule amount of light shining through the glass doors. Long lines of lockers hug both walls, with scattered doors sitting slightly ajar. A pencil here, an eraser there—mundane things still decorate the hall, yet they still fill him with some strange, melancholic feeling.

He passes a particular classroom with the door swung wide open. Inside is an orderly grid of desks, some still containing papers atop their dusty surfaces. Markus remembers vividly how he used to listen to Mr. Ruthenthal, a borderline senile old man, lecture on and on about how the Romans were tactical geniuses. Markus didn’t disagree per se, but he could clearly tell that the old man had never truly seen the battlefield, as every glorified word that left his mouth praised scenes that had no business being seen as honorable. He just kept quiet, however, because, as they say, ignorance is bliss.

Then again, he chose to come to school, even if he didn't need to. The education system here in the States was far from perfect, but he preferred an overly excited old man as a teacher to many he's had in the past. High school education was something he's glad he convinced himself to pursue.

Though, he'd be lying if he said he completed high school without regrets. While he enjoyed most of his time here, he finds that certain parts will remain tainted by memories he'd prefer to forget.

He carefully closes the school’s entry door, making sure it doesn’t lock on him, and begins to head back to his bike, only to be stopped in his tracks. A man in a shredded gray business suit stands overlooking the bus lane in front of the school. His head hangs low to one side and his opposite shoulder is lifted. Small patches of hair remain on his head, while entire clumps of skin seem to have peeled off. His body makes short, jerky motions. The surrounding air would strike fear into any normal person. A machete also seems to be part of his arm—he isn't holding it, it is part of his arm. The monster, hearing footsteps close behind him, turns around to reveal its grotesque face. The left side of its jaw has been torn off, with dried blood covering the remaining side. Its eyes are bent in a permanent expression of anger, with cuts and gashes littering any pieces of skin that remained. Infected parasites were never a pretty sight to behold.

There is no time to think; the monstrosity makes its advance with a swift lunge. Its blade whips around right at Markus’ head level. Markus, with what could be described as a sigh, ducks under the attack, sidesteps out of the way, and leaves behind a little gift right in the parasite’s jugular. The parasite takes one more step before falling to the ground: dead.

Markus waits for the count of three—not two, not four, but three—before closing the distance to the deceased carcass. He rolls it onto its back and opens up its shredded jacket. Inside, he pulls out a brown wallet and checks the contents. There are forty-five dollars in old-world cash, three different credit cards, a picture of the man's family, and, most importantly, a driver's license. Markus takes the identification and puts it in a black bag connected to his backpack. He's about to leave until he takes one last glance at the photo. It depicts a man, about thirty, with his wife and two twin daughters. The whole family is smiling, almost as if the children were told they could get ice cream if they behaved. The family was one of many torn apart three years ago, back when the parasites arose. He grabs the picture as well.

“Rest in peace,” he mutters to the invisible soul he truly believes is finally free.

These Infecteds are commonplace throughout the city, yet Markus likes to believe every kill thins their numbers. The underlying cause of the parasites isn't airborne—despite what the authorities said during the Fall. The only channel of infection is through direct blood or saliva exposure. Some superstitious people said that a bite mark would turn someone, but it won't, and it never has. Only bites that pierce the skin actually have the potential to turn someone, and that's become quite rare as many people have grown accustomed to wearing armor when they traverse dangerous places.

There's also one other reason why infection as a whole has nearly stopped. That reason is mutation. Infecteds aren't mutated much, but even this poor man has grown a sword for an arm. These mutations drastically increase lethality, and a dead person can't reanimate—only feed existing parasites.

With these reasons in mind, it becomes quite logical for a kill to matter nowadays. The Fall created millions of parasites in Pearson, but only a handful of people turn in any given month, while thousands of parasites die. This logic even inspired a faction known as the Death Order to rise up in efforts to purifying Pearson. Maybe one day, this city will be clear of the damn parasites. Markus, however, will likely never see that day. If things go well, his time here will be waning anyway.

Markus rises off the ground and scans the surroundings, already knowing that it is clear. The trees decorating the foreground of the premises cover the ground with amber leaves. A few leaves fall at this very moment as only the wind bothers to break the defining silence of this sad street.

He recalls countless times he and two others would pass this scene without a second thought. Now he's taken aback by the simplicity of a familiar sight that will never feel like it did back then. What would it take to live a normal life again? In his mind, he finds it depressing that the corpse at his feet is a more familiar sight than the simple trees losing their leaves around three friends walking home.

He returns to his bike without another word or thought, as he determines that his next stop will be to cash in his findings.

---

The trip from the high school to downtown takes him over a particularly tall bridge that crosses the Pearson River. One end of the bridge feeds into the downtown area, while the other side consists mostly of a commercial district and a particularly scenic view. Normally, Markus doesn't bother stopping at the view he's seen about two dozen times, but today he's feeling especially nostalgic.

Pulling up to the small parking lot, he swings his leg off the bike and steps up to the metal railing. The view beyond is nothing short of breathtaking, as the entire Pearson skyline is easily visible with the iconic bridge creating a trail right to the heart of the city. The skyscrapers making up the skyline aren't the tallest in the world, but they're surely impressive by Markus' biased standards—though he's seen dozens of skylines in his lifetime.

He finds himself lost in a trance as he traces the buildings with his eyes. Honestly, he'd probably continue to do so for another five minutes if he hadn't noticed a particular distraction in the corner of his vision.

Growing beside one of the railing supports is a small purple flower; its petals are a vibrant contrast to the surrounding green. One might even call it beautiful if it weren't for a strange, light brown, moss-like substance invading half of the flower's surface. This mysterious moss looks like an ugly mix of carpet and chitin, as it's simultaneously fuzzy and rigid. The wilting flower seems to be crying out in pain as the invasive moss weighs it down nearly to the point of pressing the flower bud into the ground.

Markus has encountered countless types of flora, but this isn't one he's familiar with—much less in this area. Perhaps he hasn't looked hard enough, but he has a sinking suspicion that this simply isn't indigenous, nor normal. The surrounding plants don't seem to be bothered by the wintering flower, which paints its suffering as an isolated one.

With a drawn-out sigh, Markus returns to his feet and glances across the river one last time. The familiar, calming view reminds him of many things he'd love to explore, but his finite time draws him towards his bike. Maybe he'll have to come back here sometime soon. Maybe.
