_Wilbur crouched over a pile of torn clothing in the middle of the room lit only by a dim light hanging from the ceiling. He sorted through each piece of tattered cloth meticulously as if every grain of the ripped fabric could contribute meaningfully to their pursuit. The remnants of an old parka, the split skirt, even the mostly intact scarf seemed to be of interest to him._

_"Your thoughts?" Grant inquires as he inspected right alongside his subordinate._

_Wilbur looked up with a firm look, "They're definitely still alive."_

_"How would you know that?" Jenks pointed out as he and Nox finished defusing a bomb inside a cooking pot, "There are so many variables, you can't possibly determine anything with certainty."_

_Wilbur stood up and picked up his gun from a nearby table, "You can call it intuition, but violent death usually invokes a certain amount of gore."_

_"I guess that makes sense," Jenks responded, "But how do you know they weren't just transported elsewhere to be killed, we are in the city after all. What do you suspect a hostage situation?"_

_Markus leaned in to hear this part. The conversation was rather ordinary initially, but now it seemed that there's something to be gained._

_"Well... How do I put his?" Wilbur thought meticulously, "If they were forced out of here then you'd be right, I couldn't be certain," he pointed to a particular table in the corner of the room with nothing on it but a single candle. Although not obvious at first glance, it could be seen that the dust density was not consistent across the surface of the table. Furthermore, residing above the table was four framed photos with one seemingly vacant spot residing consciously between a photo of a sacred ritual site and an old woman, "I suspect that there were framed pictures on that table. People don't have the luxury of taking possessions with them if they're about to be killed."_

_"Then why the tattered clothing?" Jenks wondered, a question that is shared by all but Wilbur and Grant._

_Grant took the lead on this one and reaches down to grab some fabric. He turned it over to reveal some strange pattern on the inside of the skirt. Between Markus and Jenks, they were familiar with just about every language spoken, written, and cryptographic, but neither of them recognized it._

_"I don't understand," Markus decided as he tries and fails to align the patterns with any recognizable glyphs._

_"It's impossible to decipher what they mean, but these patterns aren't sown into skirts," Grant explained as he received a confirming nod from Wilbur._

_"Are you proposing that tearing up the clothes was a way of concealing the code?" Jenks posed._

_Wilbur nodded as he transitioned over to the front door and dropped his night vision goggles back onto his eyes, "Precisely. If they're cognizant enough to destroy evidence, but still allow it to be found, it means we're dealing with an amateur. If they can't be trusted to destroy something like that then they certainly can't cover up their own death..." he pushed the door open and stepped one foot out the door, "We've got a point of contact to the trail. Let's start backtracking."_

---

Although begrudgingly, Markus conceded to Jenks' request to take a day off. He wound up spending the entire day in bed, completely unmoving. Jenks did stop by to ensure he ate something, but beyond that, Markus refused to move an inch in order to ensure the healing process proceeded without delay. After all, he doesn't have time to wait around—he has to move.

In light of that, he wakes up the next day and immediately hops on his bike. Breathing is still strained and the bumps on the road make his head spin, but he persists. Whether Jenks tries to stop him or he finds himself unable to continue, he'll take the risk in order to meet with Reign and figure out where this famine started. If he can do that, he should be able to backtrack to whoever this "dangerous" individual is. After that, well, that'll be one less cultist to deal with and one more city safe from the Cult's influence.

He arrives to the Radio Tower gates exactly like he did two days prior, though this time with a more fleshed out plan in mind.

"You again?" one of the guards wonders as Markus walks up, ensuring that he looks like he's at one-hundred percent, "We still can't let people in."

"I have to discuss something with General Reign," Markus announces truthfully, "Please allow me to see him. Escort me personally if you have to."

One of the guards—the same one who was stationed here two days ago—leans on the edge of the wall with a sympathetic expression, "I informed him about your arrival the last time and he did consider it. He said that he means no disrespect, but his priorities simply don't align with your services right now."

"Did Elizabeth think that too?" Markus calls out.

The guard rubs the back of his head, "Uh... I'm not sure. She's been a busy bee recently. None of us have seen her recently."

Suddenly someone on the inside catches the guards attention and they both look back.

"Speak of the devil," the guard comments to himself.

"I heard my name," a female voice says on the other side of the closed gate, "Who's asking for me."

The guard gestures toward Markus even though the gate is opaque with no gaps, "It's just Markus, he keeps trying to get it."

"Markus!" Elizabeth exclaims on the other side, "And you haven't let him in?!"

The guard hesitates, "Well... General Reign gave us orders. I checked in with him too, and he said to keep the gate closed."

"No!" Elizabeth corrects, "Markus is the exception, let him in."

"But..."

"Let him in."

The guard sighs as he exchanges a glance with the other man stationed on the wall and they both cave. The mechanical gate slowly slides open revealing an enthusiastic demeanor of Elizabeth Reign. 

Although not a Strickler for aesthetic, Markus can't help but take notice to Elizabeth's bright pink hoodie and blue jeans. He's always associated her as a rule-following, business casual sort of person who bounced between meetings more than parasites between hives. Seeing her in truly informal attire is a kind of juxtaposition that he associates with Cleo wearing formal clothes. It's not unexpected given that she lives here, but it's definitely a change of pace for him.

"Markus!" Elizabeth exclaims as she steps out of the compound to greet him, "I didn't have a way to get in contact with you so I've been waiting until you showed back up."

"Couldn't you have had your dad contact me?"

Elizabeth shakes her head as she gestures for him to follow, "Come with me, I'll explain on the way."

They traverse through the open courtyard towards the main building. The usual facilities are especially sparse today as the gun range is completely empty, the makeshift playground is vacant of kids, and the outdoor eating areas are despairingly empty. One or two full-grown men walk through the courtyard, but their not meandering so much as routinely inspecting things on their way to another assignment.

"My father has been particularly unresponsive recently," Elizabeth explains as she opens the front door of the Radio Tower and lets Markus enter in first, "I've tried to make contact with you multiple times, but I've had virtually no luck—until now that is."

"Make contact with me?" Markus wonders aloud.

She nods, "In case you've forgotten, the last time we spoke, you broke into the hive and extract government secrets. Braedon gave me a summary of what he found, but I lost contact with him around the same time."

"Braed—oh, Jenks," Markus resolves under his breath. He hasn't called Jenks by his first name in years. Force of habit it seems.

Elizabeth leads him through the Radio Tower to the elevator and they promptly step in. She hits the eighteenth floor before stepping away. Markus takes notice of the fact that the eighteenth floor is the same one Reign's office happens to be at.

"So I particularly wanted to know if anything useful has come out of the Mayor's bunker," Elizabeth establishes as soft background music begins to play, "Braedon told me he found it but refused to say where it actually was."

"And for good reason," Markus suggests as he leans back on the elevator railing.

Elizabeth gives him a puzzled look, "Why? That bunker is exactly what we need right now."

Shaking his head, Elizabeth goes still while he responds, "That place was a hellhole. Those factions that disappeared recently, yeah, they only disappeared because that bunker was opened. Turns out the mayor was hiding something more dangerous than parasites."

Elizabeth's face goes pale.

"Don't worry," Markus assures quickly, "I've already taken care of it and the bunker is safe to enter now. It's on 39123 Maple Ave, the rich neighborhood north of the Mall."

Despite the severity of his previous statements, Elizabeth seems to come to her senses quickly. With a gentle deep breath, she pulls her bangs out of her eyes and slides her hands into her hoodie pockets, "I guess I can forgive you guys. Then what's the status of the bunker? Is there food down there?"

Markus hesitates to respond. On one hand, he could be honest and shatter any hope that her and anyone else might have accumulated, but on the other hand, he doesn't want to lie and lead them on. Is the best option to avoid answering, or should he be honest?

"That's actually what I came here for," Markus says as he notices the elevator passing the sixteenth floor, "I wanted to speak to your father about the matter."

Elizabeth immediately bites her lip, "Uh, I was hoping we could discuss privately before going through him... you see he's—"

Suddenly the elevator doors open and the man in question reveals himself on the other side. His tired eyes with hanging bags open a bit wider as he notices Markus standing inside.

"Well I'll be..." Reign comments as he inspects the two people in the elevator, "Sorry for interrupting."

Without delay, he reaches into the elevator and clicks the close door button before promptly stepping back into the hall. Markus and Elizabeth simply watch the scene unfold in silence. Neither of them laugh, however, as Markus simply clicks the open door button to find Reign patiently waiting on the other side.

"Why would you do that?" Elizabeth questions as she steps outside the elevator.

Reign offers himself a pity laugh as he rubs the back of his graying hair, "Give me a break, my humor can't be on point all the time. I just need a bit of sleep is all."

Elizabeth looks her father over closely as she inspects every article of his clothing before settling the investigation by pulling some lint off his shoulder, "You really need to take better care of yourself. You don't have to run this place by yourself."

"And make my daughter shoulder the burden?" Reign argues dismissively.

This response doesn't faze the man's daughter in the slightest, "You have a whole board of supervisors. The Retainers are not for you alone to oversee."

"Yeah, yeah," Reign waves off, "I'll contemplate your words when people are safe. Now. Why is Markus here, I told the guards to refuse any visitors for the time being."

"He's here about the bunker," Elizabeth defends.

Reign goes completely silent as he stares at his daughter for a moment before slowly turning to Markus. His exhausted visage suddenly revealing a little bit more life. Even if he looked worse for wear, this news seems to have captured his full, undivided attention. Without delay, he waves for the two of them to follow him, "Come with me."

Unsurprisingly Markus soon finds himself inside Reign's office with the door close and the three of them spread out across the room. Reign practically falls into his chair behind his desk, Elizabeth leans against a bookshelf along the wall, and Markus occupies a carpet in the center of the room.

"Go ahead and speak, Markus," Reign says, "I'm listening."

Markus takes a deep breath as he takes a glance outside the windows at the street beyond. He doesn't come here often during the day so seeing a well-lit street is a bit unusable, but he disregards it with an equal exhale.

"Right, I'll tell you about the bunker, but I'll need some answers first."

"Sure," Reign agrees hesitantly, "Don't keep me in suspense though."

Markus transitions over to a nearby coffee table and takes a seat on it so he can face the two Reigns, "Tell me everything you know about this fungus, I might have a lead on a solution."

Both of their eyes widen. Elizabeth stands up straight and rushes over to her father's side, but he waves her off as he takes the comment much more seriously, "How do you know?"

"I don't," Markus answers directly, "But I can't be certain until I know more."

Reign sighs, "Very well... We discovered some of our plants dying about five weeks ago. Back then we assumed it was just some local outbreak and applied the standard damage control measures—quarantine, isolate, eradicate. We thought that would be enough, but then the same fungus popped up in every single field in the city. What's more, they were all found about the same time."

"You never told me this," Elizabeth says in disbelief, "I've been telling everyone that we don't know the extent of its presence."

Reign pinches the bridge of his nose as he takes a moment to think things over, "I didn't tell anyone because I was afraid the evidence was pointing to a sabotage. Calling it a natural disaster is a lot easier for the populace to accept."

"But—" Elizabeth tries.

"He's right," Markus cuts in, drawing all attention to him, "At least, that's what the evidence has been pointing to on my end as well. What I really need is something to backtrack its origin. I'm looking for the source."

Reign nods his head as he turns around in his chair and looks out the windows, "I figured you'd say something like that."

Elizabeth looks between them carefully as she draws her own conclusion, "If you want, we can go take a look at the fields. You might be able to learn more if you visit the first place we discovered it."

Markus taps his chin in thought, "Maybe."

Investigation has never been Markus' strong suit so he can't say he knows what the best option is. That being said, he remembers a great deal about how Wilbur orchestrates searches, so he might as well lean on that.

"Actually, I think that might be helpful."

Elizabeth smiles, "Alright, I'll come with you to ensure no one bars your way," she gives her father a dirty look.

Reign shrugs before turning his attention back towards Markus, "I'll allow it, but now you have to tell us about the bunker."

Pursing his lips, Markus leans forward and lets his elbows rest on his knees. This is not going to be an easy thing to talk about. He might as well lay it on thick from the get-go to prevent any misunderstanding. Besides, Reign and Elizabeth are two of the most pragmatic individuals he knows: they'll be able to take it.

"The bunker produced lackluster results," Markus says bluntly, "You're free to go take whatever you want, but it's basically nothing."

Calling what he subsequently sees as long faces would be an understatement as they both look to the floor. Of all the news that Markus could offer, this is probably the worst he possibly could. The low spirits don't last forever, however, as Elizabeth looks up swiftly.

"I guess we'll just have to find a cure for the fungus. We didn't burn all the crops so if we can cure it, we can still salvage enough to make it through the winter."

Reign mutters something unintelligible under his breath before turning back around in his chair, "Don't get me started on the winter."

"There's one other thing," Markus offers slowly, really not wanting to be the bearer of this bad news, "Have you heard anything about the Eagle faction."

Both Reigns look less hopeful, but Mackenzie Reign takes the lead, "They and and Nurse faction have joined forces. I've kept all visitors out to prevent any spies from infiltrating our base. I'm afraid a war is on the rise."

Markus shakes his head to contradict the statement, "As war is not on the rise."

Reign perks his head up, "What makes you say that?"

Markus takes a deep breath, "It's because the Eagles are gone. Their entire faction was wiped out two days ago."

Elizabeth covers her mouth as she steps back. Reign, on the other hand, lowers his head and closes his eyes like a real general hearing that his army lost the war. Regardless of which side the Eagles were on, they were still people, and now over a hundred survivors lay dead.

After a lengthy moment of silence, Reign picks up a pen from his desk and taps it on a blank notecard, "How did it happen. I want every detail."

Markus thinks of Jenks and how determined he is to bring Experiment T down for good. Even if Markus' experience with Ryan have been mixed, he can't deny the pure destruction he's caused. Doctor Crane may even be a contributing factor, but even that is not enough to forgive him of his transgressions. No amount of rationalization will make up for the people that have died.

"There's someone in the city that goes by the name of Experiment T or sometimes Ryan," Markus explains slowly and methodically as he stands up and begins pacing the floor, "He looks like a human, acts like a human, but he makes a Revenant look fragile. The Eagle faction disappeared, not because of food, but because that monster of a man appeared before them. I've run into Ryan on a few occasions and each one has nearly been the end of me. I still don't know his motivation, but know that he's just as dangerous as the famine right now."

After jotting down a few notes Reign looks up, "Are you certain? That's a very serious statement you're making."

"Deadly."

As Reign leans back to take it all in, Elizabeth continues, "Is there anything we can do about him? The coalition that's been making us nervous is now in shambles, but I'm not so sure that we can rest easy just yet."

Markus shakes his head, "I don't know the answer. If you want my advice, just be cautious but don't lose sleep over it. Jenks—Braedon, and I are looking into the matter; however, if the time does come, I'll be expecting some support from you. For the good of the city."

Reign nods to that statement quite easily, "Of course, if what you say is true I'd be happy to assist in any way that I can."

Elizabeth steps away from the bookshelf and crosses her arms, "It still doesn't make sense to me. How can a single person bring down an entire faction as big as the Eagles? Is he different from the thing that destroyed the factions to the North?"

Shaking his head and returning to his seat on the table, Markus sighs, "No. They're different. It's a pain in the ass to think that bringing one monster down just led to another filling its place."

And he hasn't even mentioned the Revenant yet, but he'll leave that one out for now. In hindsight, it's absurd to think that an intelligent parasite—the same mutant freak the entire city has been fighting for three years—is dwarfed by an bunker lurker and some guy who just showed up on his own. What in the world has he gotten himself into?

No.

This is the Cult he's dealing with. He has to expect a certain degree of absurdity and handle it effectively. If he can't do that, his childhood experience and entire future strategy will be for naught.

"Uh..." Elizabeth stutters, clearly trying to fill for time.

"Let's go check out that field," Markus decides as he stands up and takes a few steps towards the door, "I agree that we can't rest easy, so let's get to work."

Reign doesn't object as Markus departs from his office with Elizabeth in tow. The elevator is conveniently close so they wind up stopping there and waiting for it.

"So much is happening," Elizabeth mumbles to herself, "I just met with the Preserver faction earlier today, and they have been suppressing panic for days now. First parasites, then food, and now it's just another thing compounded on."

Markus nods, "Yeah, so this lead of mine better bear some fruit."

Suddenly the elevator dings as the doors open and a large box is shoved into his chest. Unable to see in front of him, a guy holding an oversized box nearly falls over as Markus has to assist in stabilizing the box as it hits him. The man briefly apologizes as he continues on down the hallway. Markus, however, keels over—the box hit his rib.

"Are you alright!" Elizabeth exclaims as she watches Markus almost fall over.

Not wanting to be seen as dramatic, he holds up a hand to refuse any help as he forces one foot underneath him. Breathing has been a strenuous activity for weeks but having a rigid object shoved into the injured area is another level of pain he can't handle on the fly. The pain eventually alleviates enough for him to regain focus, but that doesn't stop Elizabeth from crouching down beside him with a concerned look.

"I'm fine," Markus asserts as he slowly rises to his feet, "I'm just recovering from my injuries. I'll be fine."

Elizabeth clearly doesn't agree with his assessment, but she doesn't dispute it as Markus steps into the elevator. Their descent remains nearly completely silent.

Before their journey over to the field, Elizabeth situates herself in more appropriate attire for an investigation. Not that he has anything against hoodies, but Elizabeth simply isn't a hoodie-wearing sort of girl. A denim jacket and jeans suit her a lot better.

To Markus' relief they take separate vehicles over to the field. This works particularly well because Elizabeth can lead the way while Markus struggles to keep his eyes open with every bump of the road. Driving a motorcycle makes dodging particular potholes a trivial endeavor, but that doesn't mean the suspension is good enough to provide a perfect journey. Luckily, Elizabeth never questions it.

After cross over some railroad tracks the commercial and residential districts drop off sharply. It becomes quite apparent that Markus hasn't really driven this far out in some time as the sparse fields begin to paint an unfamiliar picture. He suspects that most people don't come this far out because what he finds is so incriminating that he has to commend Reign and Cleo for keeping it a secret for so long.

At first, it's just a single field, then it's two, and before long it's ever single one. Plots of land that should normally be green or yellowish-brown are all tainted with dense patches of brown, or in some cases, dark gray. These moss-like patches invade the fields in mass quantities reducing most crops to mere eyesores rather than a potential food source. From the sheer volume of moss patches, he suspects only twenty or thirty percent of the fields can actually be saved—and that's only a surface level observation.

Elizabeth pulls into a gravel driveway and parks her car in front of a large metal building. Markus parks his motorcycle to the side and glances off towards the city skyline. Just twenty or so kilometers away are the impressive skyscrapers that put Pearson on the map. To think he's never seen this side of the city before.

"What's there to see here?" Markus asks as he meets up with Elizabeth by the large metal door of the building.

Elizabeth gestures towards the fields, "Out there in the fields is where this fungus first popped up. I'm going to go get a key to this building, so you can take a look around while I'm gone."

Markus nods, "Sure."

Following this, Elizabeth departs for a nearby far house with a peculiar person staring through the curtains. He's not too sure, but it almost looks like there's a green lap in the background of the onlooker. Such an individual doesn't faze Markus in the slightest, but it does make him wonder what it must be like living all the way out here. They're surrounded by decay which serves as a constant reminder of the city's predicament. Perhaps it might even drive some people crazy... or skeptical at the very least.

Seeing that he has a few moments to spare, he figures he might as well put them to good use and investigate this fungus. As such, he meanders away from the metal building and towards the field of dying plants. One particular patch of brownish-gray moss resides just behind the building so he opts to inspect that first.

At a closer look, the moss becomes very reminiscent of the parasites. When a human is infected, they begin to slowly mutate into something that can no longer be seen as human. These plants, while not mutating as extremely, seem to be wilting in strange ways. Some of the plants in this particular patch have simply died outright and crumbled, others have overlapped with one another and fused into one large, mutated organism. Each plant experiences the fungus differently, yet the only constant is that none of them can thrive under the new conditions.

"Markus!" Elizabeth calls out from the other side of the building.

The field doesn't present any direct facts, so Markus rises and heads back towards her. To be honest, he's not entire sure what he's supposed to be looking for, but coming here aligns with Vincenzo's directions, so no evidence still tells him something: it tells him that Vincenzo is a liar.

"Got the key?" Markus asks as he turns the corner of the sheet metal building.

What he finds waiting for him is not Elizabeth holding a key, but a despicable green-eyed doctor holding her with one outstretch arm—the other wielding that familiar syringe.

"Not only did you survive, but you managed to find me," Crane points out as his torn mouth oozes glowing green fluid, "How in the world did you manage that?"

Markus' pistol is in his hands and aimed in on the doctor within the second. He considers going through the lengthy process of questions, answers, misinformation, and frustration, but he's in no mood to go through that again. Instead, he expedites the process and elects to shoot the syringe out of the doctor's hand.

Knowing what happened to the last syringe his guy stuck into someone, he doesn't shoot the syringe exactly and instead shoots Crane's hand so that he's forced to drop it. Then, without hesitation, Markus shatters the syringe with a second bullet. 

To further the action, Elizabeth takes Crane's shifted attention to stab his leg with a knife before slashing the tendon in his arm so that he's forced to let go of her neck. She pushes the sadistic doctor away and backpedals until he's standing alone with a pistol pointed at his head.

"Clever," Crane comments, seemingly deterred by the fact that three of his limps are severely wounded at the moment, "That was far more proactive than I expected."

"It's not so easy when I'm not paralyzed," Markus snickers as he tightens the pressure on the trigger, "I'm only going to let you life until you tell me what I want to know."

Doctor Crane lets out a long sigh which causes green ooze to splatter on the ground in front of him, "You do realize how ineffective that approach is?"

Markus doesn't answer that particular point. Elizabeth does, however.

"Who is this guy?" she pulls a pistol out of her car and joins Markus in the firing squad, "You two know each other?"

"Oh yes," Crane says with a playful tone, "Markus and I go way, way back. You could say I'm like a father to him."

"Shut it," Markus cancels out, "Did you create this fungus?"

Crane folds his hands behind his back as he looks around at the sea of dead fields, "All of this? No, how inefficient. Killing all of these plants is pointless unless something productive comes out of it."

"Then who did?" Markus presses. Unlike the last dozen or so times he questioned a member of the Cult, he's not going to let them dodge his every inquiry. He needs some answers and he needs them now.

The doctor mulls the question over for a moment as he taps his chin with a rigid finger nail, "I can't say for certain, in fact, I don't know. There is something interesting I'd be willing to show you. Please, allow me to—"

"Quiet!" Markus asserts so forcefully it makes Elizabeth jump, "I'll ask the questions."

Crane frowns, "Persuasion through force I see. That's one of the least effective strategies, unfortunately."

Markus glares at the cultist menacingly, "If you don't know who started it, then tell me what you did with Experiment A."

Beside him, Elizabeth glances over curiously but otherwise keeps her focus fixated on the disgusting-looking creep in front of her. 

"Ah, Experiment A," Crane says as he steps over to the large metal door.

"Back away!" Markus demands.

Crane's green, glowing eyes seem to give him an unimpressed look as he refuses to comply and sticks a key into a lock on the door, "Experiment Augmentation had a very lengthy incubation period, but now that I can harness a fraction of its power, I've been able to procure quite substantial results."
