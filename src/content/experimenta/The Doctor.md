_Markus hugged his legs as he buried his face. The oppressive glint of slick white floors had become the embodiment of fear for him. Every time he opened his eyes and saw the blank walls, white-washed ceiling, and the lack of distinguishable lights, an intense fear creeped into his mind and made him start crying again. He promised himself he'd stop crying, he promised himself. But he just can't._

_He's only been here one week and all he's done is cry. Cry between each session. Cry during every meal._

_If only he could just see one of those other children—the one that had lined up next to him just a few days earlier. Maybe they'd be stronger than him and tell him to grow up. They could tell him how much of a baby he's being by crying._

_That day never came. In fact, he never saw any of those children again. Though he wouldn't find that out until much later._

_A faceless man spontaneously entered into his isolating room. A nametag rested on his colorless lab coat but Markus couldn't remember what it said on it._

_"Are you ready Markus?" the man asked, his voice casual, though Markus heard only an accusingly, demonic bellow._

_"Please..." he pleaded, "No more."_

_The man stepped over to him and crouched down in front of him, "Take heart boy. By this time next week you'll either be dead or the testing will be over. We're almost done."_

_Markus looked up with his eyes wide and cheeks red. He didn't understand what was going on or why this mean man would say these things, but at this point, he was loosing the ability to cry anymore._

_What did they want? Why was he taken from... Taken from? He knew he was supposed to go back to... back to... Where did he want to go again?_

_The tears stopped. The fear remained but it no longer got to him. The final trial came and went. The training began._

_The next eight years went by in a blur._

---

The HoV is even more barren than usual, likely due to the news of the hostage situation getting leaked. At this point he wonders if he's even seen this place during a busy rush.

The usual guards are posted at their respective locations with guns drawn and a hound-dog attitude. He receives a few shocked looks as the guards realize that he has Jaylin and a bound individual with him, but no one bars his entrance as he heads straight towards Cleo's office. 

As they cross by one of the many casinos on the block, the doctor glances over listening for the clinking of coins or rolling of slot machines. Or maybe he's searching for the faint aroma of homemade booze. 

"What a juxtaposition," the doctor comments to himself upon finding the place eerily silent.

He's about to arrive to Cleo's office but Jaylin stops him saying that there's no way she'd be there at five in the morning. Instead she tells him to stay put with the hostage and she'll go retrieve the faction leader.

Markus patiently complies.

A few minutes later, Cleo appears from a nearby hallway and opens her eyes in utter shock. Given that Jaylin was tagging behind, he assumes the situation has been explained to some degree.

"I said I didn't need your help," Cleo starts the conversation with as she storms over to him and the rough-looking man.

"Right," Markus counters immediately which honestly seems to catch Cleo off guard, "Now that I've help you, I need some help in return."

Cleo stops in her tracks as she studies the strange man. The doctor studies her equally in return.

"Who is this?"

"I'm Doctor Crane," the doctor says immediately, "It's such a pleasure to meet you."

"Shut it," Markus asserts shaking the cultist, "This man is extremely dangerous. I need to interrogate him, but I don't have a place to do it."

Cleo gives the man a once over again as if gauging a threat level off appearances alone, "So you saved one hostage only to flip the table."

Markus shakes his head, "This isn't an Eagle member, he's someone I've been hunting for quite a while."

"I see," is all Cleo responds as she looks around the room in thought, "Ah, I guess there is one way I can help you. I take it that this is your top priority at the moment."

Markus nods.

"Alright," she says waving to following, "I'll lead you there."

Cleo leads Markus to an inconspicuous building at the corner of the HoV. From there, they go around back and descent a flight of stares into a musty basement. The staircase leads to a gray corridor with pipes hanging on one end; a single row of sparse lights dot the ceiling. Without wavering, Cleo leads him down the corridor until they arrive at a small room. She slides open the heavy, iron door and allows Markus and the prisoner to enter first. 

The subsequent room doesn't appear to be made for interrogations as a large water boiler sits in one corner of the room with the remainder of the place being completely empty save a single chair.

"The boiler isn't being operated so everything in the room is disposable," Cleo explains, "Take your time. I'm going to get someone to guard this door in case you want to keep him here for a bit."

Markus nods as the door behind him slams shut. Then, without a hint of remorse, Markus shoves the doctor into the chair which nearly topples over from the momentum. 

"Careful," the doctor comments with a little laugh, "Wouldn't want to damage the chair."

"I hope you realize that you're not walking out of this alive," Markus asserts. Typically this would be the last thing you want to tell someone who is being interrogated, but he hopes it will work as a sort of reverse psychology if he ever decides to open that pathway for potential information. 

"Oh yes I am," the doctor decides very confidently, "I'm here on my own volition."

"Sure," Markus says as he crosses his arms and contemplates how he wants to approach this. Interrogations were never his strong suit—that was Grant or Wilbur's job—but he has a few tricks in his play book.

The man's head droops down as he hangs it over his shoulders to look at the floor and the chair itself. A clear bald spot reveals itself on the top of his head. The skin underneath is cracked and dry like he had a terrible case of dandruff but never bothered to wash it, or maybe some sort of surgical procedure requiring hundreds of stiches. Knowing a cultist, it could be either.

The doctor looks up, "Bold assertions, a false sense of fear, and subjective authority. Those aren't techniques I'd use for information extraction, but I'm curious how you intend to play this. Consider my full attention at your disposal."

Markus narrows his eyes as he fingers his knife, "Very well... First of all, I want to know who you are—beyond your name."

Doctor Crane smiles at the question as if the words alone stroked his ego, "Someone I know has a very particular philosophy on names. It's quite ingenious how he explains it, honestly. The universal grounding of truth and implicit categorization—simply marvelous... ah, I seem to be digressing."

"You are," Markus agrees.

The doctor nods, "As I said, I'm Doctor Crane. I'm the top doctor of the Organization. I work primarily with... what you might call alchemy, though that's such an archaic term for what I actually do. In reality, my work is often seen as dubious and dangerous, but progress requires risk."

"Right," Markus comments passively, "Since you're so talkative, would you mind enlightening me on where your headquarters is?"

The cultist gives him a strange look, almost confused, "What do you mean? You were the one who destroyed our base in the Near East, why wouldn't you know?"

With a sigh, Markus paces a few steps across the room, "If that were the end of the Organization, we wouldn't be here."

"Ah," the doctor musses, "You're out to exterminate us."

Markus shakes his head, "Putting it that way makes me seem like the bad guy. After all you've done, I'd argue it's nothing more than karma."

"Don't misunderstand me, I'm not insulted by your motives. I actually support it, please, by all means, kill us all. Kill every last member of the Organization."

Markus narrows his eyes at the doctor continues.

"Though I must know—for curiosity's sake—how do you plan on dispatching all of the Experiments? Affiliates of the Organization are trivial. After all, they're still human. But what about the Experiments? What is your plan for them?"

Raising his chin and drawing his knife for effect, Markus smirks, "If they're in my way. They'll die too."

Whether that statement is actually attainable is hardly relevant to Markus. He's long since resolved that he'll see this mission through—regardless of the feasibility.

Doctor Crane narrows his tired-looking eyes, "You're not familiar with Experiments, are you?"

"Of course I am," Markus defends, "The things the Organization put me through naturally exposed me to Experiments. I've seen plenty. Heck, one of those Experiments is currently running rampant across the entire planet."

The cultist snickers, drawing Markus' attention even closer, "It seems you still know very little. It's heartening to know that you're safe little bubble is about to pop."

Taking a few steps towards him, Markus glares down at the cultist incredulously. These cultists have tried to manipulate him in the past, he's not going to make the same mistake twice, "Cut the bullshit, where can I find the next cultist."

Doctor Crane raises a bushy eyebrow at the term 'cultist', "Hm? You really want to know?"

Markus just stares down at him.

"Take heart, you don't need to look. If you haven't felt the tremors already, then know that the _Cult_ is coming for you."

Markus takes a step back. Should he believe him? Is it a lie? Well, as much as he hates to admit it, he has been feeling the foreshocks recently. Volker showing up, the strange man, and now a cultist finally revealing himself. All of this has taken place over the last few days after nearly three years of stale leads. He'd be stupid to recognize that something is coming—for better or for worse.

"Go ahead and sit tight," Markus finally decides, "This conversation isn't over."

Doctor Crane remains silent and uncomfortably observant as Markus shuts the heavy metal door behind him.

"How'd it go? That seemed rather quick." Cleo eyes him curiously as Markus inspects the door.

"Will this door hold him if he tries to escape?"

To answer this, Cleo grabs onto a metal arm and promptly drops it in front of the door. She then pulls a lock out of her pocket, slides it through a loop in between the arm and a metal band on the wall, and clicks the lock shut.

"This is the only way out," she explains, "He'll be stuck in there until you're ready."

"Good," Markus comments with a deep breath as he starts off towards the way he came in.

He knows there is most certainly a wealth of information just waiting to be extracted from that cultist, but he can feel himself loosing his edge. It's been years since he's had a proper conversation with someone affiliated with the Cult. The best option is to take a break, recuperate his thoughts, and meticulously plot out what he wants to know and how he hopes to extract said information.

Cleo leans forwards as they walk, "So who exactly is he?"

Markus glances over at her as they ascend the stairs, "Let's just call him a butcher and leave it there."

"So he really is a terrible guy," Cleo muses.

When they exit out the door to the side of the building, Markus takes the usual turn towards a back exit, but Cleo has another idea as she grabs his wrist.

"Did you still need something?" Markus asks, turning back to her, "I'll come back tomorrow or the next day to finish my conversation with him. Don't bother feeding him in the meantime. Otherwise, I'll consider us even so long as that bastard doesn't escape."

"That's... now what I was hoping to talk about," Cleo says, letting go of his arm and folding her hands behind her back, "Would you… just this once… want to grab a drink with me. You don't need to worry about a crowd around the bar, it'll be just us."

"Sorry, I'm underaged," Markus says jokingly but it comes out like he's serious.

"No you're not!" Cleo exclaims skeptically, "...are you?"

Markus chuckles briefly at the remark he finds rather humorous. "I just turned… 22 I believe, so I guess I'm old enough."

"Then…" Cleo prompts.

Markus looks away in thought for a moment, "I... don't think that's a good idea..."

"Come on," Cleo complains, "All I want to talk casually. I don't have any ulterior motives. I promise."

Swallowing hard, Markus takes a deep breath and looks back at her, "Fine... but only this once."

_Markus checked his hand for a small slip of paper as he entered the high school office. His current class, English, just so happened to have a substitute teacher today. The sub took attendance on a small sheet of paper and then randomly selected a 'lucky' student to take the paper to the office. If it wasn't already obvious, Markus won that lottery and now found himself passing the attendance slip off to the secretary, who was by no means playing minesweeper. He turned to head back to class but paused when he noticed Monica sitting in a chair next to the principal's office._

_"I didn't expect to see you here," Markus joked._

_Monica looked up from her nihilistic gaze quickly realizing that it was Markus, "Oh… yeah. I guess I didn't expect to be here either."_

_"May I ask what happened?" he attempted, not trying to sound insistent. _

_Monica sighed deeply as she laid her hands weakly on her legs, "Some girl was slandering Sammy's name, saying that she did something unspeakable. I told her not to lie which… turned out to be the wrong call. I didn't intend for the argument to start, I wanted to stay neutral if anything, I even tried to defuse it, but Sammy caught wind of what was happening… Let's just say the two of them are having a long conversation with the principal right now."_

_Markus gave her a curious look, "So why did you get brought here too?"_

_Monica shrugged, clearly not proud or content with the situation, "To be fair, I did start the whole fiasco by telling the girl to stop slandering my friends name. I guess I kind of deserve to be here… I guess I'm just torn between staying loyal to my friends and doing what I feel is right, you know. I should have just stayed quiet."_

_With little left to say, Markus let his hands slip into his pockets. "Hey, what lunch do you have?"_

_Monica glanced up to him and pursed her lips, "I… uh… second lunch," she finally admitted._

_Markus smiled already knowing what the answer would be. He didn't actively take note of the people around him during something as mundane as lunchtime, but after speaking with her at the party, he couldn't help but notice her the next day._

_"Caden and I sit in the corner of the cafeteria next to the windows. If you want, and only if you want, you could join us. It's just us so you don't need to feel like you're jumping into another crowd."_

_Monica offered him an apprehensive look before slowly nodding her head, "I'll think about it."_

_"Sure," Markus said as he departed the office. He had a sinking feeling that today's lunch was going to be a particularly special one._

Ten minutes later, Markus sits at a bar in the main casino lobby with Cleo next to him and a silent bartender quietly cleaning glasses that have probably already been cleaned multiple times. Cleo sips on her glass filled with a dark red liquid.

"Normally we only serve homemade moonshine, but today's special so I pulled out the expensive wine."

Markus looks down at his own glass and analyzes the fifty year old alcohol. He isn't a fan of wine per say, but the least he can do is try it out. Upon splashing on his tongue he is hit with the rich sweetness of the aged wine which is quickly followed by that signature aftertaste. Again, he's no critic but he can tell this is quality stuff.

"I can't believe you actually said yes," Cleo laughs as she sets her glass down on the counter.

Markus takes another sip, "What do you mean?"

Cleo smiles and rests her elbow on the counter promptly placing her head on that hand. She leans a bit to the side as her eyes soften, "I've been trying to seduce you for years now and you've evaded me for so long. What caused the change of heart?"

Markus narrows his eyes, "That's not what's happening here."

Cleo shrugs, "Oh please, humor me a little."

"Sure," Markus agrees, "But know that I have a terrible sense of humor—truly the worst there's ever been."

Cleo gives him a weird look before sitting up straight and taking another drink of her wine, this time emptying the glass. "Gosh, after drinking low quality drinks for so long, this stuff really hits the spot."

Markus nods.

"Another round please," Cleo says to the bartender who quickly pulls out the bottle and fills her glass. He offers it to Markus but he declines as his glass is still half full.

Cleo glances back over to Markus, "What's your story? Why are you the way that you are?"

Markus opens his mouth, cambering a redirected comment, but Cleo keeps going.

"I know you don't like to talk, but I'm just trying to understand you. What makes you so confident and competent. I've seen people train their entire lives and still can't hold a candle to what you just did a few hours ago. Sometimes I think you're almost too good to be true, like a dream or something, but then you show up for the next job and I realize it was real. And yet, despite all that, you still walk your path alone… why?"

Markus' gaze falls to the counter as he places both hands on his drink. He's never told anyone about his past. The time he spent in Allegiance One and the time he spent in the Cult are times he only speaks of in nightmares. It's not that he's trying to hide it or anything like that. He is simply afraid of what people would think when he tells them that he's killed people his entire life and was raised to slaughter, not to save. His past has tainted his every move. Then there's what he did just four years ago... the thought alone makes him sick.

Markus looks up to the bartender and gestures with his head. The bartender takes the hint and walks around a corner out of view.

"The life we're living right now is an accurate description of my childhood," Markus says, catching Cleo off guard. She quickly leans in, intrigued. "Waking up every morning and not knowing whether I'll be fighting for my life with words or with actions is something I've always done. I don't... I don't actually remember where I'm from. I never had a mother who held me and told me everything was going to be okay. I never had a father who set me straight when I acted up. I just had work… and that work was... Well... there's a reason I don't tell people about myself…" Markus stands up, "And now the wine tastes sour. Sorry about that."

Markus gets maybe three steps towards the door when he feels Cleo press herself up against his back with her arms wrapped around him. He stands completely still as she holds him in silence. She allows him to soak in the sentiment before saying anything.

"I don't blame you," she says softly, practically whispering it into his ear, "I'm so sorry you had to carry that alone."

"It's fine," Markus says softly, still trying to wrap his head around it all, "Sentimentality is the first thing they burned out of me. I'm sorry, but I think it's a character flaw I won't ever be able to overcome."

"I don't care," Cleo responds quickly, "My father used to hire hitman to do terrible things, but I still loved him. Despite what he did—what he was doing—he still made time to care for me and Jaylin. He knew who he was and didn't let the world or his circumstances decide for him. I think you can be the same way."

Cleo loosens her grip and allows Markus to step out of the hug and look at her.

"Maybe you're right," Markus says, unsure if he truly believes it just yet, "It's not like I wanted to be this way... but."

"But?" Cleo wonders.

Markus' miniscule amount of hope quickly falters as he remembers the disgusted faces, the tears, the rejection. The Cult conditioned him, the war reinforced him, but what came after... that's what broke him. He looks towards the ground and closes his eyes, "It's too late for me. I have no desire to stop until I've completed my mission... Sometimes it's all I have left."

Breaking his pitiful words is two cold, soft hands pressing up against his cheeks. He fights a natural reaction to push it away and defend himself and instead just sits along for the ride. His eyes meet Cleo's, who are wide and determined.

"I'm not going to ask about the trauma of who you've lost or why your personal pledge weighs on you so, but know that I just want to help you. I don't tease you because I think that you're perfect. I do it because I see someone inside of you worth aspiring to be like... it's something my father used to say, and I never understood it until I met you."

"How naive," Markus comments in foul taste. He says it mostly as a commentary on how low he's stooped in the past.

Cleo grabs Markus shirt and shakes his as her voice grows more dramatic and emotional, "Why don't you understand? Every time we talk, I do the little seductive girl routine and you play the emotionally distant soldier. As much as I love doing that, it doesn't mean anything if I'm the only one who's acting."

Markus swears he can see his reflection in her sparkling eyes that seem to glimmer in the moody lighting of the bar. What he sees is not some confident soldier but a feeble child barely strong enough to hold his head above his shoulders.

Markus clenches his fist, "I'm sorry…" he says slowly, unable to make eye contact anymore, "Where I'm going..." he struggles to say the last part, "There's likely no coming back."

Even if there is... does he really want to come back?

Cleo hesitates. For a brief moment she's completely speechless. Then, when her thoughts settle she responds with a firm tone, "And you're only hurting your odds by being alone. If not me than anyone else."

"That's what Grant used to say..."

Cleo stares at him for a good long while, enough time for Markus to overthink every word of the conversation. Eventually she loosens her grip and lets Markus go. 

"I guess no matter how hard I try, I'm not going to be able to break through that thick shell of yours," Cleo grabs her glass and chugs the whole thing wiping away the remnants of the drink with her wrist. 

Markus stands up not bothering to touch his glass again, "You know where to find me if you need me."

He heads for the door only for a silenced bullet to pass through the door, narrowly missing his head.
