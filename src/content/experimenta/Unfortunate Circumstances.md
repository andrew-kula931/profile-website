Selling this many medical drugs at one time is a good way to get jumped—if if he has a solid reputation. Therefore, it's often a good idea to split up the bounty into more reasonable portions. This also has the added benefit that small amounts lead to higher individual prices. Given the high demand and limited supply, it just makes for a profitable endeavor that will allow him to extract some, otherwise unattainable, information. 

The Market is rather slow today with most of the shops closed including Misty's. Luckily though his target vendor is still there: the Raven Faction. 

Brian sits there at the stand today, the same guy who was at the faction meeting a few days prior. The Raven faction is a group of mercenaries so medication unfortunately isn't their specialty, but they have money and enough connections to act as a sort of wholesaler for Markus. 

He knows the Nurse faction will purchase the supplies without question but he refuses to do business with them outright. The reason is because of corruptions within the Nurse's ranks. On the surface they help people whenever they can for free, but the catch is, nothing is ever free. When the Nurse faction saves someone's life they accumulate debt from that person for them to "collect" later on. Most people have come to realize this, but the Nurse have close ties to the Eagle faction which is the second largest mercenary faction after the Raven. 

Conversely, the Ravens, while still having their flaws and hot headed moments, are more decent humans in Markus' eyes. If these drugs never get sold in general he would be okay with it. As an added bonus, they also are quite informed due to their size and reach across the city and if information goes cold, they always have a healthy supply of ammunition they can spare. 

"Markus?" Brian asks as he approaches, "Curious to see you here."

Markus sets a small sack of drugs on the table making sure none of the contents are flashed. They may have a bit of bad blood from after he took down the Colossal but he'll push that under the rug for the time being, "I could say the same thing, why is a faction leader running a humble vendor stall?"

Brian laughs, "The guy who was supposed to do it today had a sudden emergency and had to leave. I'd ask someone else to do it, but I need a break from the crazy shit going on in the city. If nothing else, haggling is a sort of hobby of mine."

"I guess I get that," Markus admits with a polite shrug.

"Anyways Markus," Brian begins with a sudden shift in tone to one much more sincere, "Seeing how you're still sane, I want to apologize for my previous actions." Markus recalls how Brian accused him of being infected in the past. To be honest, he can't fault the man as he didn't rightly know how he survived either. "I came off a bit irrational back there and said some things that may have been uncalled for."

"It's fine," Markus responds, not really having any well-worded answer to ease his fears.

"When you walked out alive, I was relieved just like everyone else. But then they all started kissing your boots as you just brushed everything off. I have no reason to hate you, but I just can't stand it when the small guys are blinded by reputation. It's why I can't give Reign the time of day to listen to him half the time."

"Brian, it's fine," Markus argues, "I tried to defuse the situation the best I could. With how Cleo described it, I think you may have been the most rational one there. If it makes you feel any better, I did feel a bit under the weather yesterday. But as you can see, I'm fine now."

Brian studies his face for a few moments, "You sure we're good?"

Markus nods.

Brian sighs and relaxes in his chair behind the stall, "So, what do you got for me today?"

Markus, glad that the conversation is progressing, opens the bag and shows the contents to Brian before closing it quickly to avoid prying eyes. 

"Damn, that's quite the haul. Why would I have any interest in it?"

Markus, maintaining a straight face, explains, "Don't play dumb with me, we both know these aren't things you get very often and with how the city is playing out we both know everything helps."

Brian leans forward against the stand and his expression narrows, "You still haven't answered my question. Why would I be interested in what you have there?"

"Because if you buy it, I'll tell you where I got it."

Brian gives a smug grin, "Smart man. Now what form of payment do you prefer?"

Markus crosses his arms with the bag still in hand, "Bullets… and a few personal questions."

"I can manage that, but I can't promise you an answer you'll be content with."

Markus nods slightly, "That's fine by me. How about a box of 7.62?"

Brian, almost taken aback by the offer, digs in to counter, "I'll give you half, prices on this kind of stuff only rises with time."

Markus digs in, "Make it a box and a half and I'll double what I have here."

Brian hesitates in thought but doesn't seem disinterested. Eventually he responds with, "Fine, you got yourself a deal, now tell me what these personal questions are."

Markus holds out his hand promptly receiving a handshake, "Do you know anything about a guy named Harvey?"

Brian contemplates it for a moment stroking his short beard a few times, "Nah, doesn't ring a bell."

"I ran into a intimidating man recently. Wears a hoodie, doesn't talk much, and has an extremely oppressive presence. Know anyone by that description?" Markus continues.

Brian shrugs, "I've met a ton of people. I can't really recall anyone by that description specifically, though that's a lot coming from you."

"What about a Revenant? Heard anything about that?"

Brian freezes as his slight smirk fades like a rush of water flowed over him carrying off any positivity, "Actually I have. I've heard a few reports recently from my men of a parasite that acts abnormally. Matches the description of the rumors and doesn't run straight at the first person it sees. I'd advise you keep your distance from that one. A Colossal is one thing, but an intelligent parasite gives me cold feet."

"I concur," Markus agrees, "I ran into it at the Mall. Tell everyone you know to stay clear."

With a careful look, Brian raises an eyebrow, "The Mall? That place has been overrun since the Fall. It's been a hive ever since. How in the world did you get in there?"

Avoiding eye contact and releasing a sigh, Markus waves a hand off of his shoulder, "I have my ways."

Brian narrows his eyes, "Sure... anything else?"

Markus shakes his head, "That's all I've got for now."

Brian's serious composure quickly fades as he relaxes once again in his chair, "Say, it's rare that someone haggles a price up from what I offer. But to be fair, medicine like this is on the rise too. A lot of people are getting sick these days." He grabs the bag of medicine and replaces it with the agreed upon bounty, "Just bring in the rest next time you're around."

"Are people really getting sick?" Markus wonders.

"Yeah, I think I know more sick people than healthy ones," Brian explains, "Now where'd it come from? The goods, I mean."

Markus grins and puts his hands in his pocket, "The Mall."

"The hell you did!" Brian nearly stands up, "You said there was a Revenant there."

Markus nods, controlling his bout of pride and returning to general humility. 

Brian studies Markus' face trying to find deceit to no avail. "That's impossible," he finally says, "Even for you, a Revenant is... well lets just say there's a reason rumors spread about it before it was even confirmed to exist... You didn't kill it, did you?"

Markus shakes his head, "No, I actually almost got bested. That thing is going to be tough to take down."

Brian shakes his head a few times and bites his bottom lip trying to get a gauge of the man in front of him, "No matter where I go, I'm stuck learning things I wish I had never learned," Brian waves a hand in front of his face to change the subject, "Anyways, I heard that the House and the Eagles are having a bit of a fuss so you might want to check that out. I'd do it myself but… I don't really care about big factions like theirs, also, as you can tell my hands are full," he gestures to the stand.

"Says the guy running one of the biggest factions," Markus scoffs and waves his hands to his sides.

With a little laugh, Brian leans back in his chair, "We may be big on paper, but we're small at heart. Got to watch out for the little guys. Besides, we're both hypocrite. At least I don't say I'm unbiased."

Markus narrows his eyes a bit at the underhanded comment, but doesn't warrant it with a response.

The conversation goes dull for a moment as Markus puts his newly acquired bullets into his bag and takes a look around. When nothing else picks the conversation back up, Markus simply nods his head and starts off.

He was planning on handing some of the medical supplies off to Elizabeth as she seems to be the most responsible person in the city—and she can afford to pay a high price. That being said, Brian's warning about the HoV and the Eagles is rather concerning. He doesn't particularly care much for inter-faction affairs, but the Eagle faction has a bad history of getting into trouble. If they find themselves picking on a goliath like the HoV then people might be in trouble. 

It bugs him that he's even considering it, but some lingering conscience of his makes his eventual decision harder to refuse.

The headquarters of the HoV is unsurprisingly empty for another mid-day visit. The guards wave him in without issue and he finds Cleo in her office making a call using the new Beacon. By the time he opens the door to the room Cleo is already off-call and leaning over her desk with an interestingly restrained excitement in her eyes.

"Markus!" she exclaims as Markus shuts the door behind him.

Cleo's normal attire of casual clothes seems to be absent today as a full length camouflage vest and pants cover her body. The full military outfit is a bit out of character for such a laid back individual, but he decides that she's simply missing the performative face paint. Yeah that's what she's missing.

"I see something's going down," Markus starts after his ill timed once over.

Cleo looks down at her clothes and sighs. Markus expects her to act all arroused or seductive by such an action but she surprisingly does no such thing. 

"We just got a threat from the Eagle faction to stop hoarding food," she explains with undertones of irritation, "Our acquisition of rations is the same as it has always been. Those snobby Eagles weren't even at the meeting and now they're just swinging around accusations… I heard the Retainers are experiencing similar problems."

Cleo leans back against the wall and folds her arms in thought. "I'm trying my best to keep everyone safe and secure, but things aren't looking good. It's not even the famine I'm worried about, it's the civil war that might break out over food." Her eyes grow sad and it becomes apparent to Markus that she's carrying something deeper than she's letting on. 

He hesitates about whether or not he wants to bear the responsibility of knowing that. Over the past three years, he's gotten used to Cleo's forward, exaggerated personality, so when she acts contrary to that persona, it does more than just unsettle him, it confuses him. His training and experience don't equip him for stuff like this.

She continues, "They… they took Jaylin."

"How'd that happen…" Markus mutters, finding that Cleo's eyes strained as she glares at the floor.

"I sent most of my staff out to meetings in other factions, just so we could make sure to make as many connections as possible before the worst would happen. Then, when our backs were turned, they stole somebody that they knew would deal a critical blow to me," her crossed arms slowly tighten into a self hug.

Markus rubs his chin as he looks around the simple office, "But why Jaylin?," he asks, trying to piece it together.

Cleo turns away and stares at the map of the city against the wall, "It's because... she's my sister." 

The room goes bitterly silent as countless meaningless interactions are reevaluated inside Markus' mind. Every back handed compliment, sly remark, and suspicious gaze he's ever gotten from Jaylin comes to the forefront of his mind. Beyond a basic mental profile, Markus can't even say he knows Jaylin, but hearing that she and Cleo were family... well... that changes things.

"I'm sorry," Cleo apologizes out of nowhere, "You probably have no interest getting wrapped up into my own affairs."

Almost instantly, Markus shakes his head, "Don't apologize. You have nothing to be sorry for."

Taking a seat at her desk, Cleo holds her head in her hands as she releases a deep groan, "What am I supposed to do? The HoV can't afford to pay their ransom but they'll kill my sister if I don't."

"Wouldn't they assume that you have a duty to your people," Markus points out, "I don't know what they're demanding, but even so, wouldn't they know that one life isn't worth the suffering of an entire faction."

Cleo looks up and glares at him—a looks she's never given him before, "I know you're right, but I don't like how you phrased that."

Markus purses his lips, "Sorry, maybe that was insensitive."

With a sigh, Cleo leans back in her chair, "It's okay. Whether I like it or not, I can't bring myself to pay up and they know it."

"Then why would they propose it in the first place?"

Cleo looks up at Markus slowly, "They likely want me to negotiate the price down and then they'll settle, but only after throwing on their true charge."

Markus crosses his arms, "And that is?"

Cleo shrugs and shakes her head, "That I don't know."

Mulling over the situation, Markus can suspect a few potential motivations, but given his limited exposure to the Eagle faction, he supposes all of that is conjecture. If he wants to really help the situation, his knowledge isn't the best option here. The question for him is how involved he wants to be. Obviously, he wants to help in some way, after all, Cleo has been a rather loyal employer for odd jobs here and there and provided a plethora of information over the years. That fact alone warrants a bit of assistance, but does he really want to get hands on here?

"What other options do you have?" Markus inquires.

Cleo spins about ninety degrees in her air to glance at the map once more, "If I were as smart as my dad, then maybe I'd know."

Markus leans against the door with a sigh, "Do you at least know when your deadline to respond is?"

Her face immediately goes sour as she grits her teeth, "...tomorrow."

Of course time is a pressure here. The Eagles must want something badly to go to such extremes. By making the deadline tomorrow, Cleo won't have time to network and coordinate resources to mount a counter offensive. Not that violence is the answer, but if she could get the Retainers or even the Ravens on her side, this wouldn't be a one sided hostage situation anymore. The Eagle reputation may struggle because of this situation, but that's hardly a concern while famine is on the rise.

"I can't promise anything," Markus begins, "But I can take a look."

Cleo looks up from her desk, "Markus. You don't have to do that. This is my problem to resolve."

He knows that Cleo's the type of person to beg for help on trivial matters, but the second things actually matter, it's suddenly all her responsibility. This disposition isn't something he'd ordinarily notice, yet here he is making the clear distinction. Maybe he should be benevolent this one time and try and help her out—save Jaylin if he has to. Maybe that is the sort of thing someone like him aught to do. Despite that, he can't bring himself to accept the premise of such a risk. Going against an entire faction solo is almost guaranteed to lead to bloodshed and he doesn't want to be the source of another massacre—not again.

"If there's anything I can do," Markus begins as he reaches for the door and looks back, "Just let me know."

Cleo offers a half smile, "Yeah... It's funny. That's the first time you've ever said that, yet I'm the one denying you... I guess I finally know how you feel."

Markus nods silently as he steps out of the room and heads back to his bike. Upon arriving back, he leans on the light pole that his bike is chained to. Standing there in thought, he looks to the ground.

He could scout the Eagle camp out; try and provide Cleo with just a tidbit of information. Another option could be to help mediate this upcoming, biased negotiations. While those are definitely valid options, one is largely inconsequential given the situation and the other isn't his style. Honestly, is there anything he can do to help?

"Boom."

Markus' head snaps back with his pistol drawn. What he finds is not an actual explosion, but a smirking man with a detonator in hand.

"You must really be out of it," Jenks comments as he waves the detonator around, "You didn't even check you bike when you got back."

Looking down immediately, Markus finds a peculiar block attacked to the top of his wheel. The object is inconspicuously positioned underneath the wheel guard, but is still easily recognizable to anyone who's seen the motorcycle for the past three years. It doesn't take him long to identify the object as a remote explosive.

"Are you getting rusty?" Jenks wonders.

Markus shakes his head with a sigh, "No, C4 is just... quite rare these days. I think I'm the only one in the city who has any left."

Jenks purses his lips as he steps away from the alley way he was hiding in and walks up to Markus. They exchange a casual handshake before Jenks detaches the explosive from the vehicle.

"I though you were going to contact me via the Beacon," Markus thinks aloud, "Did something change?"

Pulling his backpack off and stashing the explosive away, Jenks returns to his feet and shakes his head, "No, finding you was actually a coincidence—I came by for a different reason."

"A different reason?"

Jenks points towards the HoV, conveniently only a block or so away, "You know what this place is?"

Markus nods, "Yeah, it's the House of Vegas. It's one of the larger factions here in the city."

"You frequent it often?"

Giving him a quizzical look, Markus half laughs, "Why does that matter? I frequent a lot of places."

Jenks narrows his eyes briefly as if catching on to something, but then his eyes widen, almost in a performative manner, "There's something special here. Isn't there?"

Markus sighs, "No, it's not like that."

"Right," Jenks comments knowingly as he slides his hands into his pockets and glances back at the HoV, "You were talking about the hostage situation, weren't you?"

Markus takes a look around to ensure that they're alone. It's not that this conversation needs to be off the record, but he'd prefer to keep it as quiet as possible for the time being.

"How'd you know about that?" Markus finally asks.

Jenks pulls a phone out of his pocket and swipes through a few apps. Once he finds the he was searching for, he proceeds to tap through a few prompts. After all is said and done, he flips the phone over to Markus to reveal a map with multiple dots scattered across the screen. He immediately identifies the map as Pearson.

"I was poking around that new beacon, you know, doing some man-in-the-middle stuff and a bit of triangulation with device locations—nothing too crazy. Anyways, I intercepted some suspicious messages using http... they were unencrypted so reading them was trivial," As Jenks explains, Markus just nods his head to the parts he doesn't understand, "...I think I might be on the trail of a cultist."

Markus demeanor immediately changes to that of concern as he double checks his surroundings—this time he actually wants it to be off the record, "Tell me what you know."

Jenks nods, "It was in another language—hence why it was suspicious—so I had to pull out some references. Anyway, these messages were talking about some sort of hostage or... prisoner. When I caught wind of the current dilemma, I thought there may be a connection."

"What language?" Markus immediately prompts, "I know there are a few bilinguals in the city. They might be with the Eagles—the faction with the hostage."

With a quick shake of his head, Jenks zooms in on a particular location. By the organization of the buildings, Markus notices it's centered on a spot not to far from here. More specifically, it's centered on the main street running straight through the HoV.

"It wasn't a spoken language," Jenks explains, "It was Latin, and one of the communications came from this region."

Markus looks back at the walled off House of Vegas street. There's no way. The whole time, one of the cultists were hiding under his nose. Something has to be off here.

"Cool it," Jenks asserts as he slides his phone back in his pocket, "Let's not catastrophize. There's a chance that I may have misinterpreted something."

"Do you think it has anything to do with the current hostage situation."

Jenks shrugs, "I'd be naive to say that there isn't, but I can't confirm that there is."

Markus reaches over to his bike and kicks his leg over the seat. He then pulls the keys out of his pocket and sticks them into the ignition, "That settles it."

"Settles what?" Jenks wonders.

With a side eye, Markus cracks his neck, "I've got to go save someone."

This announcement does not catch Jenks off guard, however. Instead he just chuckles and offers his classic knowing smirk. He then adjusts his backpack and nods, "I see where this is going. I hope you know that I'm all in—just like old times."

Markus begins to smile but then it fades just as quickly. He crosses his arms and looks down, "I know you're good, but I think I work better alone."

Jenks narrows his eyes as he purses his lips, "Well that's interesting. Never thought you'd turn down my help."

Markus averts his gaze, "It's complicated. A lot has happened since Allegiance One disbanded."

With sigh, Jenks shrugs his shoulders, "I get it, you've been through some shit. Heck, we all have. That being said, I still see you as my brother—nothing is going to change that."

"That's not what I mean—" Markus attempts.

Jenks holds out a hand to stop him, "I hear you, but I'm going to help you regardless of what you say. You'd be wise to accept the help."

Markus takes a deep breath. Of all the people left in this world, Jenks is likely the person he trusts the most. He's also the person most entangled with the Cult which means he'll have a target on his back regardless of what he does. Even so, Markus has grown accustomed to being a solo operative. Working with Jenks would be just like old times, but it would also lead to old habits—ones that he doesn't want to risk falling back into. Then again, if he does start to slip again... Jenks is likely the only person who can pull him back.

"Okay," Markus finally agrees with a seemingly inevitable smirk, "It'll be just like old times."
