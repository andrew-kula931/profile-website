The Market is the center of commerce in the city of Pearson—or rather, what is left of it. For the most part, it's free of any faction's control, allowing anyone of any background to enter without much issue. Though, because it's so open, everyone inside keeps an eye on an escape route in case things inside get dicey. Currency, as it was once perceived, no longer exists. Instead, one has to trade what other people see as valuable, namely weapons and medicine. Once upon a time, it may have been called bartering, but nowadays, it's just called normal.

Markus steps off his motorcycle and chains it to an old light pole. Most people keep to themselves, but theft is always a problem. He likes to imagine that between the custom modifications to his bike and his reputation, most thieves are kept at bay. Though, one can never be too careful.

He makes his way to the large, black gates with paint chippings peeling off over every square inch of them. Booths of merchants selling their wares litter the plaza in front of City Hall. Vendors from all around the city sell miscellaneous curiosities, such as hand-crafted utensils, mechanical gear parts, and, most popularly, guns and ammunition. The weapon-selling booths are always the most heavily guarded, creating a mutually assured destruction scenario between the competing booths.

Markus disregards every booth except for one particularly eccentric one. The booth itself is nothing more than a glorified tent with banners and old posters, but within is a large collection of trinkets and valuables that serve as trash to most, but treasure to a select few. Atop this mess of priceless and worthless trinkets is a sign displaying a name in graffiti-like characters: Misty's Mansion.

“If it ain't my old pal Markus, what have you brought for me today?” Misty says heartily as ever.

Misty is a middle-aged woman with eyes that can see through anyone. He would know, as she tends to flaunt that ability every time they meet. Once upon a time, she was a collector, but now she sells the same items for measly scraps, save for the rare occasion that someone will pay top dollar for an object in her possession. She is also a half-way info broker to those she finds… tolerable.

“Some IDs I've collected and a little something special I came across,” Markus says as he sets his black bag on the table. Misty immediately opens the bag and browses through the assortment of names and faces. She shakes her head through most of them, but then stops on one: 'Kevin Roberts', the same ID Markus collected earlier today.

“I'll take this one. I know someone related to this guy, they will want it and the photo.” Misty removes the ID and photo from the pile and sets it aside. She then pours the rest off the table into a box on the ground. “You don't accept food stamps as payment, so how about I give you five .45s for the one and three 9mms for the rest?” she asks. Markus nods, prompting her to set the goods on the table as he pulls out a key from his pocket.

“As for the little something extra, here it is?”

Misty notices the key and quickly interjects, “Is that a key to General Reign's personal quarters?!”

“What? No, why do you say things like that? Just because his wife died doesn't mean he's available,” Markus shakes his head. “Regardless, this is a key to the storage room of the museum. I had to kill a Demo-class mutant to get it,” Markus sets the key down on the table. “Hans, the leader of the Preservers, would kill for this. He and the rest of his faction will pay an arm and a leg for any chance at maintaining this planet’s rich history and culture.”

Misty puts one hand on her chin and nods slightly. “You're right about that. I could get quite a haul for it, but why wouldn't you sell that to him directly? I don't have adequate payment for you.”

Markus sighs and replies, “It's the same reason I don't tell others that I'm available for hire. It gets me caught up in too many jobs.”

Misty looks at him incredulously, “And how's that going for you?”

“Oh, shut up! I take the jobs I want.”

“I'm just sayin'”, she throws her hands up defensively, “Workaholics like us gotta stick together.”

“Oh, don't lump me in with you,” Markus slices the air with his hand, “I work because I have to, you work because you want to. We are not the same.”

“Hey now,” Misty narrows her eyes accusingly, “The internet has been down for some time, so you should find some more relevant things to recite.”

Markus rolls his eyes, “Says the woman who constantly quotes Leonardo da Vinci. You know, I didn't even know the guy had any quotes until you started preaching them like gospel.”

With a smile on her face, she waves her hands defensively, “Woah, we don't make fun of quality here.”

“Right,” Markus says dryly as he rolls his eyes.

Misty tilts her head slightly and rests her hands on her hips, “Don't give me an attitude like that.”

Markus can't help but restrain a smile as well, “Okay, Mom.”

Misty doesn't buy into it, “How old do you think I am?”

“Sorry, I meant sister.”

“Step-sister,” Misty corrects.

“Don't make it weird,” Markus attempts loosely.

“It's not weird,” Misty defends, “Not unless I'm your type.”

“No,” Markus needs no time to think about that one.

“That's what I thought,” Misty nods her head slowly with a smile of superiority slowly creeping across her face, “You're not my type either. I prefer my men a bit older, maybe former military, potentially running a large faction in the city with the last name Reign.”

“No, I get it. I mean, I prefer a woman without gray hair.”

Misty’s mouth drops as wide as her affinity for sarcasm as she reaches to take back the bullets she set on the table, “It seems someone wants to lose a deal.”

“I’m sorry, I’m sorry,” Markus pleads, holding back a laugh at his own comment.

Misty releases her grip on the ammunition and purses her lips momentarily, “You’re lucky I like you.”

“A more mutual statement has never been spoken by the likes of you,” Markus jokes a bit dramatically.

“So when are you marrying Cleo?” Misty comments underhandedly.

“Don’t start this again,” Markus pleads.

“What? You and Cleo, Reign and I, we could have a double wedding.”

“Over my dead body.”

Misty crosses her arms, hardly restraining a smile, “Corpses are pretty popular around here, I might just take you up on that offer.”

The two stare at each other for a long moment of silence before they both burst into laughter, nearly forgetting that they were still in a public space. They quickly stop and regain their professional composure.

“I’ll give you a map of the military checkpoint Beta in return for the key,” Misty finally says, returning completely back to a calm demeanor.

“I’ve already been there, but I’m sure there’s something I missed, so deal.”

Misty reaches behind her makeshift counter and pulls out a rolled-up blueprint. She sets it on the table and Markus takes it along with the bullets, slipping them into his bag.

“Oh, and one more thing,” Misty adds, “Cleo came by earlier looking for you.”

Markus groans and turns away, “So that’s why you suggested we get married.”

“Indeed.”

Markus begins to walk away as he waves with his back turned, “I’ll see you around, Misty.”

“We both know I’ll see you first,” Misty calls back.

Given that the sun is still high in the sky, Markus elects to run home quickly before heading to meet with Cleo. Every time she calls on him, it ends up being for another job, which typically requires a bit of equipment and preparation.

When he arrives home, everything is as per usual: the generators are off and hidden from view, the curtains are closed to hide the boards, and the mounted turrets are on in the bushes. The exterior of his suburban house is completely inconspicuous to the untrained eye. That secrecy is the only thing keeping him from hostile looters burglarizing his stuff.

He enters inside with the passcode he’s never told anyone. The house is dark and as empty as it ever was. It makes Markus hesitate briefly as his eyes fall on the empty sofa that his father always sat at. After a brief moment, Markus goes over to the TV and programs a random weather report that he had recorded from before the Fall. Back then, Markus used to record everything, whether it was for photo editing or something else—he liked to record things, though mostly out of habit. Now, that old hobby gives a tiny bit of normality to his chaotic life. After the TV was programmed, he did the same with a random voice line of his mom, dad, or older sister waking him up. The last thing he did was enter his most secure room in his basement: the armory.

Most people these days are lucky to have a single AR and a box of rounds, but Markus is a bit of an exception to the rule. He has an assortment of six ARs, four shotguns of different gauges, eight sidearms, and various smaller pieces of equipment, like flashbangs. He grabs an AR, slings it over his back, a pistol for his hip, extra mags for both, a last-ditch utility, namely a frag grenade, and finally his pride and joy, his one and only modified sword.

Guns run out of ammo really quickly, which is why it is always in high demand at the market, but a sword he could swing without end. Furthermore, he's installed a plethora of modifications, making the broadsword a bit thinner to be less unwieldy but still heavy enough to pack a punch. The sword is also covered in a very special kind of translucent, mutated parasite fungus. The fungus is extraordinarily flammable, which makes it a first-line counter to heat-sensitive parasites. The moss burns out rather quickly, which is unfortunate, but he has an entire array of fungus cultures, which means he can always reapply it across the blade. His striker at the base of the hilt makes this utility seamless as well.

He lifts the sword into the air and inspects the spray-painted word: "Sterben" printed across the side. If he were to have a pride and joy, this would be it.

After equipping all his gear, he heads back to his motorcycle and prepares to face one of the most influential people in the city.

The House of Vegas is in the old entertainment district. The HoV isn’t contained to a singular building; instead, it is a series of shops, casinos, and clubs all connected under the control of the same group. The name itself is misleading—the HoV is a group of survivors who made their base on one big street, but they called it the House of Vegas as more of a calling card than a title: that name attracts business to them. Early on in the apocalypse, they managed to boot up the casino and the movie theater, so once a week, this street is bustling with thrill seekers desperate to forget about the horrors of the current world.

Their leader, Cleo, is a 24-year-old mafia boss with a little morality behind her—it’s the sole reason Markus hears her out in the first place. He never sees himself using the HoV for recreational purposes, but Cleo always pays well, so he puts up with her… unique take on negotiation.

Markus hides his motorcycle two blocks away and chains it to a light pole again. He often wonders if it’s even necessary, as no one else uses a motorcycle like his, so it would clearly be stolen if someone else drove it. Regardless of the truth behind that statement, he locks it up for his own personal mental security, if nothing else.

He soon arrives at the front gate and is greeted like any other customer and welcomed in. Getting past the front gates is easy for everyone, but the security detail at the HoV was tight, to put it lightly. Mounted turrets on each wall, at least two snipers on duty at all times, and an unprecedented amount of armed agents stationed at every major attraction. They arguably have the second-strongest security force in the city, only trailing closely behind General Reign’s Brigade Squad.

When the party isn’t actively occurring, the HoV is fairly bare, and only people browsing wares and looking for special services are there. Markus easily makes his way through the empty street and ends up at the headquarters of the district. Two guards stand outside wearing old SWAT uniforms. They're of the big, broad-shouldered sort who inform any would-be attacker that this is as far as they're getting. This fear is never realized for Markus, however, as they take notice of him and call out, “She’s waiting for you.”

Inside the headquarters, groups of miscellaneous, unnamed individuals stand around chatting, most planning trips outside the safe zone and others discussing who knows what. At the back of the room is an office; the door displays the words CLEO.

As Markus heads through the crowd, he takes note of various murmurs with his name injected into them. He prefers to avoid gossip about himself, but he concludes that there are inevitably limits to how much secrecy one man can have... If only he were more like Wilbur. Disregarding the crowd, he heads straight to Cleo's room and steps inside.

The small office space is a perfect balance between a standard white-collar room and a conspirator's secret plotting chamber. A map hangs on the wall which has been marked with various different symbols and photos pinned to it. A non-aggression pact hangs on another wall. In the center of the room is a desk with documents and a rare, but functional, computer.

Two men sit in chairs in front of the desk facing the lord of Vegas herself: Cleo. At present, she has on her business attire, which consists of anything she feels like wearing at the time. Today seems to be casual Thursday, as she has on a crop top with black leggings. Three years ago, presenting oneself so informally would be seen as disrespectful given that the two men are in full suit jackets, but an imbalance of power does things to people, and there is most definitely an imbalance of power in this room.

“I’m busy,” Cleo said coldly before looking up and finding Markus at the door, “Markus!” she exclaims, pulling a 180 in tone, “Uh, gentlemen, I’ll have to ask you to step out for a moment; feel free to enjoy a complimentary drink while you wait.”

The two men look at Markus and then at each other before standing up and exiting the room. They didn’t need to speak a word to know that they recognized this infamous figure. He’d be lying if he said his infamy didn’t serve as both a gift and a curse. It speeds interactions up, as people tend to keep their narcissism in check when they are faced with a real obstacle, but he also loses out on the ability to be discrete about almost anything. He just deals with the hand he’s dealt at this point.

Markus closes the door and turns to face Cleo again, but is instead greeted with a young woman throwing herself all over him. She puts her arms around his neck and tries in vain to go in for a kiss. Markus turns his head and sighs one last time.

“Please get off me,” Markus commands politely but sternly.

“Why do you always play hard to get? That’s my job,” Cleo complains as she begrudgingly removes herself from Markus’ personal space and goes back behind her desk. “How much did you say it was for a night with you?”

“A nuclear warhead and the horns of a Demon-class parasite,” Markus sarcastically explains with irritation added to his voice to further the point. “Now, why did you call for me?”

“Would you believe it if I said I just wanted to see you?”

“Yes, but that’s not good enough to warrant me being here.”

Cleo leans forward onto her desk with her cheeks puffed out just a little, “Come on now, can’t you humor me? Just for a bit.”

Markus takes a deep breath as he forces his hands into his pockets, “I came all the way out here, you might as well tell me about the job.”

“Fine,” Cleo opens a drawer and pulls out a sticky note with six numbers on it, “This is a code to a warehouse in the harbor, port 6-A to be specific.”

Markus looks at the numbers 305132; he commits them to memory immediately. Cleo continues.

“Most warehouses were raided early on, but when we finally broke through the firewall of the harbor system, we found this: the last untouched storage unit in the harbor.”

Cleo stares at him, waiting for a response of some sort. Markus takes the moment of silence to recall the layout of the harbor.

The harbor was the main point of evacuation during the panic of the Fall, so obviously the population density there was substantially high while the infection rates were at their peak. Markus knows from experience that it was—and still is—far from safe. Many ships never left dock back then because they were overrun with parasites; today, many of those monsters still remain at the port. In certain areas, it’s certainly one of the most dangerous places in the city. As luck would have it, port 6-A should be fairly clear if his recent reconnaissance is still accurate.

“What’s in the warehouse that's worth the trouble?” Markus finally breaks the silence.

“The files never said, but it must have been pretty important to have such a strong firewall—or at least that’s what the anonymous source that cracked the code said. Anyways, I already sent a group out to check it out, but let’s just say it didn’t go so well.” Cleo glances at the report on her desk with a few red lines. People didn’t make it back.

“So you want me to do the work others can’t? Am I making my assumptions right?” Markus questions, sort of used to the routine at this point.

“Is that ever not the case?”

Markus sighs, “I’m assuming I get the first pick of anything I find.”

“Of course,” Cleo says, a bit more cheerful and less formal.

About a second before anything happens, the room feels static enough that Markus can hear the soft thumping of feet approaching the door. He promptly steps out of the way of an overzealous girl bursting into the room.

“Cleo!” she exclaims, half panting, “I’ve gotta tell you someth—” She cuts herself off as she notices Markus’ presence. “Oh God, am I interrupting something?”

“No, not really,” Markus summarizes before Cleo can display the irritation written all over her face.

Cleo takes a drawn-out breath before prompting the girl to speak, “Jaylin, go ahead.”

Jaylin is a cheeky, gossip-loving girl somewhere between a teenager and a young adult, depending on the day. This both serves as a great networking strategy and the bane of Cleo’s sanity. Markus is actually not at all surprised that the girl burst in here unannounced, as she has somehow weaseled her way into Cleo’s good graces despite the former statement. Does she deserve such a privileged position? Markus couldn’t say, as he doesn’t know much about her, but given the way she talks, it’s clear that she can think on her feet at the very least.

“I just wanted to tell you…” Jaylin begins, suddenly uncharacteristically timid, “Jeffery Williams just quit the scout team.”

Cleo isn’t able to control the disappointment she feels as a hand is the only thing keeping her head up. She lets out an exasperated sigh, “Why did you feel so inclined to interrupt me with this?”

Jaylin glances at Markus multiple times while Cleo talks before completely disregarding what the question was, “So were you two, like, making out before I barged in?”

Markus half hopes Cleo would get flustered by that statement, but that would be asking too much of the humored woman.

“I don’t know,” she smiles deviously, gazing over to Markus, “Were we?”

Markus shakes his head, “No, as you can tell, Cleo is in her business attire, so our interactions have been strictly professional.”

All eyes inspect Cleo’s obviously casual clothing, but no one bothers to question or point out that his sarcasm was fairly dry. Or at least, Markus feels like it was dry, as the room goes eerily quiet afterwards.

Jaylin shakes her head, “Yeesh, sorry I asked… Anyways,” she redirects her sporadic attention to Cleo, “with Jeff gone, there’s a big hole in the scout team that needs immediate filling.”

Cleo crosses her arms and stares at the girl like a teacher listening to a student ask obvious questions, “And who do you think should fill that void?”

Jaylin pauses and contemplates it as she glances around the room more as a filler action than an actual attempt at finding something, “Oh! Darla could fit that role perfectly!”

“Then why don’t you go talk to her about it?” Cleo prompts.

Jaylin's face lights up, supposedly at the thought of being able to finally solve a problem, “Will do! Catch you two lovebirds later.”

After that, the energy-filled woman rushes out the door as quickly as she came in.

“It’s a shame she doesn’t realize how much of a leader she is,” Cleo comments.

The room returns to its usual calm, quiet, and peaceful silence, only for even that to be interrupted by Cleo once again pressing herself up against Markus. She latches onto his arm and is sure to press every square inch of her figure against him as much as she can. Markus can’t help but blush momentarily, but he blinks it away before she notices anything. Cleo is well known across Pearson as a highly desired woman. Some even call her a forbidden fruit, as no one can recall ever seeing her with a man. To be objectively honest, she’s conventionally stunning with long, lush hair, healthy curves in all the right places, and, most of all, an athletic body which allows her to be seen not as a princess, but as a renowned female warrior and leader. In terms of combat prowess, she isn’t anything to write home about, but she definitely can hold her own. All things considered, she’s right up his alley, but he has his own reasons for always turning her down. Reasons that run deeper than simple impulse and desire.

“So are we going to make out or what?” she wonders as she bites her bottom lip seductively.

“I have a job to do, remember?” he responds without so much as moving a muscle despite her slowly creeping up his arm.

She continues to climb his arm until their faces are mere inches from one another. He remains still and stoic while Cleo’s slow advance reaches an end. A moment of one-sided tension passes before Cleo withdraws.

“It doesn’t feel right if only one of us is putting in any effort,” she comments, biting the inside of her cheek and pouting as she sits on her desk.

Markus shrugs as he turns to the door, closing his eyes and leaving with one final statement, “I know you’re smart enough to realize that every man and his son have eyes for you, but you need to find someone that will return your feelings. You’ll be happier that way.”

Her response is immediate and confident with a spice of playful humor, “Too bad I’m only attracted to strong men.”
