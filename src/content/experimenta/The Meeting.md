Markus finds that a generator has already been connected to the building resupplying the power only to the elevator and a light in the hallway telling him that. It's quite sketchy to say the least, but trying to be too discrete with his approach might cause more harm than good. As far as he's concerned, fighting this guy with bullets is a no go. Words are his best bet right now anyway.

The elevator ride is eerily silent and the dim bulbs in the elevator desperately need to be replaced. It flickers once while passing the twentieth floor but after that it keeps the space at a consistently creepy level. Aside from the rumbling of the moving gears, only Markus' breath makes any noise until the doors finally open on the top floor. 

The windows on the top floor are all shattered on one side and non-existent on the other. He can see piles of rubble and debris three floors down while purple clouds are three floors in the other direction. During the Fall, a plane flew too low to the ground and its wing clipped the side of this building. The structure was not badly damaged but half of this floor went with the plane that crashed a few blocks away. It's now a fossil of ancient history. 

The silver lining is that the sun is easily visible given that only the thin air blocks the view to the horizon. 

Markus steps out of the elevator finding the hooded figure standing near the edge of the building looking out to the city. He quickly taps the record button on his phone before putting it back in his pocket.

"I'm here," Markus says, trying not to act like he was trying to conceal his presence or anything, "As requested."

"Twelve and a half seconds," the man says. Markus expects a deep and demonic voice like Volker's but finds this man to be quite youthful. In fact, he immediately gets the feeling that this guy is the same age as him, though the seriousness in his tone makes him beg to differ. His voice is the kind that belongs to someone who has seen death's door an uncountable amount of times yet still remains on the wrong side of it. His voice is not particularly intimidating, but the way he uses it sends a shiver down Markus' spine.

"What?" is all Markus can think to reply to the statement.

"That's how long it takes to get to the ground," he says, not bothering to turn around yet, instead opting to simply stare out into the city.

"Good to know," Markus replies not seeing the relevance in the fact but too on edge to mind it, "Who are you?"

The man thinks about it for a moment letting the wind sail by on their high altitude perch, "I was going to ask you the same question."

Markus tries to get a read on the man, but given that he can only see the back of his hoodie, he can't really figure out much. "I'm just a mercenary in this city. There's nothing more to it."

"So being a former member of Allegiance One doesn't make the cut," the man correctly identifies.

Markus holds his breath as he takes a step back in shock, "So you know more than you let on. How'd you know though?"

"None the matter," the man replies, quickly shutting down any further explanation, "Did you know a man by the name of Drew Wilbur?"

Markus' blood runs cold, "Yes…"

His thoughts race as he remembers the calm and collected plotter in Allegiance One. A man who had no fear and was shameless enough to keep the whole squad honest. He was one of the few men Markus had always looked up to. 

"Before we continue, turn the recording device off," the man says coldly.

Surprised by how perceptive the man is, Markus complies. He can already hear Jenks cursing him out on the other end.

"Okay, it's off, now talk. What did you want to tell me? How did you scare a Revenant off? And how do you know Drew Wilbur?"

The man slowly turns around revealing his face though still partially concealed by his hood. As he suspected, the man is no older than Markus. Despite that, his face isn't too outstanding or shocking except for one small detail. A tiny detail that many forget to mention, but is the single defining feature upon this man's visage: his eyes. The man's eyes rest tiredly upon his face with the most cold, dead expression a human could possibly produce. If eyes are the gateway to a man's soul, the soul before him was far beyond the grave. His face looks so normal yet so inhuman at the same time; a sight that makes parasites look friendly.

"Are you that Markus?" he asks coldly and directly.

Markus is about to respond with, 'first tell me why you attacked the HoV' but the man keeps going. 

"So you want to know why I attacked that place..." he trails off.

With a careful nod, Markus narrows his eyes, "All of those people. Why'd they have to die? Why'd you kill them?"

The man's face doesn't transition, morph, or even falter from its dead expression. That pitiful look is seemingly permanently glued to his face. That being said, he does slowly turn away and look of towards the dying sun.

"Killed them..." he repeats slowly, "Maybe that is what it was."

Markus intentionally paces a few steps towards an overturned desk. If anything suspicious happens, it's better to have cover than be a sitting duck. He doesn't intend to use it, but distance will be a determining factor in the worst case.

"What was your motivation?" Markus prompts, "Are you working with someone? Was there a reason for the attack? You didn't even take anything... except innocent lives that is."

The man doesn't turn around, "Yeah... there was a reason."

"Which was?" Markus pressures, suddenly feeling like the conversation was much less on sided.

With a deep sigh, the man glances back around briefly, "So you're after the Organization. Volker's shown up to you too."

Markus takes a step back, stunned, "What does that have to do—How did you—"

At first he wonders how this man knows so much, but then he recalls their past encounters. In front of some random apartment building, in the aftermath of a fight with a Collosal, and in the Mall. It'd be naive to expect this man to know nothing, but hearing him say it feels strikingly unsettling. It doesn't beg one important question, however...

The man shakes his head, "It's better if you don't ask that question."

"If you know so much about me, why don't you start by telling me about yourself. Clearly there's a fundamental gap in our understanding."

The man doesn't simply let there be silence, he allows there to be silence. Ordinarily, it'd be counterintuitive to describe it that way, but this man truly determines that for himself. He doesn't seem like he's contemplating, nor is he evaluating Markus. The two of them simply stand with the subtle wind brush up against the building.

"Call me… ah nevermind," the man begins as he turns away once more, "A man once told me his name, and it damned my life. My only hope is that it doesn't happen to you."

Markus hesitates as he thinks about what to say next, "I don't know what you mean."

The man turns around fully this time, his cold eyes glaring through Markus' defenses, "Volker only appears to those destined to become an Experiment. Do you understand what that means?"

Hesitantly, Markus shakes his head. Sure he understands the basic principle of Experiments being monstrous constructions made by the Cult. Back when he was working with Allegiance One, they encountered a plethora of cultists whom rambled on and on about the awful things they'd done. It was unsettling to listen to seemingly ordinary people admit to atrocities, but they all had one thing in common: Experiments. Volker was one, there are surely many more, heck, the parasites in the city are probably Experiments.

"So you don't," the man comments knowingly with a sigh.

"And what exactly don't I know?" Markus presses with a keen eye. This conversation may be more interesting than a tense introduction.

The man glances at the missing half of the floor briefly, "All you need to know is this..." he clenches his fists, though his expression doesn't waver, "No matter what you do, you can't kill an Experiment."

The conversation goes eerily silent. Markus reaches for his gun, but doesn't draw it just yet. The man, however, continues to watch silently. His words seemingly providing all the necessary context.

Eventually, the man turns back around and takes a step towards the edge of the building; the front of his feet mere inches from the steel precipice. 

"I still don't understand," the man says rather aloof, "Why are you being targeted?"

"What do you mean by targeted?" Markus asks.

At this point, he should probably be confused beyond compare, but unfortunately, he's gaining a sinking suspicion about what could be going on. It may have been almost a decade since he left, but his fate has long been intertwined with the Cult. If he's being targeted by someone, or something, it might actually work in his favor. After all, it was only a matter of time anyway.

"So you were raised by the Organization," the man states thoughtfully, "But, what did they do to you that makes you an Experiment candidate?"

"Seriously, how in the world do—"

"Be careful with your words," the man finishes.

Markus goes silent for a moment taking a deep breath of the thin air. 'Be careful with your words' he says. Naturally that's exactly what he's doing. What Markus can't figure out is why this man seems to know so much and so little as the same time. Every time he asks a question, he seems to answer it before Markus can even respond properly but proceeds to ask them anyway. Is he testing him? Is this some sort of game to him?

"You remind me of someone I used to know," the man eventually says, "He was a very brave and fragile soul. A man who could face the world without a second thought, but when push came to shove, he couldn't face himself. Are you that way too?"

The man's description reminds Markus of Grant—not necessarily that last part, but everything before it. Of course, Markus doesn't bother to offer up that information for free, but it still provokes a thought. Regardless, these probing questions have to mean something and this 'Experiment candidate' claim has to have some serious implications. Whatever it is, he can't leave this conversation until he understands and identifies the threats at play.

"I don't really know what you want me to say," Markus waves his hands to his sides with a casual shrug, "I do what I have to. The same as everyone else. Now, why don't you start answering some of my questions?"

The man maintains his gaze out to the city below and beyond, "You came here to reason. To get me to stop. Is that right?"

Out of habit, Markus leans up against the table near him and crosses his arms, "Ideally, yes. Though did you only seek me out so that you could ask me a few questions?"

With a brief glance back, the man crouches down and perches himself on the edge of the skyscraper, "You've already told me more than you know."

Alright, this is the point where Markus' experience and practical thinking is reaching its end. From his point of view, this man's threat level has yet to be fully established, his motives are all over the place, and, as far as Markus knows, he's working alone. All of these factors lead Markus to a single conclusion: this man is neither friend nor foe. That sounds arbitrary, but he can actually use that.

"These Experiments," Markus begins with a genuine curiosity, "How do I identify one and what does it mean to become one?"

The man looks back, his former indifferent looks briefly shifts into a subtle surprise. The feeling fades as quickly as it started, but for the minute second, Markus' question actually seemed unexpected.

The hooded figure rises to his feet, "Doctor Crane, where is he?"

"Who—" Markus starts but quickly catches himself, "That cultist is secured in the same base you attacked last night."

The man releases a sigh of defeat, "I checked the entire street and couldn't find him. Regardless, he's escaped by now."

"I trust the person I handed him off to," Markus defends.

"And I trusted he was dead the first time I killed him," the man responds suddenly before recoiling at his own words. He turns away in a forced fashion, "If you ever see that monster again... just run."

His words float in the air for a bit as a short pause is allowed to occur. The man reaches into his pocket and pulls out a flare gun, promptly tossing it to Markus, "Fire that gun off if you ever come in contact with a cultist or Experiment."

Markus inspects the gun in hand, both for any potential dangers to himself, but also just to ensure it isn't unique in some way. After a quick glance into the barrel, around the trigger mechanism, and around the handle, he concludes that it must be safe enough to use—at least for now.

"Why would I do that?" Markus demands, "What have you done that deserves my compliance?"

The man opens his mouth to speak, but this time, Markus cuts him off, "You're threats won't work."

The man sighs, "That's not..." he lowers his head and actually grits his teeth in frustration, "You don't have to... but if you don't, I won't be able to help you when you lose everything—not even death will save you from that."

How in the world is he supposed to take that?

"Now go," the man comments as he takes a seat on the edge of the skyscraper and lets his feet hang off the edge.

Markus nods as he creeps towards the elevator with the flare in hand. With that, he takes his leave wondering whether he learned something or simply found himself more confused than before.

"What was that about?" Jenks yells at him as he returns from the elevator of which he came. "Why did you shut off your transmitter?!"

Markus stares Jenks down, finding that he's suddenly started imitating the man, "You didn't see his face."

"What?" Jenks argues, throwing his hands up, "Was he hideous? What does his looks have to do with anything?"

"No," Markus says quickly before slowing down to let his next words sink in, "His eyes looked dead. His eyes hid more than even Grant's."

Jenks settles down, "You serious?"

Markus nods.

"Dang, what did that guy go through? Wait! Why do I care? What happened up there?"

Markus lets out a long, deep, stress relieving sigh, "Honestly... I'm not sure myself. We talked about how something is hunting me down and how he wants me to use this flare gun if any cultists appear."

"Why would you do that?" Jenks wonders.

It's a valid question, but Markus can only shrug, "I'm not sure if I should believe him or call him crazy. Either way, I'm at a loss for what we can do about him."

"We'll that's not ideal," Jenks taps his shoulder with his index finger, "On a different note, I've been monitoring some of the larger factions for various reasons and I've noticed the Eagle faction mobilizing."

"Why?" Markus asks with an annoyed concern.

Jenks shakes his head, "I'm just as annoyed with them as you, but I'm not entirely sure. I saw a few major faction leaders are already jumping on the situation but it's hard to tell how it's going down."

Markus reaches into his pocket and turns his phone off Do Not Disturb. Immediately he is bombarded with numerous texts from Reign. The leading message between all of them is to meet up now.

"Looks like I'm being dragged into this," Markus comments exasperated before giving his friend a serious look, "Do you think this has to do with the cultist we captured?"

Jenks steps away and takes a seat on the lobby counter. He then takes a peak at his watch before he continues, "Most definitely."

"I guess that cultist did escape," Markus musses as he leans against the closed elevator door.

"How'd you know that?" Jenks asks, cocking his head to the side, "I just caught that on surveillance, how'd you know that?"

Markus narrows his eyes, a bit disturbed that the information was verified so quickly, "That man told me. Somehow he knew."

Jenks purses his lips while running a hand over his face in thought. He hops off the counter and starts pacing the floor for a moment. A few blueprints or elaborate plans manifest in a brief waving of his fingers, but that quickly comes to an end as he turns back to Markus, "I have a really bad feeling about this."

"What do you mean?"

Jenks picks up speed in his pacing, "A cultist on the loose, a city on the verge of famine—yes, I know about the fungus—Darek and I were talking about it earlier. Lastly, this monster of man is running around targeting entire factions at a time. Hell, this is more pressure than we had in the Near East. At least then we had Grant to tell us what to do."

Markus looks down at the flare gun in his hand, "That's not very optimistic."

With a roll of his eyes, Jenks transitions to the front spinning door, "It's less than ideal, but there is a silver lining here."

"Which is?"

To be honest, Markus could probably figure out the implications himself, but after his little chat, he's mentally taxed.

Jenks points a finger at his friend, making a statement with the motion, "When things start to happen. Opportunities emerge."

"And where there are opportunities," Markus begins.

"Cultists can be found," Jenks finishes.

With a sigh, Markus finds himself thankful to have such a trustworthy friend watching his back. He had gotten used to working alone—honestly it became preferred at some point—but seeing Jenks here, thinking about the mission, takes a weight off his shoulders... He only realizes that now. This is the relationship he was missing in life. Cleo, Reign, Misty, they're all great people, but none of them could compare the comradery he had with Allegiance One. Jenks is the last manifestation he has left...

"Wait, there was one more thing," Markus comments before Jenks can start slipping out the door, "That guy up there... he knew about Wilbur. Asked me about him."

Jenks' eyes widen, "What did he say?"

Markus shakes his head, "He refused to say anything, but if he knows... could Wilbur... could he be alive too—like us?"

Jenks leans back in disbelief, "I'd say I'm surprised, but I genuinely believed I was the only one left. Finding you was miraculous enough. To think Wilbur might still be out there."

"I know. I just hope that we'll get to see him soon."

"Right," Jenks agrees, "But it sounds like there's no actionable information on that front."

Markus frowns, "Unfortunately no."

"Then we'll just have to focus on what we can control."

"Right."

Jenks steps up to the spinning door and rests his hand on it carefully. Then, with a quick glance back, he offers up his signature smile, "I'll go do my thing, you do yours. We can touch base the next time something comes up."

"Wait," Markus calls out before Jenks can leave for real, "How can I contact you?"

With a little laugh, Jenks shows off his watch, "I've confiscated that same number we used to use. Just message that one and I'll know it's you."

After that, Jenks departs from the situation. Markus immediately pulls out his own phone and begins to browse through the not-so-anonymous messages he received in the past hour. Each message repeated the same time and place: two hours from now at the Radio Tower.
