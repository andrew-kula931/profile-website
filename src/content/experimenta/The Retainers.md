After power went out throughout the city, General Reign was swift to rebuild the network system and his faction—the Retainers. It was his belief that communication was key in survival and an organized faction was needed to protect it. When the cell tower eventually did go back online, most people didn't care much as the overarching internet was still out of commission and those who did, didn't have access to the secure network anyway. Only certain individuals were allowed access to the Reign's network which, in turn, gave Reign complete control over all cell phone communication throughout the city. Markus specifically doesn't use his phone much but as a precaution has set up a one way receiving system—complements of his former friend, that way Reign can't track him but he can still message him. Because of this, from time to time Markus will get a message from Reign, summoning him.

General Reign's headquarters stands as an intimidating fort constructed around an old radio tower—the place where some of the most familiar voices in the nation used to work. Fifteen foot walls stand around the perimeter protecting from very infrequent parasite attacks. The Radio Tower had never been conquered since it was founded not long after the Fall.

Markus approaches the main gate and waits for it to open. He gets the usual line of questions when one of the guards spot him, but once he says his name, they recognize there he's actually supposed to be here. Reign's communication network never ceases to impress. 

Inside the fort is a large courtyard used for assemblies and leisurely chatting. A shooting range sits to one side and the barrack to the other. Straight ahead is the radio tower itself with every other notable room inside. To an outside viewer the Radio Tower is a militaristic stronghold, but at a closer look, children run around playing while on break from school, women gossip at the cafeteria, the fort is simply a highly protected community. The leader of the community, however, is the reason why it seems so militaristic.

Markus assumes that general Reign is in his office on the top floor of the Radio Tower, thus that is the first place he plans on checking as he climbs an annoying large amount of stairs. Markus knocks on the door and waits for yet another meeting with an important person. It seems like an eternity ago he was working out in the high school weight room. Though before that... no use in dwelling on it.

The door promptly opens and his audience with the most powerful man in the city begins.

"It's good to see you Markus, how have you been?" Reign greets. 

Mackenzie Reign is a well built forty-seven year old man with just enough facial hair to make him look wise without seeming old. He sits behind his desk with folded hands as if greeting the leader of another faction. Paperwork liters his desk with a cold cup of coffee sitting at the corner. He pulls the scattered documents into an orderly pile as Markus begins.

"About the same as I always am. I'm currently under contract with Cleo so I can't run a job for you until I report back to her."

Describing it as a contract is a bit of an overstatement as he's never handed out his signature, but in the current economic climate, one's word is tantamount to a signature anyway. This is something that all of the faction leaders agree on—at least the one's Markus is willing to work with.

"Oh don't worry, I won't try to breach your contract, I have more respect for you than that. I called you here for another reason."

Reign places the pile of documents to the side of the desk before adjust a picture frame with a vivid image of his daughter on it. He offers Markus a subtle smirk as he glances between him and the photograph.

Markus sighs while shaking his head, "I'm not marrying your daughter."

Reign laughs and shakes his head a bit, "You don't have to remind me every time I try to hook you two up. You're both at that age though, and it would really give a strong backing to our community, but I digress. I understand that such things are not high on your priority list."

"I don't find it funny that you're the third person today to assume I'm a bachelor, but I appreciate the understanding," Markus intervenes.

"Very well. I'll cut to the chase," Reign reaches over and grabs a sealed plastic container. He promptly pulls the lid off to reveal what looks to be a stalk of corn. The problem with the object is not that it was sitting there but that it has a dark brown fungus growing all over the outside of the cob. "Our fields are being infected with this fungus, obviously linked to the parasite. It's not just us either, this is happening to everyone in the city."

Markus narrows his eyes. He recognizes this fungus—it's the same one he saw earlier today.

Dismissing any further thoughts, he strokes his chin, "That will cause some real problems won't it? I take it famine is on the rise."

"Not yet, but soon. I've managed to keep it quiet for now, but panic will ensue if we don't have a plan going forward. My people will be fine with the stockpile we have, but that very stockpile will become a target for starving people. Parasites have always been our biggest concern thus far, but hungry people can flip that on its head." He pauses for a moment coughing quickly into his elbow, "I can't imagine its good for our health either. It doesn't seem to turn people so its a flora specific strain. Even so, I'm not here to ask for help with our crops. I want you to be my daughter's guard."

"I don't do that kind of work," Markus counters swiftly.

"Hear me out, my daughter is my most trusted diplomat, better at talking than even me, but that skill will really be put to the test when we have to keep wars from breaking out. My security detail is running thin during these trying times so I don't want to leave Elizabeth unprotected. I just want to make sure my only daughter is safe. Is that too much to ask?"

Markus shakes his head slowly. Half of him understands the man's concerns while the other half understands Elizabeth's capabilities. He's not particularly close with Elizabeth—any more than with Cleo that is—but just like Cleo, he's also very aware of how capable she is. It's not really his place to act as a chaperon for a someone like that. To him it sort of feels demeaning in a way.

Reign stops and remains focused on Markus, obviously waiting for a response: one that he believed was already given. Markus, because of his respect for Reign, considers his options. He bites the inside of his cheek lightly and looks out the windows behind Reign's desk. It's a pretty decent view of the Radio Tower and the streets beyond. The clear street running between the towering buildings feels like a river winding through mountains of concrete. He almost gets lost in thought when he realizes Reign is still staring at him.

"Being a guard takes time and that's something I never have enough of. Besides, what would I get out of it?"

Nodding his head and presses his palms against his desk as he stands up. He then turns towards the window and strokes his scruff of a beard, "I was planning on paying you proportionally to the work. A flat rate of ammunition with bonuses if you have to dirty your hands at all."

Markus runs a mental scan of his armory to calculate the current stock of ammunition. After one or two seconds of summing up the boxes, he arrives at a number he's content with, "I don't know if that's worth it," Reign tries to protest, but Markus keeps going. "However, if your daughter is having a very important meeting, I might just consider making an appearance. Besides, so long as I remain in this city, the meeting inadvertenly affects me."

Reign half sighs, half chuckles before taking a seat back in his chair, "Very well, I'll accept that answer. I'll let you know when that time comes… Also, may I ask you a bit of a personal question?"

Markus shrugs and nods his head silently.

"What are your plans moving forward? From the way you talk, you plan on leaving the city soon… aren't you?"

It's not really a question he has a solid answer to as his priorities are heavily dependent on a few loose ends. That being said, with his personality and reputation, agreeing with Reign's conjecture should be pretty natural, "That's a good question. I guess the only answer I can give right now is," he pauses to think a moment more, then he formulates a proper response that doesn't lead to too many questions, "destroying the remnants of my past. As for leaving the city... it's only a matter of time, I guess." 

"I understand, you do know that I'm always willing to help?" Reign offers, "It's the least I could do after all you've done more me—for all of us."

"All I ask is that you don't get involved in my affairs," Markus says sincerely, "It's better for everyone that way."

Reign studies him carefully, "You're not that old Markus. Remember that you don't have to do everything yourself."

Markus shakes his head. From his perspective, he's plenty old—and that's not youthful ignorance talking. He's been knee deep in dangerous circumstances for over a decade. Being a twenty-two year old guy doesn't change the fact that, in his mind, age is determined by experience, not time.

"I'll be heading home then, the sun is going down and we both know what happens after dark," Markus breaks the silence as he turns to leave.

"Of course," Reign calls after, "stay safe my friend."

Markus sets his assault rifle, pistol, and sword back in his armory before going and taking a seat in his own office. It is a small living space just across the hall from his bedroom. A map of the city hangs on the wall with pins and pictures all over it, not unlike the one in Cleo's office.

He stopped by the HoV to complete his end of the deal and made sure not to get caught up in a drawn out conversation. Luckily enough, he got out quick and arrived home without issue. Now it's finally time to settle down and start plotting for his next move.

The day before was spelunking in the Pearson sewers trying to find a hideout of a cultist which unfortunately didn't bring anything worthwhile. That excursion was also the reason he woke up feeling so soar.

"My leads are running dry," he comments to himself aloud.

On the board are numerous faces scattered around the city, most were crossed off, but two faces remain, two cultists he has yet to find. One is hand-drawn sketch of a chemist who supposedly has very impressive ties to the Cult. The other picture is of a man with a long messy beard and worn eyes—the complexion that an sleep deprived white collar worker wears during a seven day work week. In the image, his clothes are ragged and beaten hanging onto his body by a thread. The Hobo facade is, of course, just a cover for an executioner of the Cult. This particular man seemingly has ties to the Mayor of Pearson as well which is why the Mayor's bunker is so interesting to him.

The sticky note with "Bunker - Harvey's House" was his current best lead. There has to be some kind of connection between this Harvey and either a location in the city or a connection to the mayor himself. As far as Markus knew there wasn't any person in the city named Harvey, which is usually a question for Misty. That being said, Markus swore that he would never get Misty tangled up into this. 

He racks his brain over and over trying to come up with something for Harvey, before a realization finally dawns on him. 

The note was written during the Fall—three years ago—so whoever wrote it, probably Colonel Prown, would have had knowledge of people who probably aren't alive anymore. He had to put it into context. When the military finally pulled out of that dock only a few got out, for the most part, everyone stationed there died when it got overrun. There was no shot that any expedition was sent out to find the mayor's bunker, so he probably wrote the coordinates in code to prevent later thieves from finding it. That code must be decipherable by only particular people. Colonel Prown was a recognizable name, but Markus had never met him personally or spoken to many people who knew him. It was a stretch to say he really knew anything about Colonel Prown. There then had to be some military secret that would help him decipher this text.

_"So Markus how'd you end up here of all places, you're only what? Thirteen, fourteen?"_

_Lieutenant Carter was a serious when necessary, jokes all else, kind of man. He was a jack of all trades, anything that needed to be done he could fill in the position. Now he found himself sitting on a cargo box with three other soldiers all standing around in a circle. Each of them sip out of coffee cups as the sun slowly rises. The other soliders turned their attention away from one another and glanced over at Markus with the presentation of Carter's statement._

_"What's it to you?" Markus replied trying to be a vague as possible._

_"Oh come on, we've been out here in the desert for almost two weeks now, you can cut the tough guy routine," Carter smiled as he wiped some sand off his camouflage pants._

_"I heard he was a super soldier for the enemy and turned on them to join us," Sergeant Nox conspicuously whispered. _

_Nox was a big guy who did all the heavy lifting for the squad, anything with muscle involved, he was on it. Also, despite looking and acting like a brute, he was actually incredibly smart. He could single handedly take apart and put back together anything he could get his hands on. All he needs is the right tools and it would be done in record time. He proved himself the day before by disarming a bomb strapped to a vehicle while in transit. The impressive part was that the bomb was inside of the car's engine. He had to disassemble multiple parts of the car while it was both active and moving. He did all of that in ten minutes._

_"You're not too far off," Markus started, a little disgruntled, "They abducted me when I was two and I had fought for them as long as I can remember. It wasn't by choice."_

_"So when'd you break out?" Carter asked, trying to hide the shock that the rest of the group had no intention of keeping discrete._

_"About a year ago," Markus explained, playing with a small pile of sand, "I joined the military shortly after. It wasn't really my choice, but I'm not opposed to getting back at those bastards."_

_"So Grant roped you in too, huh," the man to Carter's right said: Sergeant Drew Wilbur was his name. Wilber was the sharpshooter of the group, his sniper could take anyone down regardless of the distance. It should be noted that everyone in the squad could put a world class sharpshooter to shame, but Wilbur did it to another level. Not only that, but Wilbur was also the strategist of the squad—allegedly from his background as former assassin. He wasn't the leader, but not even the military could figure out how he could plot out the enemy's next move days before it happened. They sometimes liked to joke that the higher ups would poach the guy to use as their personal strategy tool, but Grant always shut the idea down by saying it wouldn't happen._

_"That's a shame," the last guy in the group said. His name was Private Jenkenson, or Jenks for short. He was the youngest and newest member of the squad, after Markus of course. He knew his way around computers better than anyone, he also was no force to be reckoned with when he had a gun in his hands. His claim to fame wasn't some crazy hacking fiasco, however, instead he caught Grant's attention from the outside when he "anonymously" sent information in about the Cult. Sure it was supposed to be anonymous, but Grant had his ways._

_"It might sound shallow, but I just want some revenge," Markus added._

_"Well none the matter, we're glad to have you on our team," Carter explained, "taking out that squad single-handedly during the last mission really earned you your spot among us."_

_The group of men were all part of a special assault squad for the government to take out foreign enemies during wartime. It was actually a new concept created by their squad leader to do things that the military couldn't. Their tactics were fluid yet never disappointed, their skills were the finest in the world, and most of all, they had comradery that pushed them through even the toughest of missions. This legendary squad was known by few as Allegiance One, a name that struck fear into each and every one of those unfortunate souls. Their conversation was cut short when another man walked up from around a tent. Everyone snapped to attention with their right hand saluting. _

_"At ease, we got orders from the higher ups. They want us to raid an outpost three clicks west from here. It has an operative aligned with the Cult. We get him and we get out." _

_This order was given from the leader of the squad Captain Grant, he was the best of the best of them all. He could stay cool under any amount of pressure and lead the squad while bullets blazed by. This, paired with the fact that each of them wished they could best him in anything, and one would understand why they all follow him so loyally. Their team synergized well together all because of him, and it is only thanks to him that this team has managed to stay together and avoid casualties all together._

_"So what kind of tactic are you thinking? Scorch and burn, surprise attack, infiltration if we're feeling it. I've got a few plans already cooked up," Wilbur asserted._

_Grant quickly glanced over all of them, "Stealth as much as we can—remember that this is deep in the Cult's territory. This operation won't be easy, but I'm not worried."_

_"You're never worried," Carter pointed out, "Heck, I don't think there's a thing on this planet that could worry you."_

_Grant didn't so much as shrug as he brushed the comment out completely. It's an action so fitting for him that the rest of them chuckle at the remark._

_"So when do we leave," Carter continued, setting his coffee cup down on the box he was sitting on._

_"Tomorrow before dawn. We'll have to track the cultist down, but that won't be hard as we already have the first crumb to his bread trail."_

Markus might not have an exact answer to who Harvey is, but he's confident that he'll find that crumb and follow the bread trail. Now he at least has a name, a name that he can follow.
