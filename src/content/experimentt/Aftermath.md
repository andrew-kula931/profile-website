There were so many bodies cluttering the prison that they knew they were never going to be able to bury them all. Time or man power wasn't the problem, it was the raging storm and the looming threat that additional zombies could be lurking inside the building. After all, twenty-ish people is far from the total capacity of the prison, which means over half of the survivors were still somewhere inside the complex. That risk was far more than they could handle at present. As such, they made the difficult decision to pack up and rendezvous a little ways down the road at a motel.

Blake was worried that the location would be inaccessible due to powered off locks, but Max was adamant about it having key locks. When Max turned out to be correct, he shocked them all by once again proving his lock picking skill and getting them into two rooms.

Now, each member of their group sits in a rough circle with blanket over them and a large pile of food and supplies in the center of the room. Ryan sits on one of the two beds leaning over with his elbows on his knees. Chloe wraps her blanket around her back as she leans against him, her eyes staring blankly ahead. Blake occupies the chair next to a small table fiddling with the parts of a pistol he took apart and now attempts to put back together. Caid leans against the wall next to Max looking over the pile as Beth sits organizing everything they’ve found. Alison hugs her legs with a blanket over her head as she sits on the other bed refusing to talk to anyone.

“Is this everything?” Caid asks as Beth takes a break from the organizing.

“Yeah,” she replies, "At least, it's everything that we could manage to grab on our first go. It'll last us... for now, but 'for now' isn't very long."

“Who even needs to survive the winter?” Max asks rhetorically as he stares out the window at the snow whipping by. All of his easy going humor has long since vanished, replaced with a conspicuous despair.

"Come on," Blake counters quickly, "If we give up now, it would mean everything we’ve done leading up to this was pointless. I refuse to accept that," he glances out the window, “It’s only November, I don’t think that snow will last long. Once it melts, we should leave town.”

Caid nods his head but says nothing along with Beth. Alison remains under her blanket and Chloe doesn’t even budge as she stares at the powered off TV in front of her as if the blank screen were an intriguing film.

"If we make it out of town," Alison comments morbidly without so much as lifting up her blanket.

"Alison!" Beth counters, "Don't talk like that."

From beneath the blanket she shakes her head, "Max said it too."

Ryan runs his fingers over the gauze on his arm. He'd be lying if he said none of this affected him, yet it'd also be a lie to say he felt destroyed right now. For some reason he's stuck in some sort of paradox where he simultaneously feels hurt and indifferent at the same time. It seems crazy to describe it that way, but that's simply the best explanation he can give himself. If nothing else, death, carnage, and destruction have simply become... normalized. Watching other people die doesn't hurt like it used to. Is that bad?

The sun outside slowly drifts down, turning the whiteout landscape into a dark abyss. Beth finishes sorting everything while Blake finishes putting his gun back together. Ryan instinctively seeks a quite room in the rest in but finds Max and Chloe following him there. Given that they only have two room, he supposes it's bound to happen that they'd need to share.

Before long, Ryan finds his back against a bed with Chloe asleep next to him. While she gives him plenty of space to lie, her hand rests against his forearm. Something like this would ordinarily make him uncomfortable or nervous, but right now, he doesn't mind in the slightest.

Ryan reaches onto his chest to feel his knife still there in its sheath. He also feels around to ensure his pistol still remains at his hip, which it does. Sleeping with weapons is something he had intentionally avoided in the past—that being while they were in the prison—but now it feels like a necessity. He doesn't think he'd be able to sleep without it. The door is locked, and the snow is still falling outside, but that doesn't change the fact that they don't live in a peaceful world anymore.

He wonders for a moment, what life would have been like if this whole apocalypse hadn't happened. He would probably be in his own run-down apartment with a low paying job—the epidemy of what he disliked about Steve. To think he was willing to drop out of school at the start of all this. He remembers how he told Bill that he didn’t want to help his foster family because ‘they weren’t his problem’. He regrets it for a moment but then lets it go. There's no point in feeling bad about it when there's no one left alive that remembers Bill... except maybe him. Are they really living in a world where you can count the number of people that remember you? Not just know you, remember you. Maybe he should feel bad about it after all.

Max suddenly rolls over onto his side in the pitch black darkness, “Hey Ryan, you awake?”

“Yeah,” he responds slowly, finding his stream of consciousness hard to break at first.

"Do you remember how I dragged you to my house that one time?" Max voice is so innocent and nostalgic as if the memory were a fond one.

"Yeah," Ryan repeats.

“And remember how we all had that moment after that?”

"Yeah," Ryan reiterates again.

Max folds his hands over his chest as he fiddles with something on his wrist, "It's crazy to think Caid got over it so quickly. You know, loosing his only family."

Ryan looks over at him through the dark room where only his vague silhouette can be seen.

"It took me a lot longer than him," Max comments quietly.

Ryan studies the boy's silhouette but finds nothing to be gained from the outline of black. Max simply continues to play with band on his wrist—Ryan remembers it being blue.

Max clears his throat, "Sorry, I guess you never asked. Trauma dumping, am I right?"

"It's fine," Ryan responds quickly before the momentum of the conversation can fade, "I get it."

Max releases and audible sigh, "I knew you would. Caid might be my best friend but... you're really easy to talk to. You know that?"

Ryan looks back up at the ceiling, "Sure."

The room goes silent for a moment at Max's discretion. Right when it seems that Max has fallen asleep he graces the room with his voice. His voice remains quiet, like a mouse trying not to be heard, "You lied to me back there, didn't you."

Ryan scrunches his face, though no one is able to see it, "What do you mean?"

"You told me that the woman in the freezer had a painless death," Max starts, "You know, the one who was wearing this blue wristband. Her death wasn't painless, was it?"

"Oh," Ryan trails off, "Sorry."

Max shakes his head which makes a shuffling noise on his pillow, "No. No. I'm glad you did. It... it gave me comfort, at least for a while. Heck, I probably needed it back then."

"But not now?"

"Nah," Max comments, "Hearing Mr. Heimes talk today made me realize that... that maybe death is a form of mercy."

Ryan's head snaps over to the boy who's still masked behind darkness.

"Calm down," Max elaborates as the outline of his hands wave in the air, "I... wanna keep living. But at the same time, I'm sort of glad for my family. You know."

Ryan doesn't know how to respond.

"It's just..." Max continues, "Knowing that they're all dead. It means they won't have to suffer anymore. Is that such a bad thing to think? It kinda makes sense in my head, I guess. It's what Heimes was saying."

Relaxing his body, Ryan mulls the comment over with a bit more thought, "Yeah, maybe that's not the worst thing to think."

"Because it's over, right?" Max snickers to himself, "Imagine if we said that to their faces. I know my mom would be livid if I said that aloud... but since they're already gone. It feels like the only logical thing to think."

"Maybe."

"Hey Ryan," Max continues.

"Hm?"

"Thanks for everything... I just... wanted to say that."

Ryan mentally stammers as multiple incoherent thoughts clash against each other simultaneously. Should be grateful? Should he brush it off? They aren't that close, are they? 

All of these thoughts swirl which leads to a long pause where, frankly, Ryan never even gives a proper response. 

“Okay,” Max voices a bit emotionless for him, but somehow better than before, “Night.”

“Night,” Ryan says as an auto response.

Max rotates onto his shoulder and drifts off to silence—hopefully asleep. The room then lays dormant for a while as each of them try to get some rest.

About two hours pass by. Ryan's sleep quality is... lacking to say the least. He wishes he could blame it on the pain in his arm, but that is really only a mild annoyance at best. What actually keeps his staring up at the ceiling is the dream demon looming over his every thought. 

Every time he closes his eyes, he sees Steve. If not Steve, then Jeremy. And the list goes on. In between nightmares he manages a few minutes of dedicated rest, but that feels meaningless when he continuously wakes up in a cold sweat. Every subsequent attempt at sleep just results in the same ending.

With two hours drained away, Ryan decides to get some fresh air seeing as the snow has stopped. He carefully slides out of the covers without disturbing Chloe before halfway limping over to the door. His legs aren't injured, per se, but he can feel just how sore and fatigued the last few days has made him and inadequate sleep is only exacerbating the problem. 

He slowly unlocks the door and opens it, being sure to make as little noise as possible. Once the door finally swings open, he hit with a gust of bone-chilling air but he proceeds outside anyway. 

The motel parking lot and proceeding road is fortunately mostly barren of snow, but instead large drifts as tall as a foot high sit against every curb and bush. With hardly any light from the moon, he has to squint to see much of anything, but at the very least he can make out the general outline of things around him. This perception is quickly tested, however, when something besides him shifts slightly.

"Couldn't sleep?" Blake's voice says in the dark, "Me neither."

Ryan slides his hands into his pockets and takes a deep breath of the icy air before resting his back against the wall beside his companion.

"I don't know about you," Blake begins quietly, "but I quite like the winter. The snow feels so... tranquil and nostalgic, if that makes sense."

Ryan glances over but finds the action meaningless as Blake's silhouette is hardly even noticeable underneath the overhang of the motel, "Yeah, I agree."

Blake releases a drawn out yawn.

"Sound like you should head back in," Ryan comments trying to keep things light while also favoring the idea of getting to be alone for a moment.

"Nah," Blake says, crossing his arms, "I'm good for now."

"Sure."

They both go back to looking at the dark scenery. Each white snow drift acts as a beacon amidst the landscape as everything else is almost completely hidden behind the darkness. Ryan takes notice of a particular sign hanging off the side of the motel. Although barely visible, he can vaguely make out ice sickles dripping off the bottom of the sign like jagged teeth. It's a seemingly meaningless detail, but he finds himself pondering it regardless.

"Hey Ryan, did you have a lot of friends back in high school?" Blake prompts out of nowhere.

Ryan isn't sure whether he should answer the question truthfully or deflect it outright as both options have steep drawbacks. On one hand, he doesn't want to admit the truth and sound like he's about to give a pity party, but he also wants to avoid misleading his friend. This is probably not a situation where dodging the question would actually benefit either of them.

"No, not really," he finally decides.

"Hm," Blake musses quickly, "Figures."

Ryan glances over. It feels like he should be insulted by that comment. Shouldn't Blake apologize for such a remark? Either way, he's probably just reading into it too much. He makes the conscious decision to drop it.

"Believe it or not, I was on student council back then," Blake continues, "Not the president, just on the council."

There's a brief lull in the conversation, so Ryan takes it upon himself to fill the void, "That sort of thing suits you."

Ryan notices Blake's head turn towards him, but he can't tell what face he's making. Maybe he's content with the response, or maybe he's annoyed by the obviously shallow comment. Either way, Ryan continues to stare forward.

"You really think so?" Blake asks rhetorically, "I've been thinking the opposite recently."

"Why's that?" Ryan wonders honestly.

Blake takes a deep breath, "I remember during one of the council meeting, Jessica, the president, brought pizza because a lot of people weren't showing up. After that, almost everyone started showing up to the meetings."

Ryan continues to listen quietly as Blake goes off on his seemingly unrelated rant.

"Tyler, who was a just a junior, came up with this awesome idea for a fund-raiser. He partnered up with a local cinema to get a hot new movie right after release and hosted the movie in the school gym. The idea really sold and made the council enough money to fund any project for the next four years," he releases another yawn but continues, "Another thing, a couple of years ago, the pep rally no longer became a mandatory event for the student body and, as such, the attendance rate plummeted. Libby noticed this and hosted an event with the freshman cohort. I can't remember the specifics because I wasn't a freshman, but the attendance of the next pep rally was boosted by no small margin."

"Sound like you guys had quite the team," Ryan comments, with smile.

"Yeah," Blake says softly, "Those guys were actually incredible—and funny too. Tyler was the life of the party during the meetings, and he was even funnier outside of them. Me, him, and a few other guys used to grab a meal after Friday tennis practice."

"You played tennis?"

"Yeah," Blake comments a bit more enthusiastically, "I was pretty average, but Tyler made state. That guy had it all. Hot girlfriend too... though I was chill with her, so I wasn't jealous mind you."

"Of course."

Blake laughs to himself quietly, "You know, high school wasn't actually that bad."

"Perhaps it wasn't."

"But..." Blake trails off, "You know what, never mind."

Ryan can't explain why he thinks it, but he gets an strong impulse to say something so he indulges it, just this once, "You miss it don't you?"

Blake doesn't say anything right away. When he does his voice is soft and low, "Yeah. I do."

Ryan stands up a bit straighter as he finds himself slouching a bit too much which has since become uncomfortable.

Blake lets out a long sigh and speaks at a barely audible volume, "Sometimes I wish I was like them."

"Hm?" Ryan wonders not hearing the statement completely clearly.

"Oh nothing," Blake recovers quickly, "I think I'm gonna head back to bed. Night."

"Night," Ryan auto-responds once again.

Ryan hears the door beside him open and then close as the presence that was once there transitions to empty space. He leans against the wall for a few more minutes before deciding that the air is a bit too cold for his taste. As such, he follows Blake's example and heads back inside, being sure to lock the door behind him.

He lies back in bed, once again trying to be silent as to avoid waking anyone up. When he eventually closes his eyes, he assumes his efforts have paid off, but then Chloe shifts in her spot. She grabs onto his arm and slides a bit closer to him. Ryan feels her do it without protest or comment.

"You're awake too," Chloe whispers.

Ryan can't help but smirk at the repetition he's been noticing, "Yeah, just stepped out for a moment."

"I hope you weren't smoking," Chloe says dryly, probably as a poor attempt of a joke. Just to humor her, he exhales as if holding back a laugh.

"No. I wasn't."

Chloe slides in a bit closer, her breath now brushing against his neck. Her body presses up against his arm which makes him stiffen up. His reaction makes her chuckle quietly before sliding even closer. Why she is acting so forward, he can't say, but he knows being awkward right now will only make it harder to sleep later. Instead of trying to overthink things, he just relaxes his body and looks up at the ceiling.

"We're... going to stay together, right?" Chloe whispers, "All of us."

Ryan nods hoping the motion can be detected despite it being near pitch black, "Yeah. We will."

She exhales as her chin rests against his shoulder, "So this is probably how it felt. When you watched Jeremy die."

Ryan's body stiffens up again at the sudden memories being injected back into his mind. The boy's deceased face, the boy's mentality on life, his desire to see his mother again. The memories make his heart ache. He would clench his chest, but one arm is being held down while the other is too injured to move around comfortably.

"Sorry," she says after Ryan doesn't make a response, "I probably shouldn't have said that."

"It's fine," Ryan recovers quickly, "...but yeah. It's never easy."

"Hey Ryan," Chloe says right into his ear, "Thank you. For staying."

Ryan turns his head in mild confusion which Chloe seems to sense. What happens next makes everything before feel trivial.

Right as his head turns just enough, he feels something very soft and smooth make contact with his lips. Ordinarily he'd... did that... what just... was it? His head spins so fast that the sensation disappears before he can adequately comprehend what happened. Then, to further the point, Chloe slides away from him and rolls over to her other shoulder. Ryan is left completely flabbergasted as he stares at her back questioning just about everything.

Intellectually he knows what just transpired—he'd be a fool not to. That being said, he just can't grapple with the fact that it actually happened. Something like that... with him... her? He just can't wrap his head around it... but it did happen. He didn't ask her, he didn't prompt her, he didn't really do much of anything. She simply brought it upon herself to make the decision and perform that action. So... does that mean?

His head rolls on his pillow as his eyes meet the ceiling once more. 

Times really have changed. Despite everything that's been lost, perhaps the future didn't have to be so morbidly bleak. He could contemplate the last month of his life infinitely—he probably will—but right now the faintest sliver of hope bleeds through. What's to come is undoubtably going to be difficult, undoubtably going to be treacherous, and certain going to take everything he has just to get by. That being said... perhaps some part of the future might be worth living for. 

Blake's words might actually be true.

He can live because of his past, not in spite of it.
