The farmstead is hardly visible on the horizon by the time Ryan stops walking. He never had a particular destination in mind when he left, but one particular windbreak down the road feels like as adequate a place as any. The barren, snow topped trees hang over a small ditch with a miniscule amount of frozen water at the bottom.

Ryan takes a seat on the ground with his back to one of these looming trees. He kicks his legs out in front of him and hangs his head. 

So much goes through his head all at once, yet it feels as though nothing is processing. Questions, thoughts, inquires, conjectures, all of them bounce around as if being thrown about by a powerful vortex inside his mind. When he tries to focus on what these 'voices' could be, he's immediately reminded of the phantom wounds and his stream of consciousness switches accordingly. He ties to recall what the talking mutant told him about dying before it's too late, but then his ability comes to mind. The world blinks and he finds himself sitting against the tree—probably only a few seconds earlier. The only steady thought he's even capable of, is the one he's having right now. Knowing that he's confused and spiraling is the only coherent thing he processes.

When his mind won't budge on its rampant context switching, he instead turns to his senses to fill the void.

The icy air makes his functional lungs sting a little. The dead grass and small layer of snow below him provides for an acceptable seating arrangement, but the frozen water slowly begins to melt underneath him. The wind is all but absent as the windbreak does its job for any gusts that may exist.

"Why?" he says to no one in particular.

"Why what?"

Ryan's head snaps to the side to find an unfamiliar face inspecting him with a welcoming smile. The person in question appears to be a quirky young man with a reading glasses—the ones with the chain around the back—and long curly hair. His disposition feels incredibly relaxed despite only wearing a thin flannel on top of a bland shirt. It's the kind of casual confidence he would expect from someone like Drew, but this person presents it in a similar vain, though a bit more... awkward?

Rising to his feet slowly, Ryan studies the man carefully.

"Why what?" the man repeats, "What seems to be the problem?"

"Who are you?" Ryan asks bluntly.

The man scratches his head, his curly hair shaking back and forth in the process, "That's... uh... it's harder to explain than you'd think."

Ryan's hand slowly drifts towards his knife, but he doesn't pull it out quite yet.

The man snickers at the lack of response, "I thought you'd ask me more questions than that."

Ryan lowers his gaze a bit, "I'm still thinking."

With a knowing look, the man walks a little ways down to the ditch and crouches in front of the frozen stream.

"I quite like winter," he looks back, "What about you?" 

Ryan shrugs, "It's fine... I guess."

The man smirks as he pulls his reading glasses off and lets them dangle around his neck. Why he was wearing them in the first place is beyond Ryan. Regardless of the reason, the man isn't bothered by it as he carefully steps on the ice and notices his foot loosing traction. He then pushes off the solid ground and glides across the ice to the other side of the ditch.

"That's wonderfully simple," the man comments as he turns around and does the same thing back, "I've seen children do this before, but I've never done it myself."

The man glances up expecting a response.

Ryan shrugs, "Sure."

The curious man quickly steps away from the ice and folds his hands nervously, "I... have a question I'd like to ask you."

The lack of response seems to be sufficient for him.

"So..." he starts thoughtfully, "I have this friend... she's of the shy sort and doesn't like to open up about things. Not only that, but I can tell she's under tremendous pressure at the moment, so I'm naturally really concerned about her. The problem is, I don't have the faintest clue how I can help her..." he stares at him earnestly, "Any idea what I should do?"

The statement strikes Ryan as familiar but hones in on a response too quickly to analyze it further, "Why would I know. We don't know each other."

The man nods respectfully, "Yes that's true. Though, is it really a bad thing to want to learn from others?"

Ryan shrugs, "I guess not."

"So," the man prompts once again, "What shall I ever do?"

For once, Ryan's thoughts seem to solidify on something. That something just so happens to be the question at hand. Perhaps the social expectation is enough to get him outside his internal storm—even if briefly.

Mulling the question over, he can't stop thinking about Alison and what he should do. Just a few minutes ago, he wanted nothing more to run after his friends in town, and now he simply wants to save a suffering friend from herself. To be honest, he doesn't have a clue what he's supposed to do.

"I... don't know," he responds honestly, though a bit quiet.

"I'm sorry," the man prompts, "what was that?"

Ryan looks up but doesn't find the quirky man. Instead, he finds the spitting image of Alison standing before him.

Although her tone serious, she can't restrain a subtle smirk across her face, "What were you going to do to help me?"

He blinks his eyes and suddenly the quirky, glasses wearing man is standing before him. The man cocks his head to the side with a confused look, "Is something wrong?"

Ryan stumbles backwards and bumps into the tree behind him. Concealing his eyes with a hand, he tries to take a deep breath. Whatever he just saw has to be a hallucination. Is this the sort of thing that Alison was experiencing—seeing and hearing things that can't be possible? Maybe this whole situation is going to his head.

Searching for grounds to speak, he says the first thing that pops into his head, "Sorry."

The man shrugs, "It's alright, I just didn't hear what you said before... or was it supposed to be a mental note?"

"No," Ryan answers quickly, "I just... have a lot going on right now."

The stranger nods his head understandingly as he takes a step closer to Ryan. His hand falls onto Ryan's shoulder, "I understand. You have no need to be shy."

Ryan brushes his hand off, "What? I still don't know you."

The two of them stare at each other briefly. This peculiar stranger still harbors an almost uncanny confidence as he casually steps back with an insightful smile. His light clothing doesn't seem to bother him as a stray gust of wind makes it through the trees. Ryan, on the other hand, reaches for the tree behind him. The situation feels precarious to be sure, but he still hasn't quite decided whether that uncertainty is dangerous or just uncomfortable. At this point, he doesn't trust himself to make an adequate determination.

Pushing back his curly hair, the reasserts his glasses onto his face, "I'm just trying to help my friend, nothing more."

He doesn't want to be rude, but he simply can't handle this type of conversation at the moment so he defaults to a proclivity he thought he left behind. Turning his back he looks back in the direction he came, "Why should I care about you?"

"Because I need your help."

Ryan freezes solid. The first time was the exception, but a second time. That voice. 

When he looks back, he finds Alison timidly holding her hands together as her lips quiver.

"Please," she says somberly, "I can't handle these voices anymore."

Ryan blinks and suddenly finds the quirky glasses guy standing in her place. Just to be sure, he blinks a few more times, but the man doesn't shift.

This can't be right? He can't be going insane. Whatever he's seeing has to have some truth to it. He's never hallucinated once in his life, nightmares maybe, but that's one thing and this is a completely separate thing. There's no conceivable way that this is real. 

That's when an idea pops into his head.

Tugging at that invisible muscle scattered all across his being, he manages to interact with it. The world flashes.

The road back towards the homestead is mostly clear, dusted with white snow. He finds his voice already activated as he says, "—y should I ca—".

He stops himself mid-sentence and turns around. The strange man is still standing behind him—exactly where he was before. And for a moment, nothing happens. Then the man's expression changes. Not confusion—amusement.

"Is something wrong?" 

The question sounds genuine, though his face doesn't agree with that tone.

Ryan shakes his head, this time running both hands through his hair. What in the world is happening to him?

His mind takes off immediately, but a gut feeling begins to override his actions. It's only a hunch, and he's not even sure what he expects, but he's going to try anyway.

The world flashes.

Ryan stares off at the road back to the homestead with a longing look. He can feel his selfish words rising through his throat—this time he doesn't resist.

"Why should I care?" he says callously, the words hurting him as he hears them for the third time.

"Because I need your help."

Alison's voice sends goosebumps down Ryan's spine, though he doesn't bother to look back. He doesn't need his sight clear his conscience. 

"Who are you?" he says, motionless.

"Hm..." Alison mumbles, "I thought we could have a bit more fun with this."

Turning around, Ryan visually inspects the thing behind him knowing full well that it's not Alison. Her face, her hair, the way she stands, all of it is identical to the real thing. There's not a doubt in his mind that he'd believe this was her if he hadn't tested it. So what exactly is standing before him?

"Allow me to properly introduce myself," the creatures says. 

Suddenly Alison's face flickers. What once was a friend, suddenly becomes a metallic humanoid figure. Every aspect of the figure is coated in a glossy, almost liquid, gray that reflects hints of sunlight like a mirror. As it moves, the metallic coating shifts perfectly as to keep its humanoid appearance. This creature than swipes it's hand over its featureless face to leave behind two slits which resemble eyes. 

"My name is Experiment S, but you may call me S-019"

Ryan clenches his fists. Any fear or hesitation he may have had suddenly dissipates underneath a surge of anger. He manages to hold himself back and avoid displaying said outrage, but he surely wanted to.

"Why?" he says in a forced manner, though quite calm considering what he wanted to say, "Why do you people always have to show up?"

S-019 chuckles despite not having a visible mouth, "Calling me a person is quite amusing," its voice darkens to a more serious, direct tone, "I'm an experiment. Don't forget that."

"I don't care, just leave us alone," Ryan claims uncharacteristically, his words don't feel natural as they exist his mouth with minimal thought, but at this point... at this point truly doesn't care anymore. He's done. He can't keep playing these games.

"Ah," S-019 musses as it strokes it's featureless face, "You really are on your way to becoming an experiment."

Ryan shakes his head and takes a step away, "What is your people's obsession with experiments. Every single one of you are treating it like it means something!"

S-019 goes quiet for a moment which causes Ryan's hand to shake. 

"An Experiment does mean something," S-019 counters calmly, "Being part of the Organization doesn't make you an experiment. Having Doctor Crane attempt his ridiculous research on you doesn't make you an experiment. An Experiment is a very particular state of being."

"I don't understand what that means."

S-019 sighs, "I'm not supposed to tell you this, but I will anyway," the humanoid shapeshift crosses its arms behind its back and proceeds to take a few steps away, "Being an experiment has three distinct criteria. The first is a supernatural ability. You know what I'm referring to. Experiment P can regenerate, I can shift forms, and you... well, I'm sure you understand."

Ryan swallows hard as his feet long to run, but his head still struggles to make a decision.

"The second criterion is likely the most important, and it is why experiments don't... often come into conflict," it continues, "Experiments... cannot die."

S-019 leaves Ryan in silence as its words hang in the air like a wet blanket. He wishes that this statement would have confused him as it makes his heart burn to know that he understands. Crane didn't die when his head got chopped off. Pesh could regenerate his entire skull. And him... the bullet in his chest, the blood in his throat, all of that means nothing? Is this why... is this why Heimes offered him a knife? Is this why that talking mutant told him to die? They did those things because they knew he was coming to this? He doesn't want to think about this anymore.

"And the third?" he asks, looking at the ground.

S-019 snickers, "I shouldn't say. After all, you'll figure that out soon enough."

Ryan looks back up at the mythical entity. He still hasn't forgiven this monster, but if there's any truth to its statement, this is not a conflict that can be resolved with force. That being said, there are still so many questions; so many things left unanswered. Is he supposed to accept this?

"I..." he tries, still unsure of how he's even supposed to respond, "When does this end?"

"When you're dead," S-019 responds unflinchingly as it glances over its shoulder. Ryan does a double-take, but the monster unapologetically upholds its statement as the inside corner of its eyes lowers provokingly, "Though, seeing as you're on your way to becoming one of us, I'm sure you understand how difficult that may be."

Something spontaneously flairs up in his mind. On one hand, he's so incredibly exhausted by all of this that he wants to collapse, yet another part of him has conceded to only caring about one thing. That solitary desire outweighs any fear or apprehension he may have.

"Okay fine," he claims with amble resolve, "But leave my friends out of this."

S-019 lowers its head as if a smirk were crossing its face, "And why would I do that?"

Ryan immediately takes a step forward and reaches for his gun, "They didn't choose any of this! Don't bring them in. Stop whispering to Alison and don't even look at anyone else."

Shaking its head, S-019 turns around to face Ryan. It notices the halfway drawn pistol in his hand and heeds it no mind, "I'll offer you this. I promise—I swear that I will never speak to Alison in any way, shape, or form." It looks Ryan over, a hint of amusement crossing its minimalistic face, "Will that satisfy you?"

"Yeah," Ryan responds without thinking, "No! You'll leave all of them alone!"

S-019 remains undisturbed, "You're free to try and stop me. But know that it is a futile effort for both of us."

Ryan clenches his fist. A burning resentment makes his arm shake as he hangs his head in protest. Finally, when he can't bare it anymore, he whips his gun into place and points it directly at S-019's ugly head.

"What happens if I just kill you here?" he states, realizing how impulsive and redundant the idea is.

S-019 doesn't laugh. It doesn't snicker, scoff, or even look confused. Instead, the inhuman creature remains motionless for some time. The gun pointed at it provides zero motivation to answer.

"Do it," it finally responds calmly, almost dismissively, "I dare you."

Ryan prides himself on not being provoked easily. Ever since he made a mental shift in high school, he's consistently put thought behind ever action before giving into rage or aggression. This one instance, however, is the counter example. Without further contemplation, he pulls the trigger.

The bullet pierces S-019's head creating a perfectly round hole that spans the entire width of its gray skull. Then, in a strangely unexpected manner, the monster collapses into a thick, metallic goop on the ground. It doesn't regenerate, reform, or anything else, it simply exists as a semi-liquid goop on the ground next to the creek. The anticlimactic ending keeps Ryan silent for some time afterward. S-019 never reforms.

Ryan trudges back to the homestead in both mental and physical exhaustion. In the heat of the moment, he didn't realize that his time jumps had taken a toll on him, but now that he has some breathing room, he notices the subtle impact that makes him blink slowly. He doesn't quite need to sleep or rest, but a few more time jumps, and he would probably be in need of one.

The subsequent walk back is slow and methodical. He neither wants to return nor stay away long. The duality of his desires makes his mind ache. Luckily, the habitual nature of the action leads him towards the homestead without much thought. Whether care or strife is waiting for him, he'll accept whatever comes—so long as Alison and the others are alright.

He rounds the end of the fence line with his head down. It takes a few more steps for him to look up but eventually he does. The whole world freezes solid at what he finds.

The invisible muscle jerks as hard as he can physically make it. The world blinks. He finds himself a few dozen yards back.

Scrambling forward, he races around the fence line and checks the scene one more time. 

The world blinks.

He stumbles forward and checks it again.

Using all his strength, he tugs at the muscle so hard it would tear and any other part of his body. The homestead appears near the horizon—Ryan nearly collapses.

Trudging forward down the road at a swift but arduous pace, he turns the corner of the fence line and is struck with the exact same scene once again.

Blink. Blink. Blink. Blink.

At some point he finds himself crawling back to the homestead, praying that something, anything could change. Even if he could have just mere seconds to prevent this. Please! Just one second!

At some point, maybe ten... maybe eleven loops in, he finally caves. He falls to his knees and stares at the scene in utter disbelief. His head shakes from side to side slowly while the rest of him trembles violently. Tears well up in his eyes as the cold bites at his sorrow.

For a moment, he feels like he's in a freezer again. The frigid late January air, the claustrophobic circumstance, his mind lagging behind. The woman he saw there looked like she was in so much pain and he found himself afraid. That same feeling returns, though this time, it doesn't subside.

Alison's window—on the second floor—lies open. Hanging from that window is a crooked visage with drool dripping from her mouth. Her hollow eyes surrounded by a red ring stare forward with pink, puffy cheeks. Her arms and legs dangle pathetically above the first floor window. A taut rope holds her up.

S-019 was right, it won't speak to Alison again. No one will.
