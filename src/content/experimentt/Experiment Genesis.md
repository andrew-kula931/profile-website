As S-019 said, a bandit camp sits a few miles north of town. Distinguished from the surroundings by multiple buildings with smoke coming out of the chimneys, Ryan and Drew have no issues finding it. The journey there is actually quite silent as neither he nor Drew engages in any meaningless conversation. They both understand the mission: one out of desperation and the other with a begrudging attitude. 

Under Drew's orders they take his personal vehicle over which incidentally allows him to park the truck where he wants. The destination of choice is a little ways away behind a thick wall of trees—just far enough away that no one can see the truck pull up. When they grind to a halt, Ryan tries to leave immediately, but Drew catches him.

"What?" he asks quickly, "Do you have a plan in mind?"

Drew purses his lips, suddenly a bit hesitant to speak, "You do understand what you've just agreed to do?"

Ryan shakes his head while looking out the window, "What choice do I have."

Taking a deep breath, Drew pulls out a pocket knife from his camo coat and inspects it passively, "You could give up on your friends and spare the lives of dozens of people."

Ryan's eyes widen as his snaps in that direction. What in the world is he thinking, abandoning innocent teenagers in their most desperate time of need... no. What is _he_ thinking. He's about to kill dozens of innocent people, just for the illusion of temporary safety for his friends. Is this... is this really the right thing to do?

Originally pressing his hand against the door handle, Ryan slowly recedes it away and looks down into his lap. This action seems to give Drew the go ahead to continue speaking.

Drew stares at his knife as it begins to twirl between his fingers, "I grew up... a lot like you, actually. Quiet, reserved, shitty luck. My father got a hit put on him when I was six. Barely knew the guy in the casket. About a year later, the hitman came back and cleaned up the loose ends after my mother started doing some personal investigation... I was the only one who survived." he grabs onto the knife's handle with a firm grip, "The assassin _was_ going to finish me off too, but something happened, and he never went through with it. With nowhere to go, he gave the seven-year-old me an offer... I accepted. Fast-forward a few years, and he trained me to kill, to be tactical, to be deceptive: to win. He was a good mentor and an even better assassin. We ran countless missions together in my teenage years and killed countless people."

Drew slows his story as he purses his lips and swings the knife as if slashing someone's throat. His demeanor is as professional as it ever was, yet his eyes harbor a deeply rooted sorrow that Ryan can't look away from. They are the eyes of a man who knows what he's done.

"Anyway," Drew continues, "The assassin that took me in was one of the finest in the world and trained me as such. I still remember his last moments. It was in a fit of coughing up blood that he told me that he was glad that my training paid off. I succeeded in fulfilling my end of the deal—I had become the monster I sought to destroy and avenged my family."

The story lags once more as the knife shifts uncomfortably before dropping onto his lap.

"I wandered for the next few years. It wasn’t until the day after my 23rd birthday that someone found me. His name was Grant. And he was the best soldier the military had to offer. Our meeting was rather hostile, but it became apparent that no matter how skilled I was, he was better. At one point he got me in a position where he had the choice to kill me or let me live. If I were him, I would have finished it. But he's not me. He let me stand up and told me that he wanted me to help him kill the scum of this world. He told me that monsters like us don’t deserve to be happy, but if we can prevent others from becoming like us, maybe our lives would have some meaning. I'd be a hypocrite not to acknowledge that I'd accepted a deal from the devil before... but this time was different. Maybe it was his natural charisma, or maybe it was the fact that I was ready to be done with life anyway. Either way, I joined the military no questions asked and, more specifically, the Allegiance One squad."

"Allegiance One..." Ryan echoes aloud, committing the name to memory. He's not sure why, but the name feels important.

Drew smirks a bit, "That squad was... something. Grant, myself, Markus, Nox, Carter, and good ole Jenks. We were... a bunch of broken men honestly," he chuckles to himself, "That being said, I never would have met any of them if it weren't for Grant giving me a chance out of that hellhole I'd crawled myself into."

"I don't understand," Ryan comments, uncertain.

"What I'm trying to say is..." Drew tries to reason out internally, "Killing people changed me, but someone brought me back from the brink. I think it's only fair if I give you the opportunity... to save yourself before any of that happens."

Ryan looks away, "But S-019 said that you can't intervene directly."

With a reluctant nod, Drew leans back in his seat, "Yeah, I know. I realize what I'm asking, and I know how you feel about your friends, but... have you stopped to consider what happens to you if you go through with this?"

He's right. Ryan stopped, even once, to consider anything beyond what is necessary to get his friends back. Even so, he's willing to risk it all if it means he can get Max's cooking back. Return Beth's playful responsibility. Save Blake's overbearing, but honest personality... touch Chloe just one more time. He knows all to well how it feels to lose someone lose to you, and here he is contemplating whether he should do that to potentially dozens of people. The one difference though, and honestly, the central factor in his mind is this: these some twenty unsuspecting people won't feel the pain for long. If Ryan doesn't do thing, he'll have to live with his friend's deaths forever—death won't save him from it. If anything, he's protecting these people from a pain they'll never need to experience.

"I don't care," Ryan eventually responds as he opens the door and steps out, "...I'm too tired to care."

Drew offers a concerned look, but doesn't respond right away. In light of this, Ryan steps out of the vehicle and begins to walk through the thick windbreak.

"Hey Ryan!" Drew calls out with his window rolled down, "I'll... help where I can."

Ryan nods as he continues on through the trees.

---

It takes him a while to get to the compound and he's pretty sure Drew drove off after he left, but in his mind the chains have already been set in motion. The bandit base is far less a series of conventional buildings and much more a set of fortified installations with a metal barrier connecting them.

“What do you want?” some guy calls out to him as he approaches the metal gate.

The compound is nothing like Ryan is used to. Instead of hastily crafted barriers made of cars and cardboard, this wall was engineered with metal beams and reinforced steel sheets. This group was not some measly survivor group but an organized force with a formidable defense.

"I..." Ryan tries, finding himself at a loss for words, "I just want to talk."

"What about?" the man questions, his face mostly covered with a thick, graying beard.

"It's..." Ryan attempts once more, his desperation feeling more disillusioned than ever before. 

"Come on," the man prompts, "Spit it out."

"I got attacked," Ryan half lies, "I... I just want to talk, nothing more. I promise I don't intend to stay."

The entire time he talks, Ryan can't bring himself to make eye contact. Whether this stranger believed his obvious bluff or completely rejected it is hard to say without looking up, but eventually the decision comes to pass.

The man lets out a sigh and steps off the stool or whatever he was standing on. The large double door is slowly pushed open with the man waiting for him on the other side.

"Right this way," he says empathetically, "We'll hear what you have to say."

Ryan looks up, his eyes widening.

Why did this man have to say it like that? Why did his voice have to grow friendly? Why couldn't he have called him a liar and refused to open the door? This isn't supposed to that hard.

Suddenly an ear rattling explosion erupts on the opposite end of the encampment with smoke quickly rising from the scene. All eyes turn in that direction as everyone, including Ryan, wonders what could be going on. Shortly after the initial explosion, screams erupt from that side of the compound and, with it, come the defiled cries of undead. 

He should be afraid, confused, or even stunned, so when everything clicks, it makes him wheezy. It's not supposed to be this hard.

While the man is distracted, Ryan pulls out his knife and cuts straight through his neck. The knife pass right by without a single ounce of resistance. The body and head fall to the ground asynchronously as Ryan watches it in silence. He should probably feel awful, horrified, hate himself—he feels nothing. Not an ounce of concern crosses his mind as he reaches down and retrieves the corpse's assault rifle.

Two people appear in the doorway of a nearby building. Ryan fires three round at them and misses all three—the assault rifle's recoil is far stronger than he anticipated. Using his demonic ability, time blinks backwards and he gets to try again. This time the bullets land.

His time jumping quickly becomes easier as the incidental practice eases the initial fatigue. Millisecond jumps seemingly feel like nothing, a subsequent jump of two seconds hardly phases him. A few more bandits rush out from a cabin and attempt to defend their home. They all receive bullets through their heads—it takes him three attempts.

Things seem to be going too smoothly as more and more screams of agony arise throughout the camp. Each scream quickly fades into nothingness only to be replaced by another. It feels like a vivid nightmare, one of unimaginable absurdity, that is until the screams are no longer of just men—but women too.

An explosion goes off near where the breach in the wall is. Gun fire begins to rapidly go off as the screams slow for a moment but then begin again. A cold chill runs through Ryan’s mind.

Suddenly a woman appears from around the corner of a building, in her arms is a little girl. She spots Ryan holding the gun but not the two dead people in the doorway. Her gaze doesn't study who he is, but relief does appear in her voice as the gun signifies all she cares to know.

“Oh thank god,” she gasps in a panic, "The zombies! They're getting in."

The little girl clinging to her mother looks up briefly and spots Ryan's horrified face before her mother does, "Who are you?"

Hearing her child, the woman looks up from the gun and notices who it is that she's speaking to. What she finds isn't that of a familiar guard, but rather a shaky teen with trembling hands. The AR in his arms barely stays in place it points back and forth quickly. His head shakes slowly in disbelief as his eyes remain as wide as they can possibly be. Ryan's teeth remain gritted together as struggles to exhale correctly or even think about formulating a response.

The woman's face fills with terror as she backs away slowly from him.

"Please," she begs, clutching her daughter carefully, "Please don't do this."

Should he let her run? Give her the opportunity to escape. Maybe there's some loophole in S-019's directions that will allow a few people to run away with their lives. Surely he doesn't have to— there's no way he could— this isn't right. He can't do this.

Suddenly a ragged form appears from the same corner the woman came from. The volatile human form rushes straight towards the woman from her blind spot. The daughter catches sight first and screams. The zombie lunges. Ryan fires three bullets. They all go still.

More people arrive to the door in a panic. Innocent blood splatters across their faces. Someone attempts to fight back with their own gun. They actually land a hit. Ryan blinks. The pain goes away. The bullet misses. Ryan's doesn't.

Pretty soon the escaping humans become far less prevalent than stray zombies. The mutating monsters are admittedly harder to kill but at least they don't fall over and beg for mercy. 

Someone does arrive to the entrance and pleads. Their body joins the rest. A little bit of life leaves Ryan's eyes.

Before too long the compound grows silent with only the crackling of the various fires to fill the graveyard with sound. 

Ryan shivers, though not from the cold. Feeling something, yet nothing, he turns around. To his shock, he finds someone else standing behind him next to the closed door. As if a reflex, he aims his gun and fires a shot but the person dodges with inhuman speed. In fact, they move so incredibly fast that Ryan doesn't even bother to recognize the familiar gray, featureless visage.

Ryan lowers his gun, “There. I did it... Now give me my friends back.”

S-019 shakes its head slowly, “No, you haven’t completed what I asked.”

"What?" Ryan says in utter disbelief.

S-019's slitted eyes narrow, "I told you to exterminate every breathing human here. There is still one remaining. Find them and exterminate them. Once that is complete, return to your farmhouse and I shall grant you your reward."

With that, S-019 opens the door and walks out leaving Ryan alone once more. 

Ryan doesn't grunt, nor sigh, he simply turns around slowly and gazes into the spotty inferno. He trudges further into the compound. Corpse after blood stained corpse passes by him as he marches through the compound with his feet dragging through the snow.

In front of one particular cabin, he witnesses four dead zombies with bullet holes littering their bodies. One such zombie has a large blade for an arm which conveniently lays next to a decapitated human. The next cabin has multiple broken windows with blood covering the inside of the few that remain. A body lays dead laying through one window with their abdomen impaled on the broken glass and large gashes on the section of their body still remaining in the cabin. A few paces later he arrives by a dead woman laying on the ground with a large hole in her back. It wasn’t from bullets nor an explosion but from gnarled fingers shredding her back and taking a great deal of flesh and organs with them. 

A little ways further is a corpse so grossly mutilated that he can’t even tell what they looked like before. Beside the grotesque scene is a smaller corpse with a detached arm. A man kneels over them sobbing softly. Ryan's gun razes slowly but his hand shakes so severely he can’t muster the strength to complete the action. The man then lifts a pistol to his head and fires it, falling back and staring up at Ryan. Steve’s bloodshot eyes stare widely at him as blood oozes from his temple. Ryan blinks and the man completely disappears only leaving the two mutilated corpses. 

He can almost feel countless pairs of eyes staring at him as he turns from the scene and continues on. As he walks, he looks down at his clothes to find them stained with blood. Everything from his pants to his face are doused in the blood of innocent people. In the blink of an eye, the blood disappears and his slick black jacket hides even the slightest bit of blood. 

Is this what he wanted?

The next group of corpses are a mixture of zombie and human as half have large bite marks in them while the others have bullet holes. The hole in the wall lies beyond this scene still echoing with the silent cries of the damned. 

Is this worth his friends lives?

The infirmary is blazing in a massive fire as he trudged on by. His feet practically sagging at this point. He glances into the inferno and spots an operating table with a woman on it. Her body is completely immune to the raging flames around her as she shakes violently attempting to break out of her restraints. She lets out a blood curdling scream as tears roll down her eyes and she whispers: “Kill me”. Ryan blinks once again only to find a normal looking operating table engulfed in the raging inferno. 

Has his experiences taught him nothing?

He walks a few more paces to find a dead man equipped with armor soiled in blood and guts surrounded by four zombies. A bloody knife lays next to his deceased body as each dead zombie has various cut wounds on each of them. Each zombie has either lost its head or cuts so deep down its neck it might as well have lost its head. 

Not much further than that, Ryan walks past a cabin with a body hanging down from the roof. A noose wrapped around the neck holding it only a foot or so off the ground. A straw bag covers the head of the body. A small gust of wind rushes through and flings the bag off the corpse’s head revealing Alison hanging there. Dead. Ryan blinks and the body becomes a simple lantern being held up by that rope. 

It’s at that moment that Ryan hears a sound from the last living thing within that compound: the cry of a baby. The sound originates from a cabin nearby which Ryan slowly heads towards dropping his gun in the middle of the path as he pushes the door open. A dead woman lays outside the front door with her blood splattered across the wooden exterior. The corpse perfectly propped in front of the door to prevent it from being opened. Ryan slowly pushes the corpses out of the way as he holds his breath and heads inside. The wailing of the baby continues inside of the small nursery looking interior with no blood or guts anywhere else within. He blinks multiple times making sure that it’s not another hallucination, but every time he wishes it would stop, another round of infant cries returns him to reality.

Ryan slowly and weakly walks over to a baby stroller turned away from the front door and flips it around. Inside an infant sits crying for its mother to return to it. 

Have you stopped to consider what happens to you if you go through with this?

Ryan places his hands against his head and drops to his knees, his breath increasing rapidly and the wailing of the baby pounding itself into his head. Each second of noise feels like an eternity of pain perpetually being thrown at him. 

This isn’t right. He should have listened and cut his losses. This isn't right. This isn’t right. This isn’t right. This isn’t right. This isn’t right.

This is wrong.

Who does he think he is?

Why are his friends so important?

Who is Ryan Lancer?

Ryan’s hyperventilating continues relentlessly as he gets dizzy and nearly collapses. He struggles to his feet out of a instinctually although he wishes he would have stayed on the ground. As his breath eventually begins to slow, tears do not build up in his eyes. Sadness does not make a presence across his face. Not even regret is allowed to be shown. 

His eyes, as dead as all those he’s lost.

He pulls out his holstered pistol and points it at the baby. 

He can still pull back and stop himself from tipping over the edge. If he crosses this line, he’ll be selling his humanity for the uncertainty of his friends’ future. But if he doesn’t he’ll be sealing their fate forever. 

His finger shivers on the trigger unable to cross this line. For the last few months he’s tried so hard to retain his humanity while also protecting those he cares most for in the world. He’s put himself into countless evils and faced the worst the world has to offer. Can he really collapse now? Is it right to? Is this really where his humanity needs to end.

He blinks and when his eyes reopen he finds himself staring out into a golden field. The warm sunlight illuminates the landscape of grain as a warm wind gently brushes against his skin. Chloe stands a few feet away with her arms behind her back. She smiles at Ryan with that warm and genuine smile. He recalls all those times in school when Chloe would look at him meekly through the halls and he didn’t notice. He remembers all those times in the prison when he would watch Chloe laugh as she told him about the time she dropped her phone into the wishing well. He remembers lying on the library couch wrapped in each other’s arms. All of those memories, associated with someone who dies if he doesn't pull this trigger.

Ryan gradually returns to reality looking down at the crying baby. His breathing begins to pick up again yet his eyes still remain as dead as before. No matter what happens, he’s no longer the same kid who tried to drop out of school.

Only a few crows landing on a nearby roof know what happens after that. But even if they could speak… they wouldn’t.

Ryan stares at the hood of the truck completely spaced out. Drew doesn't get out of the drivers seat and ask if he's okay, instead he remains behind the wheel—watching him. They exchange glances briefly, though what the man before him finds is not the same Ryan who just left. Perhaps this is similar to how he felt after meeting the creep for the first time, though this time is different. Back then he was forced into the situation and simply had to endure, this time... this time he made a choice. The results were his and his alone.

His thoughts slowly begin drifting off into oblivion. He's fairly certain he was thinking, but at some point that just ends. A vale of silence falls over his mind as he stares at the truck's headlights as if they are the only thing that exists.

What just happened?

The thought pierces through the vale like a surgical needle, a bellowing voice amidst his confusion. His eyes raise slightly to find Drew watching with an indecipherable look. Perhaps he understands, though he probably doesn't. Honestly, it doesn't matter. His warning was sincere and yet Ryan didn't listen. And the worst part... he'd probably do it again.

Why does he think that?

He closes his eyes. The corpses, the pain, the isolation. It stacks up like an accumulated Jenga tower of regret—it's foundation almost completely torn apart. Here he is standing on the top of this ever rising structure whose parts are as finite as his will to keep going. He reaches up at his friends who are only a few more levels above, just two or three more moves and he'll finally reach them. If he can just make the right move, just a few more times, it doesn't matter how destroyed the base of the tower is... he reach his goal. Just a little further, that's all he needs to do.

His fingers stretch upwards, the goal is so, so close. Then that roof disappears and the crying baby appears. The dead woman, the girl, Alison, Jeremy, Steve, Amber—more and more people begin to fill the void between him and the people he's so desperate to retrieve. He shakes his head, unwilling to accept the outcome, he shakes his head refusing it all. That doesn't stop Heimes from appearing in front of him with a knife in hand.

"Do yourself a favor… end your suffering before it begins."

Heimes voice echoes in his head like the reverberating bell of a church. The voice reminding him of what Steve did, of what Alison did... of what Doctor Crane did to him.

As his senses return, he finds himself screaming. His lungs strenuously pushing out as much air as they physically can. The muscles in his neck bulge as his face grows hot at the action. This whole process happens while he watches himself do it from afar. His consciousness stands a few feet behind while Ryan places a gun to his head. And pulls the trigger.

The world flashes and he's once again staring into the headlights of the truck. He's standing inside his own shoes with his own body. His eyes drift upwards to find Drew's unreadable expression. 

"So this is how it is..." he mutters under his breath.

Very well. He made his choice... now... and way back then. This is simply the consequence of such a decision. This is the cost of his life.
