When the sun finally rises on the following Thursdays... no, Friday morning, Ryan sits up in his bed with exactly zero hours of rest. Vincenzo's words, the events of the previous day, and everything else made any potential nightmares feel like dreams by comparison. Alas, when the sun rose, he decides it better to just get up and start his day. 

With a bit of light, the first thing he does is wash all the blood off of him. He was a little startled when he realized that blood still cover his arms, but with a quick sink visit he cleans himself off to an acceptable degree.

Out of habit, he proceeds to stretch in the center of his room, being careful not to disturb any of the hanging snowflakes or the tranquil paper crane. He extends his legs out to either side and reaches down the middle with a drawn out breath as he slides forward. At first, these stretches felt silly and a little embarrassing, but it's at a time like this that the movements of his fatigued muscles feels oddly soothing. It bugs him that it took him this long to realize the therapeutic properties of such an exercise. 

After a thorough stretching, he moves on to some calisthenic exercises. Calling it that makes it sound elite and prestigious, but in actuality, it consisted solely of body weight workouts such as pushups and sit-ups. Yet again, he's reminded of how silly it feels to lie on the floor and work a singular muscle group, but with so much on his mind, the mundane task feels like a welcome relief. As such, he finds himself pulling his jacket and belt off and repping as many push-ups as he possibly can. When his arms grow tired and demand a break, he thinks back to the screams in the stadium and pushes himself through five more. Afterward, his arms complain once more which triggers a vision of a hanging body, and he squeezes out three more push-ups. Finally, his arms threaten to collapse so he finishes out one last push-up by claiming that doing so would prevent Steve from pulling the trigger. Of course, he knows it's empty motivation, but he manages to complete the last one.

When his workouts come to an end, he finds his brow soaked with sweat. He reaches for his jacket but remembers how resistant the material is to liquid of any kind. Therefore, he pulls up the bottom of his shirt to wipe his face off. When he drops it with a deep breath, he finds Chloe standing in the doorway. Her eyes staring at his abdomen, but quickly dart upwards once Ryan takes full notice of her.

"Oh... uh, you're awake," she stammers, avoiding eye contact.

"Yeah," Ryan says slowly as he pulls a box out from under his bed.

Inside the box is a collection of clothes that were donated to him one people realized he didn't have any spares. Grabbing the first clean shirt off the top, he proceeds to start pulling his sweaty shirt off but stops when he realizes Chloe's standing in the doorway.

Chloe glances at the box of clothes and immediately turns around, "Sorry, I didn't realize you were changing."

"It's fine," Ryan comments, "I'm only changing my shirt."

"Okay," Chloe responds with her back remaining toward him, "I just wanted to check on you."

Normally, he'd probably have said "I'm fine" or "Everything's alright", but all of those answers feel too disingenuous for him to indulge. After all, she's probably going to ask him why he was acting so out of character the night before. He never got a straight answer out of Drew, but his actions spoke loud enough that "I'm fine" won't cut it.

"You probably want to know about last night," Ryan assumes as he drops his sweaty shirt on the ground and quickly pulls the clean one over himself, "I'm done changing."

Only after he says that he's done changing does he realize that he just dodged a bullet. Chloe could have seen his scar—the bite mark.

Chloe turns around slowly and nods her head, "Only if you want to talk about it."

Ryan takes a seat on his bed and folds his hands together, "There's not much to say... Last night... a lot happened."

With softening eyes and an empathetic expression, Chloe enters the room and stands right next to him. She gestures towards the bed, "May I sit."

Without a word, Ryan scoots over and allows enough space for Chloe to take a seat next to him.

"Everyone's calling you a hero," Chloe reports, "...they're not wrong."

Ryan looks over, "What's that supposed to mean?"

Rolling her eyes, Chloe nudges him on the shoulder, "Everyone at the school already knew what you're capable of."

To say that he doesn't share the sentiment would be an understatement. Both of those successes, they weren't real victories, they were just sequences of events that let to his ultimate survival. Jeremy saving a girl he's never met. Steve fighting a giant mutant. Those were real heroes. Ryan doesn't fit the bill. Then again, if he didn't act, how many would have made it out? He wishes he could perish the thought, but it clings to his mind like an incessant tumor. 

"Is that so," he eventually says, just to feel like he responded.

Chloe smiles awkwardly, "Honestly, what I really wanted to say is thank you. Thank you for coming when you did... my dad would have been the next person to be selected if you hadn't shown up."

Ryan held out fairly well through the entire discussion, but the second the word "selected" came out of Chloe's mouth, he falters. He turns his head, bites his bottom lip, and clenches his fist. What happened in that lab should stay in that lab and never see the light of day—nor his memories.

Chloe, noticing Ryan's visceral reaction, backtracks by waving her hands defensively, "Oh, I'm sorry, I didn't mean to say something insensitive."

For some odd reason, Chloe apologizing to him evokes some sort of guilt on his conscience. The guilt is powerful enough to break him out of his own head, "Don't apologize. You did nothing wrong."

She shakes her head, "Even so—"

"It's fine," Ryan asserts, "After all, I know you're just trying to cheer me up."

Ryan's gaze falls to the floor and his grip on his own hands loosens up. A lull falls on the conversation as he finds that Chloe's mere presence is enough to alleviate some of the pain in his chest. He's not sure how it works, but at this point he doesn't care. It's easier to breath, that's what matters.

"I wanted to ask..."  Chloe fiddles with her thumbs nervously as her eyes avoid looking over, "back there... was that Jeremy?"

Ryan takes a strained breath. He also expected this conversation to occur. When he brought Chloe with him to the stadium, he knew she'd find out about what transpired. He tried his best to conceal it, but there's only so much he can do.

"Yes," he says simply as his eyes close, "There was... I just..." he clenches his fist as he finds his checks growing hot, "He demanded to come."

Preparing for some words of consolation, he grabs onto his knees. He just knows that Chloe's going to try some cheesy pep talk to cheer him up. After all, she's much better with her words than he is. Knowing her, she'll probably be able to help him too, yet he can't help but feel like a weakling for wanting that. It's not her responsibility to help him. No one asked her to. He just needs some time to process it all, words won't solve anything—not now.

The articulate words and empathetic presentation that he was preparing for never comes, however. Instead, he feels soft skin press up against his hand. His eyes dart open to find Chloe overlapping his hand with hers. Not only that, she has the audacity to lay her head on his shoulder with her eyes forced clothes. For a moment, she even looks like she's mourning Jeremy—a boy she barely knew. Instead of a powerful speech, she elects silence, something he never expected her to choose. Yet, despite his surprise, he feels as though that barrier he was constructing to protect himself... was an unnecessary effort to begin with.

Suddenly the sound of rushed footsteps echoes down the hallway and Ryan snaps to attention. His sudden jolt also causes Chloe head to raise off of him. Right after that, a new unfamiliar face appears in the doorway of Ryan's room. The boy in question looks to be twelve, maybe thirteen years old and promptly glances around the room, awestruck. His attention finally lands on the two teens sitting on the bed together. He gives a knowing look.

"You leave the table for five minutes and here you are cuddling with your boyfriend," the boy comments bluntly with undertones of mischief.

"He's not—I mean, I was just—" Chloe stammers unconvincingly.

Ryan stares the boy down directly without blinking, "Is there a problem with that."

The smirking boy immediately loses any traction as Ryan's words seemingly have a stronger effect on him than he initially expected. 

"I—uh," the boy stutters, "I don't know what you see in my stupid sister."

Chloe groans, "Charlie, why do you have to be like this?" she turns to Ryan, "Sorry, this is my little brother Charlie. He and my father were part of the group in the warehouse."

Ryan stares the boy down in thought. Charlie is a few years older than Karmen's son, yet they look strikingly similar. Of course, they have to share a name... life is simply cruel like that. 

"Uh..." Charlie comments, "Why are you staring at me? It's kinda weird."

Ryan shakes his head, "Sorry, got lost in thought."

Charlie runs a hand through his hair and looks off into the distance, "It's fine, I get lost in thought too. Us cool guys think a lot."

Chloe rolls her eyes, "You just made that up. I don't think you've ever thought anything through."

Charlie's mouth drops, "Not true! I've thought lots of things through, like how I'm going to drive a motorcycle when I'm older."

In light of the comment, Chloe glances at Ryan briefly before turning back to her little brother, "Oh yeah, and what brought that on?"

The boy's gaze lands on Ryan but quickly turns back to his sister as if a bit embarrassed by the diversion, "Uh... stop asking stupid questions. Sheesh, Ryan you can call my stupid sister stupid."

Every time this boy speaks a word, all he hears is a younger Charlie saying the exact same line. Every passing phrase is another thing Karmen would have had to respond to. The resemblances stunts his response.

Ryan shakes his head, finally returning to his senses, "I won't do that."

Suddenly the boy's demeanor takes a one-eighty, and he leans his shoulders back nonchalantly, "Ye–yeah, because cool guys respect woman. You're right. My sister's just annoying, not stupid."

Chloe crosses her arms with exasperation, "You're not even going to apologize?"

Charlie looks off into the distance, "Why would I apologize to _you_?"

Chloe groans and rolls her eyes again, "I can't with you."

Right after that, another person enters the scene. This new face is still a bit foreign, but Ryan distinctly recognizes him from the night before.

"Charlie, stop being rude to your sister," Chloe's father says as he stands behind Charlie looking into the room. He immediately spots the two teens, "Is he interrupting you two?"

Chloe shakes her head, "It's too late at this point."

The man—previously wearing a baseball cap—pulls it off his head and place it on his son's, "Charlie, why don't you go back to the cafeteria?"

Charlie glares at his father through the brim of the hat while crossing his arms. Without a word, he takes three steps—just enough for his father to look away—before creeping back in behind his father.

The father takes a step into Ryan's room and places his hands into his jean pockets, "I'm sure my daughter's already told you, but I'm Eugene Frame, but you can just call me Gene."

"Okay," Ryan responds simply.

Gene raises an eyebrow, "Man of few words, I can respect that... regardless, I want to extend my appreciation for what you did."

Ryan watches him intently but remains silent. It's not that he has lacks things to say, but rather that talking with a father feels particularly more awkward than any other adult. More precisely, talking with Chloe's father is what makes it awkward. He finds it strange, though, because he and Chloe aren't dating or anything. At the end of the day he doesn't feel like reading into it, however.

Gene glances between him and Chloe, a knowing glance and a smirk begins to grow on his face.

"Dad," Chloe complains, "You're about to say something embarrassing, aren't you?"

Gene shrugs his shoulders, "What? I'm your father after all, I have to tease your boyfriend a little."

The part of being a boyfriend catches both of them off guard, though Ryan keeps his response minimal by averting his eyes to a poorly drawn picture of a lion. Chloe's reaction, however, is far more conspicuous as her ears begin to grow red. Her father takes note of this and doubles down.

"So... how far have you gotten?" he says cheekily, "It's imperative that I know."

"Dad!" Chloe blurts out with a whiney tone and a defensive voice, "That's none of your business."

Gene holds back a laugh as his daughter tries in vain to push him out of the room, "You really are your mother's daughter, you know that?"

Chloe changes her tactic and tries dragging her father away. Gene isn't a large man by any stretch of the imagination, but he isn't small either. If Ryan stands up they'd probably match eye level, though Ryan probably has a few pounds on the man as his physique screams white collar, though without the beer belly. Assumptions aside, he's still larger and heavier than his daughter which makes the struggle grossly one-sided.

"Every time I have friends over, you're always trying to embarrass me," Chloe complains as she eventually gives up and takes a seat on the chair in the corner of the room. 

Her father crosses his arms with a smile, "That's just how I show that I love you."

Chloe sighs with exasperation, "At least be more respectful to Ryan."

All eyes quickly fall onto Ryan—including Charlie who peaks his head in from the hall.

"You're not uncomfortable, are you?" Gene wonders honestly.

Ryan doesn't need to contemplate the question to know his answer, "No, I can tell you're a doting father."

Gene glances at his daughter contently, "See, he's fine with my banter."

Chloe turns her head and crosses her arms, "Well I'm not."

Shrugging his shoulders, Gene turns to Ryan and outstretches his hand, "I've been told that you've looked out for my daughter on multiple occasions. I want to thank you for that too, sincerely. It means the world to me that my family can be together again, you know, despite everything that's happened recently."

Wanting to be polite, Ryan accepts the handshake. Looking at this family, he can feel the underlying bonds between them. If he had been in this situation a month ago, he'd probably have brushed it all off and scoffed as such cheesy family dynamics... but this isn't a month ago. The connection that they share, even if mundane, puts him at a loss for words. If things had gone differently, maybe he could have been like this with the Hughes. Steve accepting him a bit more and revealing his personality. Him getting upset by little things but not really holding it against his father. Charlie—the same as always—getting into trouble by messing around but never called out for it. The only one missing is Karmen. Speaking of, he remembers seeing a photo of their family. If he's not mistaken, there was a puppy... well... no, what he really wants to know is where the mother is?

"Could... could I ask a question," Ryan offers, catching Gene a little by surprise.

"Oh, absolutely. What do you want to know?"

Ryan can't bring himself to look the man in the eyes, but he simply has to clear the air. His mind won't rest until he knows the truth.

"I... uh..." Ryan starts slowly, "I saw a picture of your family a couple of weeks ago. Did... did you have a wife?"

The whole room goes bitterly silent—silent enough for Ryan to regret ever asking the question.

"Sorry," he apologizes quickly.

Gene looks over at his daughter before shaking his head, "It's alright. My wife, Virginia, died before everything went down... It was a drunk driver. As for our dog, Baxter... well... I like to imagine he's roaming free in the wild right now. Couldn't take him when we got captured."

Still feeling a bit guilty, he hesitates to even consider the idea of asking another question, but the logistics become to great to overlook. After all, he saw the scene of the crime.

"How did you guys get captured?"

Gene nods his head as if expecting to be asked this question, "The military. Or at least I thought it was. Turns out they were just henchmen of that sadistic doctor. Though, I'm sure you already know that."

It's unfortunate to admit, but he does know what he's talking about. Though, he learned it in a rather unconventional way. The fact that it had to come back feels tragically bitter-sweet. Then again, why didn't he see any guards at the warehouse? If the creep had henchmen, why were they missing? Surely they weren't all asleep. Well, regardless it doesn't do any good to dwell on it now. He has no plans on ever returning to that warehouse.

Gene shifts in place and smirks a bit, "That's enough about me. I want to know more about you. I am... or was the head of the school district's finance department. I might recognize you from your last name. What was it again?"

"Lancer," Ryan responds, the seldomly used word sticking to his tongue like molasses despite being said for the second time in recent memory.

The man scours his vast knowledge of local people but inevitably reaches the same conclusion that Ryan expected: there are no Lancers. As far as Ryan knows, he's never met another person with the same last name as him. To be fair, his network of connections is extremely limited, but beyond his dead mother and absent father, he doesn't even know where his family tree begins. He'd be thoroughly impressed if Gene had even heard the last name before.

"Yeah, doesn't ring a bell," Gene finally determines, "Is your family new to this area?"

Ryan shakes his head, "No, I've been here a couple of years."

Gene studies his quizzically before inspecting his memories one more time. Eventually Chloe swoops in with the cleanest answer.

"Ryan was a foster child," she explains, "That's why you don't know the last name."

This seemingly simple answer, appears to resolve any subsequent questions on Gene's part as he just nods his head in understanding. A rather simple gesture to be sure, but a swift one which puts Ryan at ease.

"Well, I don't think it matters," Gene explains as he leans against the wall, "your actions have told me all I need to know about you. To be honest, when I first saw you, I expected you to be more..."

Chloe eyes him like a panther about to pounce if he missteps.

"Like a badass," Charlie jumps in causing his father to snap around.

"Where'd you hear that word? Besides, didn't I tell you to go back to the cafeteria?"

Charlie, realizing he screwed up, ducks his head out of view and rushes down the hall. His father, of course, doesn't chase after, but Ryan gets the feeling that there will be a conversation in a few minutes.

With a sigh, Gene turns back to Ryan, "No, what I was going to say is that I didn't expect you to be so reserved. Don't get me wrong, I appreciate your personality—quite a bit, actually. But, I was worried that my dear Chloe's first choice of a guy would be someone I'd have to put in his place. I'm glad that those worries were unfounded."

Ryan gets the feeling that he should probably say something, but he never does. The lack of response, in a way, seems like it's only proving Gene's point. 

Gene takes a step out of the room and glances down the hallway, "Alright then, I think I'll leave you guys alone for a bit," he takes another step towards the end of the hallway but stops in his tracks and looks back at Ryan, "Oh, one more thing."

Ryan listens intently.

"Take care of my daughter."

As Gene leaves, Ryan turns around and finds Chloe's face stuffed into her knees as she's appears to be a human ball on the chair.

"Are you alright?" he wonders innocently, mostly out of courtesy.

After an extended pause, Chloe takes a deep breath and releases her knees. She then looks up at Ryan, "You can just forget everything they said. They're both always trying to get a rise out of me."

Ryan gives a confused look, "Why?"

Chloe seconds the notion, "What do you mean why?"

Ryan looks back at the vacant doorway before taking a seat back on his bed, "I don't think he said anything too crazy."

Almost dumbfounded, Chloe's mouth hangs open a bit, "What about... you know..." she averts her eyes as she starts to fidget with her fingers, "about how you're... you're... my boyfriend."

Oh that. To be honest, he sort of filtered that world out subconsciously as his pressing questions took up the bulk of his is thought space. Now being confronted with the question directly, the word feels like it caries an immense amount of weight. Ryan and Chloe dating? A beautiful, compassionate, thoughtful girl dating a guy like him. There's no way...

False Humility.

The word arrives uninvited and makes Ryan pause. Is that honestly what he thinks? 

Of all the time's Vincenzo's words could come back to him, of course it has to be now. Is he neglecting his own abilities? His own self-worth? Is he even allowed to be proud of who he is?

He realizes that his thoughts could spiral on this staircase for days and still not find an answer, so his only real option is to make a decision and stick to it. Either accept the premise, delay it, or... or reject it. But which choice is correct?

With the inevitable decision drawing near, he resolves himself to be straightforward. There's no sense in muddling his words and causing more confusion in the future. And so, he looks Chloe straight in the eyes and takes a deep breath. She purses her lips but holds the eye contact, not expecting much at first, but now preparing for something much more defining.

"I..." Ryan starts finding words really hard to speak all of the sudden, "I don't—"

"Hey guys," Max says, suddenly appearing in the doorway of the room. After less than a second of looking between the two people talking, he picks up on the atmosphere surprisingly quick, "Am I interrupting something?"

Chloe immediately shakes her head, "N-no, it's nothing. You're fine."

It's not hard for Ryan to accept the fact that Max is giving him an out. It's even easier to follow that line of thought and relieve himself of the responsibility. But something doesn't sit right with him. Leaving this unresolved. Whether he like it or not, Vincenzo's words continues to haunt his every thought, namely the idea of choice. What will be gained from doing nothing? What does he risk losing?

"Hey Chloe," Ryan comments, cutting Max off from announcing his reason for dropping by, "I won't correct him."

When the words leave his mouth, the weight of them settles in his chest. He doesn't know whether he made the right decision. Maybe he'll regret it tomorrow. Maybe he'll regret it in five minutes. But for once, he isn't making a choice because he's afraid of hurting someone. He's simply choosing not to push Chloe away.

Somewhere in the back of his mind, he wonders whether Steve would've approved.

As Chloe's eyes widen, Ryan turns to Max, "Now what'd you want?"

Max glances between the briefly before lean on the barred frame of the door, "Honestly, I came to check on you... but it seems that job has already been taken care off."

To avoid joining the conversation, Chloe quietly picks up the small paper crane—that she made—and begins to flap it's wings. In the meantime, Max looks down the hall with a subtle smirk.

"Here comes the rest of the paparazzi," he jokes pointing at Ryan, "better watch out, they'll be more insistent than I am."

As anticipated, Blake, Caid, Beth, and even Alison appear just outside his room. They all watch Ryan carefully as he leans back with a sigh, preparing for a lengthy conversation.

"I'm fine guys," he admits trying to be upfront with the situation before anyone can ask any odd questions.

Blake slides past Max and leans against the wall inside the cramped room, "Could you at least explain what happened. You had us all worried last night."

Worried. For some reason, that notion doesn't sit well with him. Sure it's only natural for them to be concerned when someone they know acts out of character, but to be worried? It pokes him in a spot he's never been poked before. Or rather, he's never allowed himself to notice before.

"Well," Ryan begins, captivating his audience as if about to give some grandioso sob story, "it was just a long night. Saving the hostages wasn't easy."

"I think it goes deeper than that," Beth argues, "Before you left, you mentioned Mr. Heimes. Does it have something to do with that?"

Chloe—the only one ignorant of that conversation—perks up at the mention of such a familiar name. After all, up until a month ago, their principal was the subject of countless sarcastic comments. To bring him up now probably felt like a fish out of water.

"No, I never saw him," Ryan admits truthfully, "Instead I met... Someone far worse."

"Who?" Blake presses.

Ryan clenches his fist and looks down. Who does he even talk about? Jimmy Lashing? The creep? Each of them have some relation to everyone here yet the gab in understanding feels like an enormous valley. How is he supposed to even begin elaborating on how a serial killer stayed true to his title and killed over a hundred people or how a mad scientist was experimenting on humans—both in the warehouse and the school. It just... it's all to much to get started.

"Was it Jimmy?" Chloe surmises, which somehow comes as a relief for Ryan. Even if it doesn't explain anything, at the very least it offers him a start.

"Yeah," Ryan sighs, releasing some of the tension in his forearms.

"Who is Jimmy, by the way?" Beth wonders aloud, "You looked like a ghost after Drew didn't recognize him."

Chloe leans forward on her chair, pressing her hands together, "I've met him. He's a deceptive serial killer."

The rest of the room devolves into an understandable silence. Jimmy being a serial killer is old knowledge to Ryan, but to the rest of them, this is likely the first time they've heard of this. He'd surmise that they're probably hesitant to trust her words, but then he nods, everyone collectively agrees to accept the premise.

"A real serial killer?" Max comments as he wipes his hand over his mouth, "Of all the crazy things we've had to deal with."

Blake purses his lips and stares into the corner of the room. He collects his thoughts amidst the silence but soon glances between the two people who have actually met the serial killer, "I take it we should be cautious, but what exactly are we supposed to do? What can we do at this point?"

Chloe looks to Ryan for an answer. Everyone else seconds that opinion.

"Nothing," Ryan finally answers, "He's not targeting any of you so there's nothing you can do."

Beth steps forward and inspects Ryan's exhausted demeanor, "But it sounds like he is targeting you."

Ryan shrugs his shoulders, "There's nothing I can do either... just have to be prepared."

"I don't like that idea," Beth counters.

Any resistance Beth may have been anticipating never shows itself as Ryan remains silent. It's not that he wants to do nothing, but there's simply nothing he knows to do. Jimmy is a person not an event. You can't prepare for a person—especially an unpredictable one—except stay clear from his path. As far as Ryan's concerned, staying put in the prison is the safest way to avoid another confrontation with that sadistic killer. Even that has him worried because Jimmy could so easily impersonate as survivor from the other camps and infiltrate this camp. To put it bluntly, he doesn't have the faintest clue how to handle Jimmy and he needs some space from Drew—even if that's a short sighted decision.

"It sounds like there's really nothing to be done," Caid assumes with his typical casual demeanor as he leans against the wall in the hallway, "We have a security detail for a reason. Stressing about it won't solve anything. Besides, anyone can be a serial killer out there, you've all seen what it's like."

Blake nods his head, "I hate to stay passive, but I agree that we can't really solve anything except be more cautious when we go outside. Mutants aside, humans can be equally if not substantially more dangerous."

"Not to break the train of thought," Beth jumps in as she directs her attention to Ryan, "but you've dodged the question, Ryan. You were majorly spooked yesterday. Are you actually alright?"

Once again all eyes turn towards him. In light of the attention, he opts to lay down on his bed. His hand lands on his jacket and belt and he promptly squeezes down on it. To be honest, he's not sure if he's alright, but at the very least, he has one more decision that he can make. 

"I don't know," he eventually finds himself saying as his forearm covers his eyes, "Right now I'm just feeling... tired I guess."

He hears the chair Chloe was sitting on shift and then go completely silent. Her voice then protrudes through the silence in a soft, reasonable tone, "I think you might just need a bit more rest for now."

Without looking, Ryan feels that his subsequent silence is quietly accepted as an answer all on its own. He hears some shuffling of feet and a few indecipherable whispers and pretty soon he hears nothing. By the time his forearms finally comes off his face, he finds the room completely empty, save the copious amount of decorations he never put up.
