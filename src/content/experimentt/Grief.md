The clouds were utterly boring that day. Half the sky was smothered in a dull gray pillow while the pockets of blue were no more interesting than the monochrome clouds. He honestly tried to look up at them for a time, maybe because he didn't want to look down, or maybe because it reminded him of a time where life wasn't like this. Either way, his attempts at escape collapse before they begin.

The frozen soil is painstakingly difficult to disturb as his dull shovel slams into it over and over. The last time he dug a grave it was falls, the ground was wet, soft, and malleable. This winter dirt makes his body shake with each passing strike of the shovel.

When all is said and done, he completes the process by nailing two small wooden beams together in a perpendicular shape. He then sticks the wooden fixture into the tough soil at the head of the grave. The wooden sign is partially covered by a tree it stands beside, though that same tree shields it from the elements as the trunk naturally curves around a bit. Nothing is written on the wood, but in his mind he decides that it says something hopeful—something worth reading.

He remembers distinctly how he looked at Steve's grave all that time ago. The disturbed soil made him question why he was even here; it made him question everything. What could he have done different? Where did he go wrong? How much of this is his fault? They're all questions that don't have a correct answer—he knows this intellectually—yet those pointless, meandering thoughts and subsequent conjectures continue to float about his mind unopposed. No one corrects the fallacies, no one comforts the worries, he's once again alone—perhaps exactly as he always has been. 

Beth and Chloe stand over the grave with unreadable expressions. He expected both of them to cry, to blame him for everything, heck, he planned on having both of them hating him—even Chloe. This fear is never realized as they both stare at the grave in silence. From the moment they stepped outside and saw what Ryan saw, to the second the cross was stuck into the ground, their voices had become all but mute. Beth ask a few distant questions, but none of those were intended for anyone in attendance.

Chloe stands behind Ryan with her arms wrapped around herself—over a foot of empty space between them. Her eyes routinely gloss over but each time a subtle blink prevents anything from transpiring. As far as he can remember, she never looked at him once throughout this whole process.

"How... How did we get to this?" Beth asks after an eternity of silence. She looks up at Ryan in particular, "What happened before you left?"

Ryan hangs his head. Even if he told the truth, would anyone believe him?

"She tried to kill me," he says slowly as if the words were falling through the cracks of his fingers.

"I see," Beth looks back to the grave but then looks up, "Did you do something for this to happen?"

Ryan closes his eyes with a long breath, "I'm... I'm not sure."

"Okay," Beth concludes.

Their silence persists for a significant while longer. Eventually three figures appear on the horizon and gradually draw near. Upon noticing one another, the three guys hustle back to the homestead.

"We got back to town and there was nobody there!" Max exclaims as if his words were the most important revelation at the moment.

"It's true," Blake agrees, "People were just there a few hours ago, and now it's a ghost town. We couldn't find a clue."

Max inspects the three people standing outside for seemingly no reason, "What's wrong? Why the long faces? ...were my left-overs that bad?"

Beth looks up at him, some sort of restrained anger flaring up in her, "Shut up. Please just... shut up."

Max gives her a sideways look, "What's gotten into you?"

Caid steps over and tries to lay a hand on her shoulder but she abruptly slaps it off and steps away. They never make eye contact.

That's about the time that Blake looks down at the ground and notices the cross, "Wait... where's Alison?"

All three incoming boys exchange glances. Caid is the first one to move, unknowingly stepping closer to the disturbed dirt.

"All four of you were here when we left," he says calmly, "So where is she? Where is Alison?" No one responds right away which causes his eyes to widen and his lip to quiver briefly, "Where's Alison!"

Blake walks right up to Ryan who can't bring himself to match anyone's gazes. He grabs his shoulder and forces him to look up, "Ryan, what happened?"

Reluctantly, Ryan looks up and shutters at the sight of tears welling up in the eyes of Blake's fearful face. Bloody fingers silently crush Blake's head, but after blinking once the hallucination vanishes. 

"She's..." he starts, hiding his face, "she's gone."

Blake shakes his head stiffly, disbelieving every word, "No. No, that's not possible. We were only gone for, what, a couple of hours?"

"Did someone attack?" Max asks looking around the farm with wide eyes and a shaken face.

"No," Beth adds as she cradles her arms, "No one attacked."

"Then what the fuck happened?!" Caid exclaims as he steps into the center of the unorganized congregation, "Where the hell is Alison!"

Seeing as no one is giving a straightforward answer, Blake inspects the grounds on which they stand. He doesn't seem to notice much, but then his gaze falls upon the wooden cross partially concealed by the tree it stands under. Any immediately reaction is delayed as he looks to the disturbed dirt proceeding the wordless sign. His fearful eyes fill with dread as his teeth grind and chin raises. Fear and confusion is suddenly replaced with disbelief and an unapologetic, unrelenting sorrow.

"No..." he mutters, "No, this can't be," he looks back to Ryan, "Tell me I'm wrong."

Max and Caid look to the cross in silence. They each go silent for a long moment.

"Why?" Caid eventually says with an uncannily calm tone, "Why is she already buried?"

"It's because..." Beth tries with no real answer in mind.

"We weren't gone for long!" he shouts as he points towards the ground below him, "Why the hell did you bury her before we even got back?!"

"Because!" Chloe shouts. Everyone stops and looks at her seeing as she had been quiet the whole time, "She killed herself!" she places a hand to her face to conceal a tear dripping down her face, "We couldn't bring ourselves to look... so Ryan buried her."

The whole group goes eerily silent. Caid, Max, and Blake exchange looks briefly, but that doesn't disturb the overwhelming sense of shock that each of them hold tightly too. Ryan clenches his fist and stares back at the grave only to find a three dead bodies lying on the dirt. A quick blink of the eyes erases that vision too. 

“I don’t really know what to say,” Blake break the silence by saying, “...I do know that we can’t just—”

“I swear to God if you tell us to brush this off and keep moving,” Caid interrupts.

Blake looks almost taken aback by the comment but no one else even bats an eye, “I’m just trying to help.”

"She really saved herself," Max comments to himself. Someone looks at him with disgusted but no one criticizes him for it.

A small gust of wind whips through the farmstead causing the poorly secured cross to tip over and lean against the trunk behind it. Maybe Ryan should fix it—he doesn't.

"Back in middle school, I didn’t know Alison all that well," Beth starts, "I remember seeing her sitting by herself and reading a book in the cafeteria. I never would have guessed that she’d join the speech club with me. She wasn’t the best public speaker, but her speeches were always the most thoughtful one’s I’ve ever heard," Beth wipes a tear from her eye, "She always thought a lot harder than the rest of us—got... stuck in her head. I just wish I could hear one more of her speeches."

What follows is long-winded silence. No asks for further clarification. No one points fingers. 

Eventually, the cold weather gets to some of them, and they begin to head inside one at a time. Beth is the first to go with Chloe following closely behind. Max creeps towards the door over the span of five minutes with him only stepping inside when Caid opens the door for him. Blake and Ryan remain for a considerable amount of time as they stare at the dirt in utter silence. That too finds its eventual end when Blake takes his leave and heads for the door. At some point, Ryan finds himself alone on top of the grave—just like that day.

He falls to his knees making indentations in the snow as he looks helplessly at his handiwork. After a blink of his eyes, he suddenly finds dozens... no hundreds of graves scattered across the yard. Names of high schoolers, names of prison camp members, names of people he doesn’t even know. On top of all of those nameless graves is a single tombstone, one he almost forgot existed—his mother's. To think his first encounter with a grave was not due to the apocalypse, but the start of his entire pitiful existence. 

What’s the point to any of this if death is all that awaits them? ...and he may not get even that.

“Hello there,” says a very distinct voice from behind him.

“Pesh…” Ryan says weakly without even looking back.

Pesh doesn't say anything as he inspects the scene, "What this someone close to you?"

“I don’t care anymore. Do whatever you want,” Ryan whispers, completely and utterly defeated.

“I’m afraid it doesn’t work that way,” the experiment comments.

Ryan rotates his head slowly with wide eyes seeing the face of a sympathetic man with dead eyes behind him. His entire body spoke of understanding yet his eyes told him that he had no life left in him. It was a complete duality of compassion and the complete absence of anything. It catches Ryan off guard to say the least.

"I came here to apologize," Pesh says sincerely, not an ounce of deceit in his tone or demeanor.

Ryan goes silent for a moment letting the words soak in, "Why?"

"No experiment takes pleasure in watching the birth of one of our own," Pesh explains, his head twitching as he steps up beside Ryan, "But... we only get what we want by doing what we hate."

Squeezing his eyes shut, Ryan falls forward only catching himself with his forearms on the ground, "But why?"

"I knew the creature who had that power before you. When he wasn't eating, he liked to talk. He told me that this ability of yours is as cursed as they come. That's cheep for an experiment to say, but he had met nearly every experiment alive," Pesh lifts his hand in front of his face and looks over it methodically as if his skin were the most peculiar thing he'd ever seen, "Honestly, I find that hard to believe. My body is like a cancer, rapidly undergoing mitosis at a rate conventionally impossible, yet my cells are killing themselves so quickly I cannot outgrow my current state. The process is excruciating... beyond your comprehension,” Pesh's hand trembles as he struggles to close it, "No experiment enjoys being this way."

"Then why?" Ryan says slowly as he struggles to unclench his teeth and look away from the ground, "Why would he help that monster? Why is any of this happening?"

Pesh’s eyes grow sad and tired, "The nightmare only ends when you concede to the devil. My desire to spare you was strong, but my desperation to escape was stronger."

“What am I even supposed to do?” Ryan asks pathetically.

With a slow shrug, Pesh takes one step away from Ryan, "I don't know the answer to that."

Ryan shakes his head weakly. Everything is exhausting. It feels like everything is cascading on top of itself creating some sort of crushing weight on his shoulders. Every time he thinks about the past he grimaces, every time he dwells on the future he falls into despair, and every time this damn ability returns to his mind he wonders when it will all end.

"How many people have to die," he says slowly to himself, "How many? Before I'll finally be able to save someone?"

Pesh turns and begins walking away. He doesn't get far before the crunching of the snow underfoot stops.

"Ryan, look up," he commands lightly.

Without resistance, Ryan complies and raises his head. What he finds, about a hundred yards beyond the grave is the exact scene Alison had drawn. The barn on one side, the field on the other, between them a featureless gray form stands. His blood runs cold.

S-019 doesn't charge, it doesn't leave, it just stands there. It watches him with its slits for eyes and foreboding presence. The position it resides in feels more like a mockery of a life than a point of intimidation. Ryan wants nothing more than to get revenge for everything that it's done, but his body simply won't move. His knees stay glued to the ground.

"Allow me to give you one piece of advice," Pesh explains carefully as he too watches S-019 stand amidst the scenery, "You'd be wise to keep your friends close, but you'd be a fool to not keep your enemies closer. Doctor Crane may play with your body, but Experiment S plays with your head... I would know."

Ryan looks around slowly to find Pesh drifting away from the scene. He doesn't turn nor look back but instead waves the back of his hand. Pesh's voice is unconventionally cheerful as he chuckles to himself, but underneath is a deeply rooted melancholy, "Maybe if you're better than I... you may escape without falling to hell. And... if you do, let me know if its worth it."

When he checks back on S-019 he finds the experiment gone. The spot between the barn and the field is now empty with only memories to reserve that space.

“Are you going to come inside?”

Ryan stands up slowly and turns to face Blake. He can’t even look him in the eyes though he knows he's being interrogated with concern.

"Yeah... probably should."

“It’s not your fault, you know,” Blake says, hiding any personal hurt he may feel. “It’s none of our faults and all of ours at the same time.”

“Isn’t this where you say that we need to live because of other people and not in spite of them,” Ryan says lightly believing that lie might lighten his own burdens.

Blake shakes his head, “I tried to think like that in the past and look where it’s gotten us.”

Ryan goes silent.

After a moment of hesitation, Blake shifts and slides his hands into his pockets, "You know. Since we couldn't find anyone in the city, maybe it'll be alright to go try that restaurant idea. Normally I'd say no but... hell I think we can make an exception for today. What do you say?"

Looking up, he finds a restrained, hopeful expression on his friends face. It's clear that this idea is not something he particularly wants, but maybe it's something they all need. Whether that's true or not doesn't matter—it's Blake's way of trying. 

"Okay," Ryan says slowly, "Let's go."

---

Convincing the group to go along with this plan was about the most trivial endeavor ever partaken as everyone unanimously agreed, almost instantly. Beth and Max gathered cooking ingredients. Blake and Ryan grabbed the generator and stowed it in the back of the truck. Caid ran off somewhere to grab something of importance, though he wouldn't say. Lastly, Chloe ensured all the doors and windows were locked shut to ward off any unwanted company while they would be gone.

Not long after that they were on the road.
 
The restaurant they selected was on the far corner of the block from the museum. Blake was a bit hesitant to get so close, but he didn't put up a fight like he usually did, and they took to the location without much issue. Max sprinted to the kitchen to investigate what he had to work with while the rest of them relaxed in a booth. At some point they connected the generator in such a way to allow Max to do his thing, though Ryan wasn't part of that so he's not really sure how it worked.

Now all five of them—minus the cook—find themselves seated around the booth with long faces. Despite a six-inch gap between them, Chloe deliberated elected to sit next to him which put Ryan at ease—even if only a little. 

"So Caid," Beth asks in almost an ice-breaker way, "why weren't you helping us earlier?"

A smirk crosses the guys face as he reaches over the back of the booth and retrieves a bag he placed there. Inside the small black bag is a glass container with a liquid sloshing around as he unzips it. Subsequently, he reveals a wine-looking bottle with a clear liquid inside.

"Is that..." Blake trails off.

Caid shrugs, "It was supposed to be a surprise for a birthday or something, but I found it a few weeks ago... it's Everclear."

Beth's jaw crops, "Isn't that like, super strong?"

Caid nods, "It is... and I'm willing to share it."

Blake narrows his eyes as he leans against the table, "Are you proposing that we drink our situation away?" Caid gulps hesitantly as Blake smiles, "I better get the first drink."

Everyone stares at Blake, a bit confused, as he looks around indifferently. 

"I guess that's that," Beth agrees as she looks directly at Caid, "Though after dinner."

Caid nods, "Of course."

"Um..." Chloe hesitates, drawing the attention of the conversation, "Is it too late to ask how strong that is? I'm not good with alcohol."

Blake, Caid, and Beth all laugh. Ryan smiles. 

Caid waves his hand dismissively, "It's fine, you don't have to drink it, but know that it's really strong."

Chloe immediately shakes her head, "You know what, it's fine. I'll try it anyway."

"At it girl," Beth supports as she humorously pats her on the shoulder.

Shifting her shoulders forward, Ryan catches a glance of a knowing smile cross Chloe's face. Maybe he's just seeing things, or maybe she too is doing her best to alleviate the tension in the room. Her demeanor then shifts, and suddenly she's looking at him. Their eyes meet. Ryan looks away.

"So I've been—err—thinking," Blake says a bit awkwardly as he leans back in the booth seat, "Isn't it great that we didn't have to sit through one of Dr. Swanson's boring speeches. You know, since we didn't graduate, we lucked out."

Ryan gets the feeling that Blake's treading on dangerous territory, but he's not one to resist such things. Instead, the concern turns towards the group and how they will respond. Caid is the first to answer the call.

Caid widens his eyes, "Are you kidding me? That man could put a fly to sleep. If the whole crowd wasn't passed out by the time he finished his introduction, I would have... honestly I would have been asleep too."

Beth laughs, "Tell me about it. I never had him for a class, but I've only ever heard horror stories. How can one guy be so monotone and so oblivious?"

Chloe leans forward on the table, "I heard that he does it intentionally. He wants to know who's just sitting through class and who's learning from class."

Blake nods, "Yeah, I heard that too. It still didn't change the fact that I nearly passed out during the lecture on molecular terminology."

"Hey, I found that one interesting!" Beth counters.

Blake gestures at her in an accusatory manner, "You just said that you've never had him as a teacher."

Caid reaches over and starts rubbing the back of Beth's shoulder. The action catches her off-guard momentarily, but she settles into it rather quickly.

"Don't fault her for actually enjoying school," Caid debates with a diplomatic look in his eyes.

Blake throws his hands off to the sides, "What?! I did like school. I just struggled through his class."

"Okay straight A student," Caid says dismissively.

Blake crosses his arms, holding back a smirk, "I didn't get all A's... I actually got one B."

The whole table looks at him in shook, though Ryan suspects a bit of it is performative in nature. That's not to say he finds it unwelcome.

"Blake..." Chloe thinks, "I always thought you were the smart guy of the group?"

A few people chuckle as Blake shrugs his shoulders, "What can I say? I'm still human."

The conversation stalls briefly as a few of them start to look around at nothing in particular. As he carefully inspects the surroundings—not really expecting anything to stick out—he notices Chloe in his peripheral vision. In an effort not to tip her off, he decides to stare at a particular poster above them but really he's watching her. She glances over at him quickly but looks away just as soon. Her hands come together, and she begins fiddling with a bracelet as she deliberately looks around the room with a forced smile. No one seems to notice—except Ryan.

Blake closes his eyes for a bit and just takes long, slow breaths. It almost seems like some sort of meditation technique though he doesn't make it very conspicuous. Honestly, it's probably just him relaxing and feeling a bit drowsy. Nothing more.

Caid watches the doors to the kitchen expectantly with one arm resting on the back of his seat while the other falls underneath the table. Judging by the angle of his arm, Ryan's willing to bet he's hold Beth's hand. Beth looks nervously at the table as she checks in on Caid and the kitchen every now and then.

"I worked at this fast food place on the weekends," Caid begins as he taps his finger on the side of the booth wall. The whole group rejoins a conversational dynamic almost instantly, "It was just down the street from my flat—we actually passed it that one time. Anyway, I basically ran the joint, so I knew how everything operated. I'm looking at this restaurant and for some reason I'm wondering how different it is from that," he turns to the group, "Any of you have more high quality food service experience—I think fast food is as low as it gets."

"I do," Beth chimes in, "I just started the job, so I don't have much experience, but I worked as a waitress at the pancake place on Elm Street. Honestly I only got four shifts in before, you know, so I can't say much. I will admit that it felt like fast food half the time. Get food here quickly—remember that order—don't forget this—lousy customers—the whole shebang."

Chloe looks at her knowingly, "And who go you that job?"

Beth rolls her eyes, "Chloe—my master—you got me the job."

Chloe giggles, "I'm only kidding. I didn't work there long either. I think the owner just preferred hiring high school girls for his staff."

Beth shivers, "Don't remind me. If it weren't for you and my need for money, I never would have agreed to work there."

"I guess if we're talking about first jobs, I shadowed my dad every once in a while," Blake explains casually.

Beth gives him a careful look, "Wasn't your dad a doctor or something?"

Blake nods his head.

"So were you planning on med school?"

The question takes Blake a moment to process. Once he does sort through whatever he was thinking about he shrugs his shoulders, "I hadn't decided. I know my dad would have supported it, but I wasn't sure. I planned on getting an undergraduate degree in Bio or Chem, but after that... I can't say."

"Bio?" Caid points out incredulously, "Didn't you struggle through Dr. Swanson's Bio lecture?"

Blake waves his hands again, "Okay, that's not fair. I still got a ninety on the first test."

"Oh, how formidable," Beth says mockingly.

The room goes quiet for a moment, but then everyone bursts out into laughter. It isn't the haphazard sort of laughter that stupid boys have at a lunch table, it's a much more intimate laughter—one that feels genuine and earned. At least Ryan thinks of it that way.

"What about you?" Blake asks directing the attention of the group towards Ryan, "What was your first job?"

Ryan finds himself at a loss for words almost instantly. The truth of the matter is that he's never had a job before, not even close to one. Admitting that isn't the problem as being unemployed is supposed to be typical for a high schooler, the problem is that everyone leading up to him had some cool story to go with their experience. The only story Ryan has is bad foster families, running away, and four years of high school silence. At this point he's starting to move on from that, but trying to explain might bring the mood down. That's the last thing he wants to do.

"I... uh," he mutters, "I never worked."

"Oh, cause you were a foster kid," Caid points out only to get a stern look from Beth, "Oh... was I not supposed to say that?"

Before anyone can question him—save the people who already knew—Ryan decides to face the comment head on, "It's fine... I guess while we're recounting stories, I do have one fun memory." The whole table leans in. Ryan takes a deep breath, "I remember this one time late at night when I was down town. I was walking by this bar or club of some sort with a really fancy limo out front. Anyway, outcomes this drug guy who can barely stand. He crosses in front of me and tries to open the limo door. He fails. Four times. Honestly, I was about to leave, but I did feel bad, so I turned back and opened the door for him." the table leans in further, awaiting the resolution eagerly, Ryan shifts uncomfortably, "He looked me over _reeking_ of alcohol before he hands me a twenty and says: 'Rob you're not on duty this late'."

A few people chuckle at the recounting while Caid actually laughs. All the while Ryan just wonders whether he told the story right or now... at least that's what he thinks initially. As the group laughs, he decides it doesn't matter and laughs with them.

Not long after that, Max appears from the kitchen with three trays of stir-fried rabbit, venison stew, and skewered vegetables. He, of course, gives them a drawn out introduction to the food with flamboyant vocabulary, but Caid ends it early by grabbing a skewer and digging in. That small action kickstarts the feast.

Ryan can't deny it, Max's cooking is genuinely the best he's ever had. To be fair, he's only been to a real restaurant on very seldom occasions, but even with those memories, he still would prefer this feast over any of that. The meat is rich in flavor—the vegetables too. To be frank, he doesn't know how Max does it, but he truly has a gift. Now if only the guy didn't ask for people to complement his work.

When the food is scrapped clean, Caid disappears into the kitchen before returning with glass cups. Placing all six glasses in a row, he proceeds to pour out his special booze proportionally into each cup. He passes them out one at a time but everyone waits to take the first drink. Finally, holding his own glass above the table he looks around at the whole group.

"This one's for Alison," he says, the words struggling to come out, "And where she'd want us to go."

Everyone else raises their glasses towards Caid's. Ryan follows suit.

"Cheers!" they all exclaim, Ryan lagging behind but managing to sneak his contribution in. 

After that, they all down their alcohol... or rather, attempt to down their alcohol. Except for Caid, everyone stops drinking after the first five or so drops hit their tongue. Chloe, Blake, and Max start coughing while Beth stares at the liquid like it were lighter fluid. Ryan too can't bear the taste as his throat burns upon the liquid passing and his nose fizzles like he just drank the most carbonated drink ever. 

"Holy hell that burns," Max complains as he promptly takes another sip, "It's awful," he drinks some more, "I hate this," another drink, "Why does it taste like that!"

Caid grabs the glass out of Max's hands and sets it on the table. Instead of resenting the deprival of the potent concoction, Max falls forward and sprawls his arms across the table.

"I can't drink anymore," he comments as he closes his eyes.

"My gosh," Caid says rolling his eyes, "It's not that bad." Caid immediately follows up by taking a sizable drink of his glass and grimacing. 

The aftertaste stings Ryan's mouth as he waits for anyone other than Max to give their opinion on the matter. For his very first liquor, he gets the feeling that this is... nonstandard. Then again, he wouldn't know. Despite being around alcohol quite extensively with Steve's palette, he never considered trying any of it. It doesn't help that he can feel his cheeks growing hot just smelling the pungent odor.

Blake takes a considerable drink—that being a third of the cup—and nearly slams the cup on the table, "Damn I needed this."

Beth raises an eyebrow, "Wow Blake, it's not like you to let loose like this."

Give her a glare, Blake selects a leftover crump from his plate and tosses it into his mouth, "What's that supposed to mean?"

"She means you're detailed oriented," Chloe adds, "So much so that it's nice to see you relax once in a while."

Blake rolls his eyes and leans back with his glass in hand, "I can relax."

"Sure, s-sure," Max slurs, giving into the drunkenness a little too easy—it's hardly been sixty seconds since he downed most of his glass.

"Bro, why'd we even give you alcohol?" Caid comments as he shoves Max's conspicuously approaching body rolling towards him.

"It's to make us feel better," Max decides as he haphazardly returns to a seated position.

Looking around at all of their glasses, Ryan supposed the remainder of liquid in each closely aligns with their personality. Max, obviously, is almost done, Caid is close behind, and everyone else falls closely underneath. Ryan's cup is largely untouched as he only took the initial cheers drink and abstained since then. He's not scarred or anything, he simply doesn't like the idea of loosing himself. In recent memory, he's lost himself enough, he doesn't want to mechanically invoke such feelings... it unsettles him.

Chloe glances over at him while he quietly contemplates unnecessary details and she smiles at him cheekily, "You don't seem to like it."

Ryan shrugs, "It's fine."

Giggling lightly, she holds out her drink, "I hate it too. So what do you think about saying YOLO together. You in?"

He's about to question what that's supposed to mean but then he finds her cup raised off the table and only one conclusion makes sense in his mind. Does he want to do it? No. Does he want to do it for Chloe? ...well maybe. Does he want to escape? ...there's only one way to tell.

Without further ado, he nods and they both lift their drinks. The fowl, despicable liquid goes down hard and the next forty five minutes result in a bit of blur.

---

He never lost consciousness and, incidentally, remembers everything that happened rather vividly, yet the experience itself felt rather jumbled. Max absolutely tried to climb on the table. Caid certainly knocked him onto his back. He remembers distinctly how Blake nearly cried about his dad leaving with him. It's impossible to forget how Beth threw up into a bowl. And although he can't say it confidently, he's sure that no one brought up Alison—not even once. All of these details are as clear as day but he never remembers actually being there, he just... remembers them.

Now he finds himself seated in a separate booth across the room with Chloe nodding off next to him. They came over here because Max returned to the kitchen and never came back. Blake feel asleep and the other two started hardcore flirting. Incidentally, that put the last two people conscious somewhere else. Though, if he's being honest, Beth and Caid are probably already asleep as he stopped hearing drunken comments a couple of minutes ago.

Looking at Chloe's eyes flutter closed only to crack open feels oddly satisfying to Ryan. She is trying desperately to stay awake yet the dark room, the long since set sun, and the overarching alcohol making it near impossible to say awake long. Ryan too is feeling the effects, though he's more of the night owl sort to begin with so staying awake in the dark is second nature to him. That being said, he knows he won't be last long.

"It's okay to sleep," Ryan whispers gently as Chloe's head bobs back and forth slightly.

She glances over quite drowsily and shakes her head, "No... I can't do that."

"Why not?" Ryan wonders seeing as she's barely warding off sleep as is.

"Because..." she offers slowly, her hand drifting towards him, though it never makes it there, "I'm just afraid is all."

"Afraid?" Ryan repeats, "I guess I understand. I can't blame you."

The talking seems to wake her a little as her eyes open a bit wider than they were before, "Are you not?"

Ryan shrugs as he slouches in the booth a little, "No... I'm—I'm terrified. It's just that... that word doesn't feel the same anymore."

Chloe's eyes soften as she reaches a bit further but then closers her fist and withdraws her hand. Not wanting to push anything—especially given that he can feel his hands twitching from the earlier drinks—he elects to watch in *silence*.

As the conversation lulls, Ryan's attention wanders out the dark window. They don't have any lights on, but he can't help but feel like something might be out there watching him. The unwanted ponderance frightens and exhausts him. In response, he hangs his head a bit.

"Ryan..." Chloe practically whispers, "You're not... going anywhere. Right?"

He looks over slowly, her face mostly concealed by darkness, "No."

She suddenly goes uncomfortably quite and still. He honestly wonders if she spontaneously feel asleep mid conversation, but then he notices something unexpected. Her cheek, a mere silhouette in the dark, releases a round droplet the descends quickly to her lap. She doesn't whimper, she doesn't sniffle, she makes absolutely no sounds whatsoever.

"Chloe?" he whispers, just loud enough to be heard.

She shakes her head, "I don't want to be left alone... I can't take it anymore."

Ryan stares in utter disbelief. It's not that he doesn't understand, he simply can't process what to do. What does he do?

This question is quickly answered on its own as Chloe reaches out for his hand but struggles to find it as he's already removed it from the table. When her attempt at physical contact fails she nearly withdraws again. 

Maybe it's the alcohol taking control, or maybe this is what he wanted to do all along, but he makes the decision regardless. With one fell swoop he grabs onto her back, pulls her towards him, and wraps her in an embrace. She hesitates to react, but eventually she hugs back. Although as silent as before, he can feel his shirt beginning to grow damp. 

He strikes her hair slowly, "It's okay... I promise I won't leave."

Whether he can hold up that statement or not is hardly important in his mind—it's simply what he wanted to say. His entire life has been a collection running, avoiding, and wishing it were over. This is the first time he's ever said, thought, or believed that opposite. It doesn't matter whether it's the truth or a cruel lie. He wants to protect all his friends, but he couldn't bear to lose her.
