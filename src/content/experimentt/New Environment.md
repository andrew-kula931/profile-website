The creaky, old cellar door, covered in snow and undisturbed for months, suddenly shifts quickly and flies open, throwing the thin layer of powdered snow off creating a small cloud of icy particles. Ryan steps up the stairs from the cellar and looks around at the frigid January afternoon. The leafless trees around the farmhouse retain a small layer of snow on each branch outlining the beautiful design of each dormant plant. Snow covers the landscape all the way to the horizon creating a tranquil environment without so much as a shallow breeze to disturb it. The icy air causes Ryan’s breath to puff out in a small white cloud as he slowly exhales. His winter coat, scarf, and hat all keep him warm for the moment but Jack Frost still nips at his nose as cold air fills his lungs. 

He holds up his right arm. Beneath layers of clothing he can feel the old injury healed up, though not without some pretty significant scarring. Max told him it looks cool, but he's not particularly in favor of that opinion. He sometimes wishes those scares weren't there.

“I think we’ve searched through everything,” Blake says as he walks towards the stairs from the cellar below carrying a backpack clanking along as the contents inside collide with one another.

Ryan looks back at him into the dark basement, “Perfect, let’s head back then. I’m sure Max is cooking something good for dinner.”

“Right,” Blake nods as the two of them exit the vacant farm house and begin heading down the road leaving only their footsteps in the snow behind.

It’s been nearly two months since Heimes died and they were left to fend for themselves. They originally planned on leaving the city to find another group to survive with but heavy snow slowed their efforts a few weeks after leaving and then the bitter cold and lack of fuel for their vehicles stopped their efforts entirely. They managed to make it a few towns down but everywhere they went they always found the same thing: deserted towns with only zombies and mutants remaining. There were plenty of traces of surviving groups but each of their camps were either abandoned or worse. 

Because of their lack of progress in finding others, morale turned sharply to an all time low. Their mentality was already struggling to begin with, but the despair of being completely alone really started to sink in by that point. No one took it well, but some took it worse than others.

Nowadays, Blake and Ryan take care of any looting of the surrounding areas. It wasn't really much a decision but a necessity when their rations ran low. Originally all seven of them were scouting out through buildings to retrieve any cans of mush or bags of stale food, but after the second encounter with a mutant, they decided it be better if just Ryan and Blake did it. Surprisingly, it wasn't a capability thing that brought on that determination, instead it was simply that Blake and Ryan were the only ones willing to do it. The girls were tired of walking into rooms covered in blood, stinking of a pungent decaying odor, and threatening them with a volatile groan—honestly he couldn't blame them. Caid and Max probably could have been convinced, but they had other ideas instead.

On the topic of Caid and Max, they initial didn't know how to contribute—other than Max cooking the meals—but they eventually did stumble on a good idea. By stumble, they literally stumbled over it as they took up the age old strategy of hunting after Max tripped over a raccoon carcass. This revelation kickstarted their desire to wake up at five in the morning, head out to the nearby woods, and pray they stumbled upon a live deer. For six days they attempted it and came back empty handed, but on the seven day they took one down. That buck fed the whole group for over four days which only made their hunting excisions a routine effort.

The girls—Beth and Chloe mostly with Alison on occasion—would do the secondary search through houses. After Ryan and Blake cleared out a house and took anything that stood out to them, the girls would come through and pick the place clean of anything they could need or want. Honestly, they often brought back more than necessary, but no one complained when a beautiful painting appeared on the dining room wall or a new spatula entered the kitchen inventory.

Overall, they were all fairing pretty well, but they knew this was only a temporary solution. Their gasoline was scarce and the looting spots were growing further and further away. By Blake's speculation, they could probably only keep up this lifestyle for another two or three months. Either way, they were just content to have life slow down for a while.

“I’ve been putting some thought into how we should exterminate the zombies in the museum,” Blake says as he adjusts the bag on his back, his nose red and his breath steadily pushing out white clouds.

“Okay, I still find it weird that a small town like this has a museum, but I digress. What did you have in mind?” Ryan lifts his legs up a bit higher than usual as they wade through a tall snow drift.

“I was thinking,” Blake explains as he nearly loses his balance as his snow boot sinks deep within the drift, the snow nearly reaching the top of his knee, “Uhg… I was thinking that we should light the building on fire, that seems to be their weakness after all. The museum is mostly wooden and if we cause a commotion, we might even draw some more in.”

“It could work,” Ryan thinks aloud.

“Yeah,” Blake agrees as they both arrive at a broken bridge, "I still need to think through how we start the fire. That is the bottleneck."

"Sounds good," Ryan says.

The broken bridge before them used to be a simple wooden bridge to allow a single car to pass, but after years of neglect it simply couldn’t hold the heavy snow and bits of it collapsed. Just enough to make walking over it impossible. 

Instead, the two young men head down to the frozen creek to cross. Ryan carefully hops on a large stone and then jumps the rest of the way not wanting to risk stepping on the ice and falling through. Blake follows suit before they successfully make it to the opposing side and trek their way back up to the road. A few minutes later, they find themself back at base in a different farm house just outside of town.

Their base used to be an apple orchard sitting on the outskirts of some unnamed town—it wasn't really unnamed, they simply couldn't find a sign. The orchard is just far enough not to receive any visitors but also near enough to walk to town if the need arises. The same cars they took from the car dealership sit outside the place buried in a bit of snow. The truck works great in the winter conditions but the other three struggle under any patch of ice and ultimately was part of the reason they stopped traveling and called it a day in this farmhouse. Thankfully, their little retreat center comes fully equipped with a wood burning fireplace, a second story, and a basement. Caid and Blake even managed to hook up a generator they found to work the stove when Max really wants it. They’ve only been here for a few weeks, but it already has started to feel like home. 

Blake pushes open the front door and the two snow covered men enter into their warm, welcoming humble abode. Their entire group arrives at the entrance to greet them as their welcoming party.

“Get anything good?” Beth asks, wiping something off her hands.

Blake takes the bag off his back and hands it over to Beth, “Thank you quartermaster. My job is complete, now it’s your turn. Let me know if you need any help.”

“Don’t worry,” Caid says, taking the bag before Beth and grabbing it, “We won’t let a single thing go uncounted.”

Beth briefly places her hands on her hips as she looks offended but then smiles as she and Caid walk into the kitchen together.

Chloe steps up and retrieves Ryan’s backpack as he leans over to take his snowy boots off. They don't need to exchange any pleasantries as she brings it over to Beth for counting.

“Still vacant out there?” Max asks as Ryan finishes taking off his boots and begins to remove his gloves and coat.

"Yeah," Ryan responds, "The mutants already seemed to like the indoors. The cold just makes it necessary."

“I see,” Max sighs, "Welp, there's always a silver lining. Because it's so cold, we can walk around outside just like we used to."

“Yeah,” Ryan says simply.

Max shrugs his shoulders, but then stops as if the most marvelous idea had just spontaneously dawned on him, "Wait. We can go sledding."

"Only if I get to push you!" Caid calls out from the other room.

Max rolls his eyes as he and Ryan enter into the dining room where everyone else has congregated. Counting the supplies that were retrieved has become a sort of spectacle for them these days. As such, Beth has since turned it into a bit of a game just for fun.

Beth reaches into Blake's bag and grabs onto some item without revealing it to the crowd. She glances around with a keen eye and a smirk, "Any guess on the retail price?"

"Six ninety-nine!" Chloe exclaims, her excitement most born out of a light-hearted humor.

"Ten," Caid guesses as he nearly falls backwards in the chair he's leaning on.

"Priceless!" Max asserts very confident.

Beth gives the latter boy a knowing look, "You can't say priceless to everything. 'Priceless' is subjective."

Max crosses his arms, "And this is a subjective economy. Therefore, I'm always right."

Beth rolls her eyes, "Whatever you say," she returns to the game at hand, "drum roll please."

Blake, Caid, and Chloe begin hitting the dinner table lightly.

As Beth pulls the item out of the bag the drum roll stops and every watches the reveal very carefully. The content of Beth's hands is a family sized bag of potato chips, namely the barbeque kind. 

"Six dollars, Chloe's the closest!" Beth announces as Chloe shakes her hand with excitement and gleefully accepts the bag out of Beth's hands. 

Despite appearances, she doesn't actually get to keep the valuable junk food, she just gets to hold on to it until the end of the game. This is reinforced as she places the bag in front of her to start her pile of winnings.

"Darn," Caid comments, "I need to stop guessing so high."

"At least you're not guessing priceless," Beth comments, shooting Max a stray glance.

Ryan looks around the room and spots all of his friends, save one. The missing party is much of a surprise, but he does feel so inclined to say something, “Where’s Alison at?”

“She’s up in her room,” Chloe explains, “I just checked on her a few minutes ago. She said that she’s waiting for the stars to come out so she can see the constellations,” Chloe walks over to the kitchen door, “The stars really are beautiful around here.”

"Of course they are," Max says matter-of-factly, "It's because there's no light pollution."

"But there's still Max's failed experiment pollution," Caid comments dryly.

Max raises his chin and glares at the boy seated in a chair, "Very funny. That's probably the funniest joke I've ever heard. Truly hilarious."

Beth rolls her eyes as she sticks her hands back in the bag, "Cut it out you guys, it's time for the next round of guesses."

Ryan glances around the room one last time before departing for the living room. The burning fireplace casts a warm glow across the room as he take a seat in a large lazy boy before leaning back letting the chair rock back and forth slightly. His eyes rest on the fireplace and the black TV screen above it. There is no power or service to watch anything, but sometimes he likes to imagine there’s a reality show on retelling what life was like before all of this. 

Ryan kicks the recliner up letting his legs rest in the up position as his eyelids slowly drift closed. They have enjoyed a good spell of peace, but how long will it last? How long before something bad happens again? Only time will tell.

_Ryan’s eyes open up to view the dim light trickling through the waves as he floats in the open ocean. The sun light shakes as the water refracts its creating a beautiful flurry of light carefully shifting with the waves above. Despite the water all around him, he breathes normally and the temperature of the water isn’t even noticeable. This white and blue abyss is like a tranquil grassland letting him float in his own thoughts and nothing else. Without a care in the world. Without a family to protect. Without people to disappoint. This is like paradise._

_Then his body slowly begins to rotate away from the sparkling light above and slowly turning to the dark abyss below. At first he sees only the dark nightmare of the unknown but then a faint glow appears far below. His eyes quickly adjust to the light to find more and more speckles of light appearing. At first he thinks they’re sea pickles or something like that, but then the lights become more vivid and the form they create becomes clear._

_He's no longer floating in the ocean but standing in front of a door—a long forgotten door. Instinctively he turns back to find a much more familiar kitchen and fridge decorated with crayon-drawn animals. The kitchen is warm and welcoming while the door before him is dark and foreboding. What lies on the other side, he can only assume._

_He steps up to the door and reaches out for it. On the other side is his mother, the only person he's truly ever trusted. If he can just see her one more time—just one more time._

_His fingers make contact with the cold wooden surface and right as they do, he hears his name. The sounds that make up his name are uncannily clear as if they were being said directly into his ear. Pushing that aside, he begins to press the door open only for his name to be repeated._

_Growing a bit impatient, he pushes the door harder this time. Just a little more and he can see his mother. The door swings further and further open with his mother's decoration revealing themselves with each passing moment. Then, right before the bed becomes visible, everything fades away._

Ryan's eyes open slowly only to find Chloe softly nudging his shoulder to wake him up. She smiles as he looks at her, still trying to regain his senses. Her welcoming blue eyes and smooth dimples calming any nerves that his dream may have given him.

“The foods ready,” she says, walking off towards the kitchen.

He slowly closes the recliner and pushes himself out of the lazy boy. From there he makes his way through the kitchen and into the dining room to find everyone sitting around the dinner table with bowls and utensils at the ready. A pot of soup sits in the center of the table with smaller pans of corn or any other canned food Max cooked up tonight. Everyone, even Alison, waits patiently as Ryan takes his usual seat at the end of the table across from Max himself.

“Alright,” Max announces, “I made this dinner with love and grace so that our hungry stomachs and tired bodies could relish the sweet nectar of elegant cuisine. The soup contains bits of dried venison and canned potatoes. The broth—”

“That’s enough,” Caid interrupts, “Let’s dig in already.”

With that everyone breaks out into an organized rhythm of dipping food for themself and passing the ladle to the next person. It’s a familiar rhythm they have built after spending nearly two months together with only each other to keep them company. It really is starting to feel like they are a family braving the winter together. Ryan wonders if this is what a real family feels like. He wonders if this is what it would look like if the Hughes had made it to Bill’s family farm and sat together awaiting the end of the apocalypse. 

Ryan shakes his head getting rid of the thought as he digs into his portion of soup. The aroma is fairly good for subpar ingredients and the taste surprisingly strong. Ryan has to give it to Max that he really knows how to cook. 

“Is this how you got so many girls back in high school?” Blake asks as he collects another mouthful of soup with his spoon.

Max rolls his eyes, “Being a good cook is not my calling card, it’s a bonus.”

“Even if it's a bonus,” Beth adds, “I’d date a cook this good any day.”

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Caid argues slightly offended.

Beth throws a hand up to calm him down, “Relax, I’m not pointing fingers. I’m just saying that I’m dumping you for Max now.”

“Bet,” Max calls out excitedly.

“No way!” Caid exclaims nearly on his feet.

Beth laughs and covers her mouth with one hand to prevent any food in her mouth from leaking out. “You’re so gullible.” She then swallows the food in her mouth and leans over to give Caid a kiss on the cheek. This action calms Caid completely.

“So, Ryan? Did you find anything special today?” Chloe asks, folding her hands on her lap, "Anything you didn't bring home?"

“Just the usual,” he says, simply taking another spoonful of his soup.

“What about that one shed,” Blake points out.

“Oh right,” Ryan agrees, “We found this one butcher shed.”

He receives a few confused or slightly startled expressions by his comment. All of them, however, lean forward a bit as he prepares to tell a story from the day. He can feel the courage building up inside of him, but then it hits a wall and backs away. Seeing as the potential tale is fading from his mind, he turns back to Blake, "You were there, why don't you tell it."

Blake shrugs his shoulders and puts his spoon down, “It’s not all that special honestly, but dried blood littered this one table with frozen pig guts all around. In the back room there were even some frozen butchered pigs, but unfortunately the wolves got to it before us so mostly everything was torn clean.”

“That’s dumb,” Max complains, “I’d kill for some good pork to cook with.”

“Oh shut up,” Caid says lightly, "We've got plenty of venison."

“So what? Pork is so much better tasting and easier to cook than deer. I’m just saying that I want to cook something else rather than soup and some side dishes.”

"You know full well that we can't do much about that," Caid argues with a slightly rising tone. 

"Maybe we just have to go deeper into town to get things" Max expresses, matching Caid’s dismissive tone.

Caid glances over at Blake, "Yeah, why don't you go deeper into the city?"

"It's dangerous," Blake responds in his practiced manner.

"Oh come on," Max complains leaning forward on the table, "Pretty please, just get a few more things. For me."

"No," Blake asserts, "Not until the coast is clear. There's a whole hive in the museum."

Max waves his hands to his sides, "I don't see the problem. Just don't go into the museum."

Blake shakes his head, "I don't want anyone getting close."

"Come on!" Max exclaims with a bit more attitude this time.

"Yeah, you said it," Caid adds, "Maybe it's time we trade jobs."

"We're not trading jobs," Blake directs as he takes a spoonful of soup.

"Please!" Max complains again.

"No!" Blake argues with an irritated tone.

Max places both hands on the table, "It's not fair!"

"I said no!" Blake asserts with rising authority.

“Please stop,” Alison says softly as she hunches her shoulders forward making herself look smaller.

The table goes quiet for a moment as they realize they crossed the line. It's an unofficial rule, but they never fight. Both for the logistical complications and the... the more psychological ones.

Blake looks back at Max and lowers his head a bit, “Sorry, I didn’t mean to raise my voice.”

“Nah,” Max responds, “It’s cool. I get it, we’re all trying our best.”

“Well if it makes you feel any better,” Blake begins, "Ryan and I do plan on clearing out that museum. Hopefully soon."

Everyone at the table perks up, “Really?!” Beth says first.

"That's right," Blake responds pointing his spoon at Ryan, "I have an idea to exterminate that place."

The meal ends shortly after that and with the sun going down many of the group decide to call it a day and head to bed. 

Ryan walks up the stairs to his room saying good night to Caid, Beth, and Blake who still remain in the living room chatting before the fire. He arrives at his room door and pushes it open to find Chloe sitting next to the window looking up at the stars. Ryan can almost see the reflection of the constellations off her eyes. She has on old pajamas that she likely found in the closet meant for someone living in the sixties, but it fit her perfectly and even looked a bit sexy, though he'd never say that aloud. She notices the sound of the door opening and glances over.

Their room is some sort of guest room with only a bed, a wardrobe, a table and a single chair. A lamp sits on the table but it has been off for a long time now. A painting of the farmland hangs on the wall across from the bed but other than that, the room is fairly barren... or at least it was initially barren. Chloe has since customized the room with a couple posters, some origami for the table, fake flowers, a cute little beanbag chair to read, and a much of other miscellaneous things that he can't be bothered to create a name for.

Ryan heads over to the bed and sits down stretching his arms out. Without a word, Chloe walks over and sits down next to him hugging him. His arms sink down while one of them wraps around Chloe’s shoulders.

“Missed me that much?” Ryan asks rhetorically.

“I always do,” she says playfully.

"Sometimes it sounds like you're my wife," Ryan categorizes very analytically in his mind, thought Chloe doesn't seem to take it that way.

"Is that so?" she ask, sliding up to look Ryan in the eyes, her eyelashes low as she stares at him almost seductively, "Are you happy with this wife?"

"No," Ryan responds quickly which causes Chloe to do a double take, "I'm..." he searches for the right word, "Enamored with my wife."

Chloe raises an eyebrow, "That's one way to say it."

If there's one thing that Ryan is willing to admit before anything else, it's that he finds conversation with Chloe far easier than with anyone else. He's never been in a relationship before where he feels comfortable saying the first thing that comes to his mind, but if there ever were one, it'd be this one. She always seems to bounce off his comments so easily and he does the same. In a way, it's the sort of interactions that he notices Caid and Max always having—one person makes a suggestive or unnecessary comment and the other just rides it into another partially related idea. The flow just works and neither party seems to contemplate it any more than face value. Despite his unsuccessful attempts with his other friends, Chloe's the only one he's gotten to this point with.

Chloe disengages from the side embrace and claps her hands together with an excited look, "You'll never guess what us girls did today."

Ryan gives her a knowing look. He figures is either going to be awfully trivial and gaining way to much attention or it's going to be quite significant and she's just restraining herself. It could be a third option, but he's just taking a shot in the dark at this point, "You found a cure?"

She looks at him sideways for a moment, "Oh, no. That's tomorrow's plan. Today we found a working printer that we could connect to the generator outside." 

She rushes over across the room and grabs a pink device from the corner before hustling back to the bed. Once retaking her spot beside him, she lifts the device to her face, swipes up on it, and proceeds to navigate to the photos app. 

Phones are but an artifact of the past seeing as they seldomly have power and zero internet, but the entire group—save Ryan—still keeps them around. He admits that they are a handy flashlight and occasionally provide decent entertainment with an offline game or downloaded movie, but otherwise they're largely valueless to him. The group takes turns charging their phones in the car or with this small solar portable charger, but thankfully no one is too device heavy anymore. It'd honestly make him feel a bit left out seeing as he hasn't had a phone in nearly a year.

Chloe sorts through her massive inventory of photos—in the thousands last she check—and settles on one taken that day. She holds it over for him to see.

On the small phone screen is an image of a large book with blank white pages. Tapped to each page is a collective of colorful photos that tell a small narrative. He recognizes an image of their homestead, an image of Caid and Max with a deer carcass, and many more, some of which were taken before the apocalypse.

"We're creating a photo album," Chloe explains as she swipes through a few additional photos, "Beth's storing it in her room until were done, but hopefully it'll tell our story."

"That's..." Ryan thinks, "really cool. I never would have thought to do that."

Chloe shrugs, "Yup, it was actually Alison's idea once we found the printer. It took us so long to figure out how to set it up. Who knew a printer could be so complicated when all it does is print photos. You know?"

With a little laugh, Ryan shrugs his shoulders, "I actually don't but I'll take your word for it."

She rolls her eyes as she returns her phone to it's place in the corner of the room. Occasionally they'll use it as an alarm, but tomorrow isn't one of those days.

Ryan leans back and places his head on the pillow, the strenuous nature of trekking through snow all day is finally getting to him. Chloe steps around to the other side of the bed and lays down next to him. Her head takes up the pillow beside him while her hand falls on his chest.

"Sometimes I just have to imagine how furious my mother would be if she found out I was sleeping with a boy," Chloe explains whimsically as she taps her finger on his chest, "She was one of those: 'don't even look at boys' kind of moms. It's why I never dated anyone in high school."

"Seriously?" Ryan wonders.

Chloe nods as their eyes meet briefly, "Yeah, she had good intentions I'm sure, but it did piss me off sometimes. Heck, it really got to me when I wanted to ask this cute boy to homecoming."

Ryan narrows his eyes, "What was his name...? Jack?"

Chloe snorts as she pinches his chest lightly, "Jack! Not in a million years. No. The boy I wanted to ask had dark hair, sat a few rows behind me in math class, and on occasion used the name Ryan."

He looks up at the ceiling, "How many Ryans did we have in our grade again."

Chloe pinches him harder this time, "One. We had one."

"Must've been a nice guy."

She settles down as she shifts her head closer to him, now on his shoulder, "I don't know. I couldn't bring myself to talk to him."

"Probably for the best, I heard he wasn't much of anything junior year."

Chloe narrows her eyes, "What makes you say that?"

Suddenly Ryan's flow in the conversation crumbles internally. It's one thing to bounce of ideas he can easily disassociate with, but this is a real question he's honestly never answered aloud before. The fact that he's genuinely contemplating answering it, worries him. His junior self—and everything before that honestly—is a Ryan he doesn't want to revisit.

"I guess," he starts, mostly to stall for time, "that guy, allegedly, hated the people around him."

A deep seated sadness watches over Chloe's face as she looks at him with concern, "Really?"

Ryan shakes his head, "Never mind. It's probably better that that guy is gone."

Chloe purses her lips as she reaches her hand across his torso and hugs him, "It's okay."

"But that's enough about him," Ryan says trying his best to pivot, "I wonder how that Chloe is doing these days. I hope she's fairing well."

She opens her eyes partially, "She lost a lot of people close to her so I suspect she's still hurting... but..."

"But?" Ryan echoes.

Chloe places her forehead against his cheek, "But she's gained something to hold her together."

"Is that so?"

Lifting her head, she slowly kisses him on the cheek, "Good night."

“Good night,” Ryan responds.

If only this feeling he's experiencing right now could remain forever. If only this shield defending him from the outside world could hold strong. That would mean that he doesn't have to fear tomorrow, he could look forward to it. Life may suck and the times are only getting harder, but he doesn't particularly hate what he has now. He feels it'd be selfish to want to keep it this way, after all, his friends lost so much. At the same time, however, this is the first time in his life where he feels like he's gained something irreplaceable. If only time could stop, just for a moment.
