_Something about Ryan's first home feels bitterly nostalgic and warmly foreboding. The poorly drawn parrot on the fridge, the sharp island in the center of the kitchen, and the looming door at the end of the hall. All of these features pain a picture of home that he's begun to intellectually despise. The only part of this place he ever wants to remember again is his mother, but she seems to be increasingly sparse with each subsequent nightmare.

_"These goggles are so weird!" Jeremy exclaims as he forcefully tosses a pair of light-obscuring goggles on the floor.

_Ryan didn't know Jeremy back then._

_"You're just weird," five-year-old Ryan comments as he practices his letters in a workbook._

_"Now, now Ryan," a voice says in the background, "Don't treat your friends like that."_

_Ryan looks back at the voice with a shy expression. As he looks over he finds a familiar white apron yet no face. It's not that the speaker doesn't have a face, it's more like a thick mist is obscuring his vision of it—very similar to the goggles Jeremy has._

_Turning to Jeremy, Ryan hangs his head a little, "I'm sorry. That was mean."_

_"It's okay!" Jeremy claims, his face beaming with a smile, "I still think you're a great person."_

_The woman behind him claps her hands together, "Jeremy that's so nice of you."_

_Jeremy beams brighter._

_Returning to his workbook, Ryan scribbles down some half-baked glyphs. Of course, he knows what they mean, but he simply doesn't comprehend why they look the way that they do. He saw a foreign language the other day, and they look like symbols from Mars. So where did his school get these English characters?_

_"Come on, you can do better than that," his mom complains as she takes a seat at the kitchen island beside him, "You always put effort into your work."_

_"Yes mom," Ryan responds meekly._

_He looks up but only finds a disorienting mist above the woman's shoulders. Her voice is of a mother's, yet he can't remember her face. Why?_

_Some other adult—someone with much broader shoulders—approaches him from the other side and inspects his work. With a contemplative demeanor, he shakes his mist-covered head, "This is no son of mine."_

_"Hm?" Ryan wonders looking up at the stranger._

_The man crosses his arms, "My boy would never give up on his work."_

_"Honey don't say that!" the woman strikes back._

_The man shrugs, "Maybe if he were worth raising, I'd be home more."_

_"That's no way to treat your son!" the woman rebuttals_

_Ryan hangs his head before slowly covering his ears, his eyes slowly shutting. His parents never fought before. In fact, hearing his mother raise her voice is not a memory he consciously possesses._

_The arguing grows and grows to point where glass shattered in the background. He wants to look up to find what happens, but fear gets the better of him, and he squeezes his eyes shut tighter. He prays that the distant screaming will end, yet it continues to go on and on._

_Suddenly the escalating voices cease and Ryan, being caught off guard, opens his eyes to look at them. He doesn't find his parents fighting, however, as the familiar home has transformed into an empty void. No parents, no Jeremy, no nothing._

_Then he spots something at a short distance away. Kneeling on the ground with his back turned is Steve—a pistol pressed up against the side of his head. Fully grown Ryan outstretches his arm and makes a silent scream. Steve doesn't even shift._

_Finding himself stuck in place, as if his feet were planted in dried concrete, Ryan fails to step forward. From the void a metallic gray figure appears with two dark slits for eyes. The malicious experiment casually strips the gun out of Steve's trembling hands—the man never looks up. Finally, Experiment S pushes the barrel back into Steve's head. It looks over to Ryan with a slender, gray smile creeping across its face._

__Bang__

Ryan awakens with a start. Thinking he only imagined the gun shot, he tries to take a deep breath, but this action is interrupted by the smoking barrel of a gun two feet from his face. Beside him a bullet hole penetrates the restaurant wall. 

His first thought, however, is not confusion, fear, or even paranoia. The only thing he's concerned about is that Chloe's no longer resting in his arms. Instead of someone he trusts beside him, he finds only a white-camo covered man with an M4 and aviators resting atop the bandana covering his mouth. 

"Are you the real Ryan?" the man asks directly with a complex mix of authority and distance.

Sitting up, Ryan finds his head spinning as nausea eats away at his consciousness. He's been sleep-deprived plenty of times, but this isn't the same feeling, it's... far worse. His body struggles to perform the basic tasks of readjustment without feeling as if he is about to vomit.

"Who are you?" Ryan groans as his hand braces his throbbing head.

The man shakes his head, "I don't have time for this. Answer the question or my next shot won't miss."

Leaning over the table, Ryan searches the surroundings for Chloe and his friends. The subsequent restaurant is completely barren of occupants with only Ryan and this man remaining. The remnants of their party last night remains as dirty plates and an empty bottle of alcohol remains on a nearby table. 

"I don't understand," Ryan eventually concludes as the man's threat seems to be increasingly genuine, "I am the real Ryan."

"Prove it," the man responds quickly and without a hint of sympathy.

With his head still aching and his body struggling to keep up, he uses the brief pause in the conversation to sit up a bit straighter, "I... I don't know what I'm supposed to do."

The man takes a step closer, the barrel inching its way towards Ryan's face, "Before interacting with a cultist, you had a foster father. What was his best friends name?"

Ryan looks at him confused, despite the circumstances, he finds himself rather numb to the idea of getting his brains blown out—thought that'd be impossible anyway. 

"Bill?" Ryan finally guesses, rather aloof at the moment.

The man narrows his eyes behind the aviators, "And what was his daughter's name? The one whom the cultist killed."

Daughter? Bill never had a daughter... or did he? If he can recall correctly, Bill lived alone as far as he knew. Even if he did have a daughter, the man never spoke on such matters. Their conversations were always... less than ideal anyway.

Ryan shakes his head, "Bill didn't die with a family."

Almost immediately, the man lowers his gun with a sigh. Following this relaxation, he pulls the bandana off of his face and hooks his aviators on a strap across his chest. What remains is the dirty face of Drew Wilbur. 

"Drew!" Ryan exclaims, nearly falling forward.

The old friend crosses his arms with an annoyed look, "To think I'd finally find you again in a state like this?"

Ryan blinks slowly, "What do you—" he shakes his head, "Never mind that. Do you know what happened to all of my friends?"

Drew glances around the room at the after scene of their little party. He rolls his eyes at the Everclear. Finally, he takes a breath and looks back to Ryan, "You were the only one here when I arrived."

Clutching a hand to his chest, Ryan feels a mix of heartburn and a sinking pit in his stomach. He can't assume something bad happened... he can't assume... please, he just—he can't assume... not yet.

"They were just here!" Ryan backtracks in his mind trying to remember if anything transpired before he passed out, "They—they wouldn't just leave without me."

Drew holds up his hands in a calming manner, "Take it easy kid. Getting worked up isn't going to solve anything."

Ryan goes silent. Drew is right. Even if his head is spinning and the situation is making his heart race, he knows that overreacting is the worst possible option. The best course of action is to think through his next steps. That logic would comply with the training he received from the man before him now.

As Ryan starts to calm down, Drew relaxes, "Good, I'm glad you're easy to reason with. It gives me confidence that you're real."

"What do you mean by real?" Ryan wonders. He found the initial question peculiar, but the reemphasis is making him uneasy. 

Immediately, Drew narrows his eyes and returns his free hand to the front of his gun. He thankfully doesn't threaten Ryan with it, but the caution alone keeps Ryan's attention.

"Have you not met it?" Drew questions vaguely.

The obvious answer would be to continue to press the man for a clear explanation, but he catches himself before he makes such a blunder. The fact that this entity was referred to as an 'it' says far more than he cares to admit. Although its gender is irrelevant in Ryan's mind, the fact that Drew came to the same conclusion can only mean one thing: he's talking about an experiment. Furthermore, he's likely on guard of an imposter which means he knows about Experiment S. 

Ryan hangs his head, "Unfortunately I have," his head snaps up suddenly, "Don't tell me—that thing was here?"

Drew gives him a confused look before slowly turning his attention towards the front entrance. When Ryan eventually chooses to match his gaze, he finds the brutal answer to any question.

The front entrance window—the largest in the establishment—is nearly completely shattered with fragments of glass decorating the perimeter of the window like jagged teeth. The concerning part isn't the absence of a window, however, instead he finds the horrifying truth to be that all the broken glass isn't inside the building, but rather outside on the pavement. Whatever broke that glass came from within.

"Judging by your expression, I take it you were passed out when that happened," Drew musses as he starts pacing across the room towards the scene. 

Ryan scrambles out of the booth to follow him.

Inspecting the damage carefully, Ryan finds a small trail of blood through the snow on the other side. Multiple sets of footsteps follow that trail of blood down the street and out of view. Without hesitation, he proceeds to follow the trail, but Drew grabs his shoulder before he can exit the building.

"Where do you think you're going?" he questions as if the answer weren't obvious.

Ryan rests a hand on his throbbing head before pointing towards the trail, "I have to go after them."

Drew shoves his shoulder back, "Are you dense or something?! You've met the experiment. You should know that it's a threat that exceeds even a cultist. Being reckless isn't dangerous, it's suicide."

Ryan halts in his tracks as he stares at the man before him. Never, in all the time that he's know him, has Drew ever said something so cautiously—so out of character. Drew is supposed to be relaxed and overly confident, why would he break that facade... or whatever it is.

"It's..." he hesitates, "I'll be fine."

The man leans back and takes a deep breath, "Kid, this isn't the time to be delusional."

Ryan shakes his head, "I don't care. I—I can't just do nothing."

With an exasperated look, Drew takes a seat in a nearby booth, "No one's telling you to do nothing."

Perhaps this is the moment where a normal person would escalate, an immature person would complain and simply leave, or a smarter person would come up with some sound logic. Ryan does exactly none of those things and gives in to silence. Even if his head were perfectly clear, he'd probably still opt to just stand there—contemplating what he's even supposed to think at the moment.

"Why are you here?" Ryan eventually asks when the conversation fails to be productive.

Drew looks up with a blank expression, "Oh... right," he stands up and leans against the backrest of the booth, "I was on the trail of another cultist. I thought I was chasing a tail, but I wound up finding the head. I'll spare you the details, but I'm here at the behest of a frighteningly knowledgeable masked man. Allegedly, all I have to do is give you a hand and all my questions will be answered."

"Wait," Ryan backtracks, "Did you say a masked man told you to come here? Like a man with a raven mask?"

Only a weird look is returned, "What? No. This guy had a strange Mayan mask. Like the ones in ancient Latin America."

Ryan can't say he's familiar with what that looks like, but then he recalls one more detail that might be relevant, "Was it Vincenzo?"

Drew shrugs, "He never gave me his name... though he said plenty to earn my compliance."

"Right," Ryan comments as he looks back out the window, the cold morning air making his shiver.

The discussion stalls once more which leads Drew to push off the side of the booth and pull the strap of his gun until the weapon rests securely against his backpack. He then crosses his arms and takes a glance around as if ensuring the scene was safe.

"I'd ask you what I need to help you with but..." he directs his attention towards the empty booth seats, "I think that's already obvious."

"I don't understand something," Ryan begins as he takes a deep breath, mostly to calm his racing thoughts, "What questions do you have that's worth something like this?"

Drew nods, "Fair question. As you've probably already realized, the Organization is about as enigmatic as they come. Getting information on them is pretty damn hard, and I used to be a well networked assassin. Anyway, if I want to find the leaders of the Organization," he studies Ryan for a moment in contemplation, "if I want to avenge my former squad, getting that information is imperative."

"I see," is all Ryan can think to say.

"So," Drew concludes as he takes a few steps towards the door, "What do you say we get started? I'm sort of the impatient sort."

Ryan leans against a table behind him after noticing his legs feeling oddly heavy, "I thought you just said going after an experiment is suicide?"

Drew nods as casually as he usually does, "And I stand by that statement. I stopped you because you were going to run recklessly into a trap. I'm here to help you get your friend—safely—not to catch an experiment."

Going silent for a moment, Ryan looks down. He absolutely wants to go out right now and save his friends—where ever they are. That being said, some nagging feeling in the back of his mind is echoing the idea that its futile. If he saves them today, what's stopping another incident just like this from happening. How much power is required to keep people save? Why can't life just be simple?

Evidently he started making a face while thinking as Drew transitions across the room and stands right in front of him. Ryan never took himself for a particularly tall person, but looking straight at the man reveals that they're actually the same height. Their matched eye level begins to occupy some of Ryan's idle thoughts.

"I can tell you're not in the thinking mood so why don't I do the planning?" Drew begins in a sort of comforting tone—one that Ryan isn't used to.

"Why?" Ryan blurts out without thinking, "Why are you acting strange?"

Drew gives him a reserved, speculatory glance, "I don't... I don't think I'm acting that strange?"

Ryan shakes his head, "Sorry, I'm not... acting like myself either."

With a sigh, Drew resigns his hands to his pockets, "If you want the truth, I don't really like you—I mean you're better than most—but I hate the pair of youthful ignorance and a lack of competence. When that masked man told me to go help out some kid I'd ordinarily be annoyed as all hell, but..." he looks away rather uncomfortably, "Oh fuck it. You remind me of a kid I used to work with—the only person younger than me that I've ever respected."

They match eye contact briefly. Drew rubs the back of his head.

"His name was Markus," he continues, "Unlike you, he actually solved his own damn problems but... you possess a lot of the same mannerisms as him, so I can't help but feel—I don't know—nostalgic I guess."

Ryan looks down, "I assume that I shouldn't feel complemented by that." He's not actually sure why he said that last part, but at this point, everything exiting his mouth is off the cuff anyway.

Drew shrugs, "To be honest, Markus was actually more impulsive than you. But he was also phenomenal at what he did so... you've got shoes to fill."

With a begrudging sigh, Ryan stands up off the table, "Sure... if you say so."

"Now," Drew says, pivoting in tone, "Let's get out there an assess the situation."

Although unresolved, Ryan feels as though the conversation is unsatisfactorily heartening—which is a significant improvement from whatever he was feeling before. Things still suck, and he has a lot of work to do, but having someone else beside him that's willing to help makes the whole situation... easier to bear. The idea alone makes his heart ache just thinking about how much it sounds like Chloe.

"Assess the situation?" a disembodied voice says out of thin air.

Drew snaps his gun to attention while they both scour the room and surrounding area for any indication of where the voice came from. To their dismay, they find nothing with the capacity to speak.

"Do you honestly believe I'd just steal a bunch of annoying teenagers and hide them for reason," the disembodied voice echoes sarcastically as Ryan recognizes it distinctly. 

His blood runs cold at the thought.

"Show yourself," Drew commands as he snaps his gun towards every entrance in a structured pattern.

"Very well," the voice says.

Suddenly a chair at the end of the nearby counter begins to shift and in the blink of an eye, a humanoid, gray figure stands before them. Two entertained, malicious eyes glare darts into Ryan as S-019 takes a step forward proudly. 

"You really don't die," Drew comments quietly, "I burned your corpse and yet here you are."

S-019 chuckles to _itself_, "Only an experiment can kill another experiment—at least that's how it has worked historical. Not that it's an absolute truth or anything."

"Where are my friends!" Ryan exclaims, feeling the urge to be direct rather than passive for once.

S-019 inspects his for a moment, "Yes, I was just getting to that. Seeing as your friend is here, we can finally get started."

"What do you mean?" Drew asks cautiously, "I'll admit that I suspected that this were a trap, but what exactly do you have in store for us? That man's words were potent, but even I have my limits."

With a casual shrug, S-019 takes a few steps towards the broken window and looks through it, "Would you prefer a monologue or for me to be frank. I've impersonated both, so I feel comfortable doing either."

"Why should we listen in the first place?" Drew comments adeptly. 

S-019 laughs, "Of course you'd say that Drew Wilbur—yes I know your name—but silencing me won't lead to an end for either of you. As it stands, I have Ryan's friends in custody and I have the information you want—or rather need. After all, you have searched for Markus for years and I happen to know where he is."

"Then cut to the chase," Drew demands a little more forcefully this time.

"Oh please," S-019 waves its hand dismissively, "spare me the volatility. Creating a new experiment is a process, one that cannot be rushed and cannot be withheld once initiated."

"A new experiment," Drew wonders under his breath.

S-019 nods, "Yes. That unfortunately individual is right beside you."

Drew's head turns slowly towards Ryan but he doesn't say anything.

"Now, allow me to answer your previous question," S-019 explains as it turns away from the window and faces them directly, empty visage and all, "there is a bandit camp near here, approximately 10 kilomet—" it looks at Ryan, "About 6 and a half miles north of here. Drew Wilbur, you may aid with tactical advice and peripheral support, but Ryan has to do the deed."

"The deed?" Ryan echoes ominously.

S-019 crosses its arms behind its back, "Ryan, I need you to kill every breathing human being in that bandit camp. Do that, and I'll return your friends, if not, I'll kill them—all of them."

Drew glances between Ryan and the experiment with a tactical gaze. Although it takes him a while to formulate an answer, it takes Ryan longer.

"That's hardly a fair ultimatum," Drew admits, his finger tightening on the trigger.

S-019 shrugs it's monochrome shoulders nonchalantly, "That's the deal."

"But..." Ryan tries, "I—I can't."

Despite the unconfident noncompliance, S-019 turns and begins walking out of the restaurant, "Contemplate it, mull it over, whatever you need to do. I'll see you there."

Drew never fires a round as S-019 disappears into the landscape outside. Whether it became an inanimate object or simply cut line of sight hardly matters to Ryan as he leans against the nearby counter. He rings his cold fingers through his hair with his gaze fixated on the polished wood surface. The unorderly swirls of wood are the only thing that sidetracks his mind from the impossible decision.

"You don't have to do it," Drew offers as he lowers his gun and takes a deep breath, "An experiment's words mean something but that doesn't mean they're infallible."

Ryan takes a seat at one of the counter chairs and leans his elbows against the wood. As far as he knows, he stopped breathing when S-019 left and now his lungs are feeling it—yet he can't seem to breath.

"I suggest we look for your friends first," Drew strategizes "we'll likely have to fight that thing sooner or later, so we might as well head for the source. We seemingly can't kill it, but a bullet to the head will at least slow it down."

"And then what?" Ryan wonders pathetically as his head hangs lower, his hands cover his eyes, "It's just going to happen again."

Maybe this is the point where Drew gives him some inspirational speech, or maybe some practical advice. After all, he's the guy who's seen war and carnage more than anyone else, he should be used to this sort of thing. Ryan's just some kid who has gotten in over his head. They aren't the same. It's not Drew's responsibility to make him feel any better, but at this point, Ryan will take anything—he's loosing the will to keep fighting.

"Kid..." Drew says slowly while Ryan remains locked in his position, "I want to correct one thing about what I said earlier. Markus may have been tough and incredibly skilled for his age, but... we were fighting humans back then. These experiments... well... I pity you."

Ryan squeezes his eyes closed tighter. He doesn't know why he remembers, but Heimes pitied him too back then. Back before the school incident, Heimes said that very same thing, so why? Why does he remember this now? Why does his control seem to be so fleeting? Why? Why? Why? Damn it, now he's using that word Doctor Crane loves so much. 

"What am I supposed to do?" Ryan groans softly to no one in particular.

Drew, however, takes it upon himself to answer, "It's your choice."

Choice.

First Doctor Crane's, now Vincenzo's words are starting to come back. It's as if they were all pointing him down the path he's currently on. Why can't he get these thoughts out of his head.

"If we did go after my friends directly," Ryan wonders, mostly out of desperation, "Do you think we could get to them... before..."

Drew shakes his head, "You don't have to say it. Normally in hostage situations, the offending party wants something which is why the hostages are left alive, but it doesn't make sense here. An experiment doesn't need to offload trivial work like killing a bunch of innocent people. There has to be another reason."

Ryan hangs his head again, "It's part of the process, isn't it. To turn me into an experiment."

The man purses his lips and crosses his arms over his gun, "I can't say for sure."

"I talked to S-019 before and it told me that experiments have three criteria for creation," Ryan begins as he turns around in his chair slowly, "It said that every experiment has a supernatural ability and cannot be killed."

"And the third," Drew asks, raising and eyebrow.

Ryan shakes his head, "I don't know."

"And how many of those have you fulfilled?" Drew presses, a bit more interested this time.

Sagging his head even lower, Ryan closes his eyes, "Both."

Drew opens his mouth to say something but then they both hear a knocking at a nearby window. As their heads snap in that direction, they find S-019 standing casually on the other side with its fist slightly tapping the glass. It doesn't use its voice to prompt them but rather points to something outside their view. The two of them exchange glances, but Ryan is the first one out the door.

Turning the corner he finds S-019 standing in the middle of the snowy street beside someone hunched over with their hands tied behind their back and a blindfold over their eyes. It doesn't take Ryan more than half a second to identify them: Caid.

"I thought you could use some more motivation," S-019 calls out as its left arm begins to elongate into a sharp scythe blade.

*Bang*

S-019 collapses into a pile of gray goop as it splatters on the ground. Beside Ryan is Drew with his barrel smoking just the faintest bit.

Ryan doesn't stop to consider any of that as he rushes to his friend's aid. He reaches for his friend but the guy jumps at the contact as if the presence of a mouse would scare him. Ripping the bandana off, he finds Caid's face... though it's far from the same. In place of Caid's usual brown, contemplative eyes are two wide gashes with blood still draining out; the inside of the bandana being completing covered in the red liquid.

"Caid!" Ryan exclaims as the boy collapses to the side.

"Ryan?" he says weakly, "Is that you?"

"Yeah, it's me!" Ryan forces out quickly as he searches Caid's body for some sort of antidote to the profuse bleeding. He doesn't find anything within reason, he does the only thing he can think to do and pushes the bandana back against the boy's face. 

Caid recoils in pain, "Ah."

"I'm sorry!" Ryan apologizes as he holds the bandana in place despite Caid's squirming.

"Keep the pressure up," Drew commands as he scours the landscape for any indication of S-019 coming back. He also checks in on the lifeless puddle of gray goop which is no more threatening than the snow around it.

Ryan continues to keep pressure on the bandana, his fingers growing wet as the blood penetrates the thin fabric, "How did this happen?"

At some point, Caid gave up on resisting the pain and slowly grabbed on to Ryan's arm, "It's fine, you can let go."

"No!" Ryan asserts, "I'm not going to let you die."

Suddenly growing a bit frustrated, Caid shoves Ryan's arm off of him with enough force to make Ryan step back. Afterward, he just takes a deep breath, "The bastard poisoned me, this is probably the end of the line."

Both Ryan and Drew's eyes widen.

"What are you saying?!" Ryan demands, his forehead sweating despite it being ice cold outside.

"He stuck me with some needle," Caid says weakly, "I've never felt this faint in my life."

"It could just be a sleeping agent," Drew points out in the background.

Caid chuckles as he lays his back on the ground, "Wouldn't that be ideal... I'm still glad it was me though."

"Caid..." Ryan tries.

Caid shakes his head, "That monster was going to take Max to do... well this. At the last minute I convinced it to use me instead. Heck, Max would have cried like a baby if he'd got his eyes gauged out."

"That's not funny."

With a light laugh, Caid coughs, "Yeah, Max's jokes weren't funny either... I guess he rubbed off on me."

"Then... then you'll have to see him again."

"Get them back for me, won't you?" Caid asks rather upbeat despite the fact the blood is beginning to seep out the corners of his mouth.

"Y–yeah..." Ryan stammers, "Of course, you can count on me."

Caid smiles, "Oh and Ryan," Ryan leans in closely, "Tell Beth that she's good enough... and that I love her."

"No!" Ryan says quickly, his cheeks growing hot, "Y–you can tell her yourself. You just need to get up and then we... we can... we can go back to the way things used to be. It can be in the farmhouse, or maybe we can go back to high school. I don't even care if none of you remember me, as long as you're alive an safe, that's all I care about."

Caid's movements grow as minimal as possible, "You know Ryan," he says with a shallow, drifting tone, "You've always cared a little too much about the people around you... never change."

"No. no no no no no," Ryan repeats in disbelief, "We'll get out of here. I promised I'd protect you. You've just got to hold on. Hold on damn it! Just hold on! I promised I'd protect all of you. Please don't leave me too."

Drew grabs on to his shoulder with a empathetic look.

One hundred restarts later, Ryan would crawl out of the restaurant only to find Caid as dead as he is now.
