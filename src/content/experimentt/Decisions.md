The walk back to their farmhouse is understandably quiet. They finally made it out of a horrible situation and now it seems like they're being dragged back in. Whether they run or investigate is still up for debate, but either way, this is not a position he ever wanted to be back in.

“You sure about this?” Ryan asks as they walk down the long driveway of their farmhouse.

Blake glances around at the glistening snow around them, the road only partially shoveled, "I guess I agree that we can't be too hasty, but I still think there's something there that we should see. We didn't know why Heimes sacrificed the school until it was too late, but this time can be different."

Ryan nods his head a few times as he looks down. He still finds himself conflicted.

They arrive at the porch of the farmhouse and Ryan halts before opening the door. He places an arm out to stop Blake as well, "I don't know what we'll decide, but... if we do investigate, not everyone gets to come."

Blake nods slowly, clearly taking the matter serious. A flash of concern strikes his face, but he suppresses anything further, "Yeah... sure."

Suddenly, the door swings opens up revealing an eager Max beaming with joy. Behind him Beth walks over from the living room curious to hear what happened with the museum.

“How’d it go?” Beth asks, crossing her arms as the two young men enter inside and slowly begin taking their winter gear off.

Ryan sighs remembering that they did in fact take down a building full of zombies today. It’s not that he had forgotten, but the situation has changed enough that such a feat feels insignificant by comparison. That's not even considering the fact that the talking zombie came back. He may not have told anyone about those experiences, but maybe it's about time he did.

“It worked out just as planned,” Blake explains, “Nothing is left of that place or the monsters inside.”

Beth sighs with relief just as Chloe steps down the stairs, “Nobody told me you guys got back. How’d it go?”

“Perfectly,” Blake repeats.

Ryan is glad that it all worked out, but he can’t bring himself to smile about it given the dangerous problem that awaits him. If things go the way that they should go, they'll stay far away from town, but that's assuming he can convince anyone.

“Why don’t you look happy?” Beth asks as she inspects Ryan's unintentionally grim expression.

Ryan gets his coat off and hangs it up. 

Blake crosses his arms and looks around the room, “We have a bit of a problem.”

The room goes silent. Max glances at Blake expectantly. Beth narrows her eyes. Everyone else is frozen in the thick tension that spontaneously appears.

Blake continues, “Let’s all take a seat in the living room and I can explain everything.”

Beth calls up the stairs to Caid and Alison in which they both hurry down to join the tense group in the living room sitting before a slow burning fire. They all glance at each other nervously as Blake stands up and walks in front of the fire.

“While Ryan and I were in town, we found some survivors,” Chloe, Alison, and Max gasp, “Those survivors told us about a dangerous camp in town. I plan on going over and investigating it to check out what the situation looks like.”

“Why do you say it’s dangerous?” Beth questions.

Blake glances over at Alison who’s nervously holding her hands together at the thought of another potential group conflict. 

"Potentially dangerous," he corrects, "That's why I want to check them out."

“Alright then!” Caid exclaims standing to his feet, “Let’s go check it out.”

Blake glances over at Ryan in thought for a moment before he addresses the comment directly, “It’s only going to be me and Ryan.”

Caid is taken aback and clenches his fist, “What do you mean? I can help you.”

“We don’t know how dangerous it is,” Ryan offers without making eye contact, he doesn't want to know what Caid looks like.

“I can still help!” Caid exclaims, "This is huge, we should already be doing something about it!"

Alison's gaze darts between Blake and Caid with increasing velocity. This cue makes everyone stop talking awkwardly as she takes some deep breaths by her lonesome. 

“Chloe,” Blake says keeping his gaze fixed on Caid, “Could you take Alison back to her room?”

Chloe nods as she nudges Alison to stand up. From there, she escorts Alison back towards the stairs and out of view. Once the footsteps on the steps cease, Blake returns to the conversation with a serious composer and tone.

"This camp is killing its own people," Blake asserts to an understandably quiet crowd.

“But… why?” Beth says slowly, still grappling with the global shock hanging about the room.

Blake turns around and faces the embers of the fire pit, “We don’t know yet, that’s why it’s only going to be me and Ryan. The less of us there are, the less likely we’ll be spotted.”

“What if you do get spotted?” Caid voices, rather callously.

“We’re going to try and avoid it,” Blake admits, though not as confidently as before.

Caid stands up and stares directly at the guy, “But the more of us there are, we’re that much more likely to be able to defend ourselves.”

Blake glances briefly at Ryan but pulls away before anyone can notice. He thinks the dilemma over with a tapping foot and pursed lips. Eventually, he looks back to Ryan first before addressing the rest of the group.

"It's just going to be Ryan and I, that's my decision."

"Who died and made you the leader?!" Caid demands while standing on his feet, growing increasingly heated with each passing word.

“That’s not what he’s saying,” Beth tries to explain.

Ryan catches Blake looking at him expectantly, but averts his eyes the second they meet one another. For some reason, Ryan can't help but feel guilty of the situation.

"I get it, you just want to be safe," Max butts in with a much more relaxed, playful demeanor, "this is the same sort of thing you demanded when we were in our base. But this isn't like then. We're not helpless, besides, what ever happened to the Boys? We're a team remembers."

"Plus me," Beth adds.

Blake looks away, his eyes full of hurt, but he tries his best to conceal it. He mouths something to himself before taking a deep breath. With a passing glance at Ryan, he continues, "I still remember everything that's happened, but that was then and this is now."

Caid stares Blake down for a moment in utter silence. The whole room awaits his decision. With a disgruntled groan, Caid falls back onto the couch which his arms crossed, "Fine whatever."

No one is relieved by that response, but they accept it. No one presses further on the matter, or at least as far as Ryan can tell.

"Alright then," Blake says in a conclusionary manner, "We'll head out tomorrow, I don't want to push our luck and have it get late."

Later that afternoon, Ryan finds himself chopping wood in the yard. The thick axe feels quite sturdy in his hand as he slams the blade into a chunk of wood and watches it split into almost symmetrical pieces. He proceeds to kick both pieces over to a pile of similarly looking wooden logs before grabbing a much larger, heavier piece and setting it on the cutting stump. 

At the sound of crunching snow behind him, he turns around to find Caid walking up with his hands in his pockets and his gaze lowered.

"What's up?" Ryan asks casually as he places the head of the axe onto the ground and rests his hand on the handle sticking up.

Caid glances over at the axe, "Mind if I split some?"

Ryan nods before lifting the axe and offering the handle over to his friend. Caid accepts it quietly before gesturing with his head for Ryan to step back. When he complies, Caid draws the axe back and slams it into the wooden log Ryan had placed on the stump.

"Sorry about earlier," Caid offers in between strikes of axe, "I don't like getting emotional like that... Blake just... gets on my nerves sometimes."

Ryan shakes his head, "No need to apologize."

Caid presses his lips together and goes back to chopping. In the meantime, Ryan takes a seat on the trunk of a downed tree. About two weeks earlier, Max got the wise idea to cut down a tree just to see if he could. It did provide for excellent firewood so no one was particularly upset by it, but they lacked a proper chainsaw so they couldn't access the copious amount of wooden hidden behind a dark bark and a foot-and-a-half wide trunk. The branches of the tree became the firewood they're actively chopping up.

"I still can't believe he did that," Caid comments as he places the head of the axe down and wipes some sweat off his brow before it freezes.

"Who Max?"

Caid nods, "Yeah, he really likes to pull shenanigans like this. He's been doing it for years."

Ryan runs his hand over the bark contemplating what really constitutes as a 'shenanigan', "Is that so?"

Caid shrugs as he picks up the axe, "Want to swap out?"

Without needing to confirm, Ryan rises to his feet and takes the axe out of Caid's hand. In the span of a few minutes at best, Caid has somehow managed to split four pretty significant logs into a dozen pieces. Ryan inspects the handiwork with a bit of awe before placing another log on the stump.

After a few lousy strikes, Caid decides to bring the conversation back, "Were you an adventurous kid?"

Ryan stops swinging and looks back to the guy now seated on the log. He folds his hands together on his lip as he raises and eyebrow, awaiting a response.

After mulling the question over, Ryan shrugs his shoulders, "Yeah, you could say?"

Caid narrows his eyes with a smirk, "And what does that mean?"

To avoid matching his gaze, Ryan proceeds to swing the axe again and cut clean through the present log. His stroke seems to have a bit more impact that his previous ones did—interesting. 

"I was a bit of a troublemaker back in the day," Ryan explains casually, though underneath he gets a repulsive feeling about the subject matter, "Particularly in my few foster families."

Caid raises an eyebrow, "You were a foster child?"

Ryan stops chopping for a second, "Have I never mentioned that?" he shrugs his shoulders, "Guess it doesn't matter though."

Caid goes oddly silent, though Ryan doesn't look back, instead he goes back to chopping wood.

"I ran out a lot," Ryan comments, mostly to himself, though he's not really sure why he's saying this to begin with, "Couldn't stand the feeling of living in a stranger's house. Shenanigans aye?"

It takes him a moment to process it, but eventually Caid forces a small laugh, "Yeah, yeah..."

"I leveled out though," Ryan admits as he slams the axe through a log and winds up getting it stuck in the stump, "...hm... I think I'm must more behaved nowadays."

Caid shakes his head, probably to relieve himself of some underlying thoughts, "Just like Max."

Ryan looks back as he places his foot against the stump, "Max? This is better than before?"

Caid chuckles under his breath with a roll of his eyes, "Oh boy, that's a story."

Feeling that he needs another break, Ryan pulls the axe out of the stump and props it up on the ground. He then turns around and takes a seat on the stump with his hands rubbing together for warmth. The bitter cold takes a toll, but it's not so bad after a workout.

"So I didn't know Max until middle school right," Caid starts as his hands become engaged in the recollection, "When we met in sixth grade, I hated the guy. He was an absolute buffoon—a class clown. He was the sort of kid to argue with the teacher that their reading goal couldn't be one page per week. That sort of personality was one I simply couldn't stand." 

"Really?" Ryan offers idly. 

Caid smiles, "I went the whole year absolutely detesting the guy. And then, do you know what happened? In seventh grade, we got placed in the same class AGAIN and what's more, he even sat right next to me. It was completely random, but I'm still suspicious of that teacher."

"So what changed?" Ryan wonders as he leans back on the log.

"Right," Caid shifts in place, "So, I hate the guy for the first semester and had to listen to him non-stop the whole time. Then, about halfway through the second semester, something happened. Our class had gotten homework the day before, but I completely forgot about it—didn't even spare a thought. I only realized that I hadn't done it when the teacher started walking around the room to collect the damn thing. So anyway, my tiny seventh grade brain thought that maybe I could complete the whole assignment before the teacher arrived to my desk. The work wasn't hard, but there was no way in hell I finish that thing before she got to my desk. That's when Max noticed."

Ryan leans in.

"I fully expected him to laugh and rat me out, just like that. After all, I was the only one who had forgotten about the assignment, so he probably had the right to make fun of me—heaven knows he was good at it."

"That didn't happen," Ryan predicts aloud.

Caid nods, "It didn't. Instead of pointing and laughing, Max stood up, walked over to the teacher actively collecting papers and started yapping. That kid had spent nearly a decade giving unsolicited opinions about just about everything. So when he starts talking to the teacher, she rightfully tried to shut him up and keep collecting papers. Max didn't let go, he started asking questions about the assignment, asked what we were going to learn next week because he'd be gone, heck he straight up asked the teacher if she was single. Somehow that kid distracted the teacher for almost five minutes—just enough time for me to finish my assignment. After that, I handed it in and didn't get a zero."

"Wow," Ryan comments, "that's one heck of a save."

Caid raises his eyebrows as he slides his hands together and breathes into them, "It was. I won't say I liked him after that, but I did perceive his actions with a bit more... ur... understanding."

Ryan rests his hand on the handle of the axe and looks off into the distance. If only he had a friend like that when he was in middle school. A friend who had his back, even when he didn't think he deserved it. Maybe if he just had that... he could have turned out different. Alas, no sense in dwelling on it now.

"I later found out that we lived right next to each other," Caid continues, "our relationship was... shaky at first. I still got annoyed when he mouthed off in front of other people, but I could tell underneath that facade was a kind-hearted kid—someone worth befriending. By the time we got to high school, we basically were best friend. I like to make fun of him but... yeah..."

The door to the farmhouse suddenly opens and outcomes Beth dressed in her puffy wool coat. She walks up to them at a casual pace before claiming a spot on the log right next to Caid, "Staying busy I see."

Caid chuckles, "Yeah, something like that."

"So Max is better now?" Ryan wonders, still a little dissatisfied with the conclusion of the narrative.

Caid nods, "He's grown up, quite a bit, actually." he looks around just to make sure no one unintended is close enough to hear, "You can't tell Max I said this, but I think he's really brave. Reckless. But brave. And I respect the hell out of him for that."

Ryan looks around to verify that there were no prying ears and thankfully finds no one. He notices Alison's window on the second floor but thankfully doesn't find anyone sitting inside. Not that anyone would hear through the window, but he just wanted to be sure.

"Sometimes I wish I was brave like that," Caid says softly to himself.

Beth reaches around Caid's arm and interlocks her fingers with his, "You're someone worth respecting too."

"Oh come on," Caid says rubbing the back of his head, "I know where I stand."

"Please," Beth tries, giving him a sore look, "Everyone has a high opinion of you. You should have more confidence in yourself."

Caid averts his eyes as he looks out to the distant fence line of the farm, "I do have confidence but... this is different."

Beth purses her lips in a sort of pouty look, "Don't be stubborn on me now."

The two of them reach a subtle lull as a seemingly stubborn force meets an unyielding pestering. Ryan finds himself captivated by the dynamic for some reason though his true thoughts lie with Caid and how he holds himself. Someone about his self perception makes him feel uncomfortable—like he can't simply let him think that without recourse. 

"You _are_ brave, Caid," Ryan says looking up to the chimney, just to avoid eye contact, "Brave enough to let go of a loved one... I'm sure Nana would be proud."

When Ryan eventually looks back to the guy, he finds his lip quivering. He seems to notice Ryan's attention and the quivering immediately ceases but not without him looking away. Beth rubs her hand up and down his arm with a sympathetic expression.

Ryan's not sure if what he said was a good or bad thing, but he knows it definitely meant something. It just that... he couldn't... he couldn't sit still knowing Caid felt that way. To think he of all people isn't brave. If anyone should be saying that they're a coward, it's him, though... maybe thinking that alone makes him no better than Caid in that respect. These thoughts swirling through his head are like an avalanche of indecipherable messages that wash over him with a gritty texture that leaves a lasting sting. It doesn't make any sense, but he knows that he still regrets not speaking up when Steve needed a hand. If his words can touch Caid—even a little—it'll be worth it.

"So Ryan," Beth begins, clearly trying to change subjects, "with the museum burnt to a crisp, is the town even the slightest bit safer?"

Ryan gives her a quizzical look, "You did hear the conversation right? With the other survivors."

Beth nods, "Yes, but we've been here for weeks and never crossed paths with a single person until today. That would lead me to believe the side of town we've stayed on doesn't have anyone there, so it should be safer than before, no?"

With a shrug of his shoulders, Ryan leans forward and places his elbows on his knees, "I guess... when you put it that way."

"Well you see," Beth continues, narrowly withholding a smirk, "there's this cute little store on the outskirts of town. I recognized the brand name and would like to check it out."

Caid rolls his eyes as he keeps his gaze fixated on the fence line, "Oh please, Blake would never let you go. Not now anyway."

Beth smiles mischievously, "But, what if Ryan came with us."

"Us?" Caid and Ryan say together.

Beth nods as she taps her lips with a finger, "I was thinking we could have a little double-date. Me and Caid," she turns to Ryan, "and then you and Chloe. Sound like fun?"

"I don't know," Caid comments only to receive a playful slap on the back of the head, "I mean, yes, that sounds thrilling."

The mastermind turns to Ryan next, "What do you think? Blake seems to trust you, so I think I can convince Blake to look the other way if you're tagging alone."

Caid shoots his girlfriend a sideways glance, "You make it sound like he's a third wheel."

Beth rolls her eyes, "It's a double date, there's no third wheels."

"Right," Caid says, mostly to himself, "As long as he has a partner for when we make out."

Beth's eyes flare open as she shoves him, nearly knocking him off the log, "Caid you can't just say that aloud!"

Caid laughs, a welcome sight after his bout of self reflection, "Sorry. Thinking about Max has got me acting like him."

The two lovers eventually notice the actual third wheel in the situation and halt their flirting. At least, that's probably what they were thinking, though Ryan was too caught up in his thoughts to dwell on it. He's still grappling with the fact that she thinks this plan will work.

"I still don't get it," Ryan wonders, "Blake and I are going to check out the camp tomorrow... when would we do this?"

Beth stands up and tugs at Caid's arm, "Right now. We've still got a few hours of daylight. Besides, dates are best at evening time anyway."

Ryan holds up a hand which pauses their efforts to head inside, "Are you sure Blake will agree to this?"

Sure he and Blake go out and do the scavenging, but he's not, so sure Blake will agree to a little stroll through town under the pretext of a double date. Under the current circumstances, staying home and waiting until tomorrow is likely going to be the only agreeable plan for everyone. Ryan's own intrigue aside, he can't simply be careless just because the idea of a date is brought up. Sure he's always wondered what a date is like... but that isn't important right now. 

Beth waves her hand nonchalantly, "He seems to be rather agreeable to most plans as long as you're around. Don't worry though, I'll handle all the talking."

"No!" Blake asserts, "There could be killers, you can't just. Seriously? Go on a date? Do you think I was joking about what we heard?"

The predictable response only comes after Chloe was informed about the situation and joined Caid, Beth, and Ryan by the front door. She seems rather startled by the proposition but ultimately agreed to it rather quickly. Ryan half expected her to be bashful or need further convincing, but it seems her best friend had a silver tongue, sharp enough to get her on board in only a few sentences. Now they finds themselves with their winter coats on and all set to depart. It just so happened that Blake caught them in the act.

"Blake listen," Beth attempts jumping in front of him with a defensive demeanor, "We're not going into town, we're just checking out a shop on the outskirts. I promise we won't try and get any closer that we feel comfortable," she turns to Ryan, "I'll even let him make the call."

"Right," Caid agrees which actually catches Ryan by surprise, "We're not doing this to be funny, we just need to a quick break. That's all."

Blake studies them each individually with a perceptive eye. He glosses over the first two rather quickly, but then he dwells on his inspection of Ryan. During the visual interrogation, Chloe shifts a bit closer to Ryan. Blake's jaw tightens before he looks away. Before anyone can notice anything, he pinches the bridge of his nose and lets out a sigh.

"Blake," Chloe adds, "It'll be perfectly fine. The museum is gone right? If anything it should be safer than before."

"Thanks Chloe," Beth smiles before turning back to Blake, "I've had on eye on it for a while, and this might be our last chance if things don't go well with the survivor camp."

Blake waves his hands off to the sides, "it could be your last thing that happens to you if those strangers show up!"

Beth goes silent along with Caid and Chloe. It's not that any of them were speechless, per say, but it seems as though Blake's assertion is a bit harder to circumvent than a little emotional or logistical plea.

He could offer his opinion on the matter. Say that he agrees with Blake that this is sort of risky and out of the blue, but on the other hand, part of him wants this plan to fall through. A dat—going out with friends is something he never did enough of in the past. This could be his chance, even if it is rather impulsive at first glance.

Beside, he needs to decompress a little after the encounter with the talking mutant and the cryptic message. A little getaway might be more necessary than he originally believed.

"It'll be fine," Ryan says out of the blue, catching the attention of the entire group, "I'll make sure it is."

Beth smiles knowingly with her back turned to Blake. When she turns back around, any mischievous look she may have had disappears and returns to controlled negotiation. In the background, Max watches the scene unfold with a smirk as he dusts off his hands against his apron. Alison, too, watches on from the top of the stairs.

Blake mulls over the situation rather diligently rather than continuing to reject it. Ryan's not sure why his comment would incur such a deep contemplation, but regardless he doesn't seem as dismissive of Beth's idea anymore.

"Ryan," he eventually asks, looking through the crowd, "You'll be back by dark?"

"No later than an hour after dark," Beth agrees, "You have my word."

Blake's gaze hangs on Ryan for a moment before he turns around and starts walking into the living room, "Don't make me regret this."

Immediately Beth starts jumping up and down as Caid carefully catches her so she doesn't jump into something. It's enough to bring a shallow smile to Ryan's face. Then, right as he's about to open the door, Chloe steps up to him. Their eyes meet as she offers a very warm smile—one he wasn't prepared for. She then grabs on to his hand with hers covered in a thin glove.

"Let's go," she says.
