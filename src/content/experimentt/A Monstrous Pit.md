When they depart that afternoon, they're met with a rather chilly wind. The Fall had been so prominent in recent memory that the dawning Winter takes Ryan completely by surprised. The icy air that makes his lung dry, the slow rolling of gray clouds, and a subtle pickup of the wind all paint a picture of a coming storm. He just hopes that the storm remains exclusively a weather-related one and nothing more. 

In light of the temperature, he pulls his leather jacket a closed despite there not being any buttons to hold it together. Between his shirt and jacket, he's certain he'll be able to handle today's weather, but he makes a mental note to acquire more suitable winter wear as soon as possible. 

Despite his grievances with the weather, Ryan opts to drive his motorcycle to their destination. Part of the reason being that he can scout ahead just a little when driving by himself. The rest of the gang, save Max, crowd into Blake's truck. Max, not accepting no for an answer, takes his Corvette out for another spin.

Ryan leads the way to an unsuspecting parking lot about a block away from the stadium. He's still not sure what he's going to find when he goes back to the place of death, but he decides it's best if he keeps their escape route a fair ways away. As such, he pulls over into the convenience store parking lot and watches as the two vehicles following him pull in after.

"All I'm saying is that you're too serious sometimes," Beth points out as she slides out of the truck.

"Serious doesn't have to be a bad thing," Chloe defends as she follows after her.

Blake, shoving the truck keys into his pocket, jumps out of the driver side door, "Thank you, Chloe, at least someone understands."

"Well, being serious may have been important when we were on our own, but that's not the case anymore," Alison adds as she's the last one to get out of the back row of seats.

"Ryan!" Beth exclaims as she walks straight over to him, "We need your opinion on something."

"What?" he asks as the three girls create a half circle around him with Blake sticking his head over a shoulder. Caid simply waits by the truck as Max gets out of his car.

"Do you think Blake can be too serious?" Beth asks pointing at the person in question, "Just think about the party we had, he was telling everyone to stop drinking."

"Okay," Ryan prompts wanting a bit more context.

Blake forces his way into the circle and crosses his arms, "I'm just trying to be rational. Back me up here."

"I still on the side that Blake's seriousness isn't a bad thing," Chloe voices.

"I don't know," Allison thinks, "I'm on the fence."

"Come on girls, you've got to back me up," Beth complains as she waves her hands dramatically at her friends.

"We're getting off track," Blake asserts before turning to Ryan, "What do you think?"

Ryan studies the group one more time before procuring an answer. Blake clearly wants to defend his behavior, and he respects the guy to some degree for doing it. After all, his leadership kept the teens alive for almost a week. That being said, Allison is right, times are different, and he can take a load off once in a while—it might do him good. Though telling Blake to relax makes him feel like a hypocrite so there really is no good answer here. 

"I think," Ryan starts off slowly, "I think Blake is Blake."

He receives quite a few eye rolls from that comment.

"Great, dodge the question," Beth comments dryly as she looks at the convenience store in front of them, "anyway, what are we doing here?"

Ryan swings his leg off his motorcycle and stands up, "I thought you guys can check out this store while I go assess the situation at the stadium."

"What do you mean?" Blake questions, "We're out here to help you. So we'll come to."

Ryan shakes his head, "I'm not saying you can't help, but I need to check it out first."

Blake is about to rebuttal again—Beth joining him—but that is halted as Chloe steps forward in front of them, "Just let him go." 

"Of course you'd take his side," Blake complains throwing his hands up.

"You don't know what's there!" she exclaims bringing the conversation to a bitter silence, "You weren't there last night, so you don't understand what he's trying to say. Just—just let us go and check it out. Then we'll come back."

"You two?" Blake repeats glancing between them.

To be honest, Ryan opposes the idea of Chloe coming to check things out with him, but out of all of them, she's the only one who knows about Jeremy, so perhaps this plan if for the best. All he really wants to do is make Jeremy a bit more presentable and see what the aftermath of the slaughter is. Neither of those things will require him to stick his neck out, but he has no idea what he'll be walking in to.

After a lengthy amount of time contemplating his options and the lack of response from Ryan, Blake speaks first. He turns to Beth with an exasperated look, "It seems your friend has switched up her loyalties."

Beth takes a deep breath, "I'd agree with you, but I'm no better," she turns to address Ryan and Chloe, "Just go check things out and come back. Don't you dare do something risky."

The group watches them closely as Ryan and Chloe depart down the street on foot. He intentionally picked a spot that was a walkable distance from the stadium, but seeing the large building just a block down the street makes it feel all too close. The thought fades quickly as he notices Blake and the others heading into the convenience store. Max yells something which makes him and Chloe look back, but then they all laugh which eases their tension—even at such a distance.

"I'd never noticed it before, but Alison is a really good artist," Chloe says, clearly piggybacking off the sudden shift in atmosphere, "she's started painting a mural on the side of the prison."

"Is that so," Ryan offers trying to sound as interested as he can.

Chloe nods, "She says that depending on how it turns out, she might ask to paint one on the front of the building. I'm excited for her, the sketch looked amazing."

As they continue to walk down the street sidewalk, Ryan takes note of a nearby building with tinted windows. He doesn't expect anything to come out of the secretive building, but not being able to gauge what the windows conceal keeps his guard up. Chloe seemingly takes notice of this tension and nudges him with her shoulder.

"It'll be alright," she says with a comforting looking in her eyes, "I'm sure..." she trails off, "at least you won't have to do it alone."

Ryan can sense the awkward correction coming so he jumps in before anything can change, "Thank you."

The singular comment makes Chloe hesitate, but she quickly settles into. Their formerly tense walk finally ends in a much more casual stroll despite what's up ahead. Ryan finds himself getting a weird feeling by having someone else next to him. It's not even the normal complexities he has to navigate with Chloe around: this feeling is far simpler. Walking into situations alone has always been his preferred way of doing just about everything, so for that not to be the case, he should feel unsettled or at least a bit confused. This feeling, however, isn't like that. He can't really put it into words, but he'd surmise that it's a feeling similar to how girls always want to go to the bathroom together or how guys always grouped up before doing stupid stuff. It's not necessarily a strong feeling, but at this point, he's realizing that he's never felt it before—that fact alone makes him pause.

As they journey inevitably arrives at their destination, Chloe intentionally steps closer to him with a nod. Ryan gives her a look of mild confusion.

"You were going to tell me to stay close," she predicts, "like last time."

Ryan purses his lips. To be honest, he wasn't even thinking that yet. The 'yet' part is what ultimately gets him the most. So, instead of dwelling on it, he just keeps walking through the sparsely populated parking lot.

Just as last time, Ryan draws his knife and prepares for a zombie to lunge out from behind a vehicle. Nothing happened the first time they did this, so he hopes that his luck will remain the same. That being said, he isn't going to risk it so he ensures that his pistol is readily available.

The first few cars present no trouble, their vacant chassis' being nothing more than a road decoration to him. The last few cars obstructing his view of Jeremy's corpse are also as empty as they ever were which gives Chloe the go ahead to let go of the back of his jacket—though he didn't realize she had even started doing that—and hustle around the last car. He follows closely behind but finds her staring wide-eyed. When he finally turns the corner, he supposes that he likely harbors the exact same expression.

Jeremy's forlorn corpse is nowhere to be found. A pool of dried blood remains where it once laid but the body itself has vanished. The only clue left behind is a smeared trail of crimson leading away from the dried pool and towards the stadium. 

They exchange glances slowly.

"What could have happened?" Chloe wonders with a shaky tone.

Ryan wishes he could call the sinking pit in his stomach mere déjà vu, but he already knows that it's not. A missing corpse, streaks of blood, and an epicenter of catastrophe. He knows he's seen a scene like this before, it may not have been at a scale of this magnitude, but the signs are all pointing in one direction. That direction makes the pit sink deeper.

He looks back, but Chloe simply shakes her, "It's fine, if I don't have a weapon, I'd be putting us both at risk."

On all accounts, her reasoning is solid and probably the best course of action. In all the situations he's been in she's actually the first person to not insist on coming further. Maybe she forced her way to the parking lot, but that was out of concern for their friends. Now, in a similar vain, she's electing to stay back out of concern for him. Ryan simply can't wrap his head around why she's being so considerate, so practical. At this point it feels like she's just predicting what he's thinking—like she's seeing a clearer image of himself than he would see in a mirror. 

His hand unconsciously moves to his pistol, but he makes the choice himself to pull it out of its holster. Then, after inspecting the gun for a moment and clicking the safety off, he hands it over to Chloe.

"What are you doing?" she asks, strictly uncertain by the offer.

"Just... point it and shoot if anything happens," he explains, unannounced memories of a can and a foster father flashing through his head, "I won't be long."

Still a bit confused, Chloe hesitantly accepts the gun and holds it as if being given a foreign relic. She stares at the weapon intently for a moment before something silently dawns on her. Then, positioning her hand correctly around the grip, she matches Ryan's gaze with a confident nod. What she was thinking, he'll never know, but it gives him just enough confidence to start walking towards the stadium. He repeats over and over in his head that a fight will not be necessary—not yet.

He sneaks through the back entrance—reverse of how he came out the night before—and tries his best to keep a low profile. With each step, he considers how Drew taught him to avoid unnecessary noise while crouching and how to stay close to corner to listen around before peaking. These small details bug him—because they came from Drew—but he accepts it as wise knowledges and proceeds with caution. 

The final corner of the concrete structure approaches quickly, what lies beyond is anyone's guess. If he recalls correctly, there should be a gate leading to the bottom row of the stands. Everything beyond that should be... well, he's about to find out.

As he reaches the corner, he listens intently to anything that may be awaiting him on the other side. When nothing catches his ear, he peaks his head around gets a clear picture of the field and stands. What he finds makes his stomach churn and his body freeze.

Splattered around the stadium in dense pockets are clusters of blood and dismembered limbs. Fingers, feet, intestines, and countless other extremities litter the stands more than people the night before. The most horrifying detail of it all is that not a single contiguous corpse lies within those puddles of gore. Instead, each pool of blood is connected via a smeared trail leading towards the field. There resting in the center of the stadium is a mountain of mangled corpses. Heads, arms, legs, and everything else is conglomerated into a mass of rotting flesh. In a way, it's an exact replication—multiplied twenty times over—of what he discovered in the zombie woman's basement. The same basement he had to use a drone to descend into. This file of corpses he now casts his eyes on is not through a digital screen but something much more tangible—much more visceral. 

He stumbles backwards with his hand covering his mouth, praying that nothing hears him do so. The sight is so repulsive, the stench so unbearable, the feeling so absorbing, that he genuinely considers running. Running and never coming back. And that's honestly what he should do.

But he doesn't.

Instead of giving in to his instincts and turning tail, he gets back to his feet and creeps forward once more. It'd be so easy to just give up, but Vincenzo told him to make a choice... running away feels like the failure he was referring to. And besides, if Jeremy really is inside that mountain of corpses, he has a responsibility to give him a proper funeral—even if that funeral is nothing more than a mass grave.

Wanting to feel informed, he glances around the stadium one more time in search of any zombies or mutants that could be lurking. If his experience holds true, the zombies guard theses 'hives' in a rather peculiar fashion. Heck, the zombie woman found him before he found it.

His head snaps around and up just to be sure he isn't about to be assaulted by a stealthy zombie. To his relief, nothing waits for him. 

After a thorough inspection of the surroundings, he doesn't find a single zombie, mutant, or anything else for that matter. The stands and field are completely void of any potentially hostile entities. While he doesn't trust this initial determination, it gives him hope that he'll be able to see Jeremy off the right way.

He's about to turn and report back to his friends, when something moves and catches his eyes. Looking back, his gaze lands on the mountain of corpses. At first, he thinks he just hallucinated it, but then it moves again. An arm twitches, a torso rotates, each interaction is separate yet connected by the mass as a whole. The subtle movements don't stop there, however, as the entire mountain soon begins to softly ungulate in place. It's as if something inside of it was moving and activating the muscles of the corpses. Whatever it is, it's massive.

Then, as quickly as it started undulating, the movements stops—completely. The mountain of corpses returns to what it's supposed to be. At least for now. 

Ryan takes a step back his eyes wide. It all makes sense now. What Vincenzo said about "Future tragedy", "resolving a failure", and "a choice" wasn't mere abstract reasoning. He meant it literally. If he doesn't do something now, this... thing... may grow into an abomination that will make his entire life feel like a bedtime story. 

He returns to Chloe stealthily, probably with a morbid look on his face as she purses her lips and holds the gun closely. Without a word exchanged, she offers the weapon back to him and he accepts. Their next destination is to relay the information to the rest of the group.

When they get back, the entire gang is loitering outside the convenience store with some sort of dark bar in hand. They seem rather unconcerned and relaxed given the circumstances but, then again, they don't know what awaits them. Perhaps that's for the best.

"Hey Ryan!" Max calls out waving a box over his head, "Look what we found."

They get a bit closer and the spoils of their search becomes more apparent. Being waved over Max's head is a vibrantly colored box of packaged brownies. Those same brownies are now half-eaten in everyone's hands. 

"Don't worry," Max comments as he sticks his hand into the box, "We saved one for you guys."

The boy proceeds to draw out the last two brownies in the box and tosses them over. The crunch of the wrapper as it hits Ryan's hand is a familiar sensation he forgot he missed. 

"So what's the situation?" Blake asks sincerely.

Ryan pulls the top of the packaging apart, finding that his hands are a tad bit shaky, "It's not good."

"That many?" Blake wonders?

Ryan shakes his head which seems to be intriguing for the whole group, "Just one."

"One?" Caid thinks aloud, "What could be so serious about one zombie?"

Blake stands up and leans against the brick wall behind him, "Not zombie. Mutant."

Beth and Alison exchange confused glances. Caid and Max do something similar but with a more relaxed demeanor. All the while, Ryan gathers the courage to say what needs to be said.

"It's not a mutant," the whole group looks, "at least not yet."

Blake steps off the wall with his arms crossed, "Yet? What do you mean by that?"

In Ryan's mind, this is the point of no return. Does he give in and tell everyone that there is a pile of corpses waiting for them in the stadium? Or does he sugar coat it and try and make a plan without that heavy knowledge? Well, on second thought, what good does it do to withhold crucial knowledge from a group of people who are here to help him.

"There's a mountain of corpses in the stadium," he starts, amassing a wide array of surprised and fearful looks, "I think a really large mutant is about to be born."

Beth crushes her brownie wrapper and stands up, "Ryan are you serious? This isn't a joke right?"

Begrudgingly, he shakes his head.

Caid touches his chin thoughtfully, "So you're saying that inside that baseball stadium is giant mutant about to be born."

"I'm still confused," Max jumps in, "What exactly is a mutant? I remember hearing about it once, but no one has ever explained what they are."

"Yeah," Chloe seconds, "I'm not sure what it is either."

"Mutants are monsters from your nightmares," Blake explains, "They have body parts that don't quite fit their bodies and abilities that... would make you piss your pants."

Beth shakes her arms and takes a step into the middle of the naturally formed circle, "Why haven't I heard about this before? You'd think Michael would tell us about something so important."

Blake takes a deep breath and goes back to leaning against the wall, "Probably didn't want to scare people more than they already were. That mutants we're talking about are pretty rare."

Ryan swallows hard, in his experience, the line between zombie and mutant has become so vague it's hardly even a boundary. He's been operating on the assumption that zombies are just normal people while mutants have all the dangerous abilities, which still holds, but zombies feel sort of rare to him. In recent memory, every 'zombie' has always had some sort of strange mutation that separated them from a typical human. Some 'zombies' had claws or blades for arms or even a really large arm. Often times these 'zombies' didn't have any physical abnormalities, but he could see very disturbing disfigurement in their face and skin. All of these points would lead him to believe that there really is no 'zombie' in the traditional sense, it's simply infected individuals who've succumbed to various levels of mutation. This pile of shaking corpses posses the singular problem with that conclusion. Could zombies somehow combine? Could these piles of corpses create larger abominations... like the one he saw back then?

There's are too many unanswered questions and half of his reasoning is based on conjecture so he hesitates to void it. Honestly, slapping a category and title on it doesn't even do them any good in the short term, they need to focus on how they'll get rid of it before it becomes an unavoidable problem.

"It doesn't matter what it is," Ryan finally voices, "right now it's a pile of corpses and we need to get rid of it... I just don't know how."

The whole group goes quiet. Most people simply don't have any idea what to say or add. As few probably just need some time to think—Ryan included. One person, however, looks around the group cluelessly. 

Max clears his throat with an incredulous look, "You said it's corpses right? Can't you just burn 'em?"
