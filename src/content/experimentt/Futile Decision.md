Arriving back at the prison nearly gets him shot as the guards spare no time in their distant interrogation. Eventually, one of them does recognize his face after a bright flashlight blinds his vision. With that, he and the two vans are allowed to enter into the prison. 

Almost immediately, they're all meet by a curious crowd and a worried Michael. When the hostages eventually file out of their cramped vehicles, the prison's leader is the first one to break through and embrace his wife. Chloe steps out and finds her father while various other hostages embrace one another in gratitude that the horrors are finally over. Blake and Beth find Ryan on his motorcycle, but he's not interested in their curiosity. He has a vendetta to settle and everyone short of that can wait. 

He visually interrogates the crowd which gets him a few strange looks, but doesn't find his target. In light of this, he swings his leg off his bike and storms straight through the crowd and heads inside. A few people follow him in, but he doesn't bother to look back. 

A few voices call out his name and tell him to stop, most of which are the people tailing him, but he disregards it all in search of just one person. He checks the cafeteria—not there. Then the conference room—vacant. He goes so far as to peek through the window to the armory—empty. After that, he's about to double back to the entrance, but then he's stopped by Blake who grabs his shoulder.

"Ryan! Where are you going?" his grip is rather tight overtop his jacket, but Ryan quickly slaps it off.

"Not now," Ryan asserts, "Where's Drew?"

Blake steps back and offers a confused expression. He glances back to Beth, Caid, and Max behind him. They all shake their heads. Then Chloe appears at the end of the hallway and hustles over.

"What's going on?" she asks.

"Where is Drew?!" Ryan repeats very forcefully.

Chloe shakes her head, "I don't know."

When no one gives him a helpful response, he immediately storms off down the hall without a second thought. Multiple people call out his name, but he once again disregards their pleas for explanation.

His next destination is the prison entrance where he'll seek Michael out, hopefully he'll give enough information to track Drew down. He turns the final corner and spots the entrance just a little ways away, but then the bastard himself appears right in front of him. Drew leans against the hallway wall watching the crowd of hostages mingling with the prison survivors.

"Drew!" Ryan exclaims beelining it for him.

Drew turns around and raises an eyebrow, that is, until Ryan grabs the collar of his shirt with both hands and holds him against the wall. Despite Drew's expertise, he seems to allow Ryan to pin him to a wall. His eyes don't even look concerned as Ryan's tell the complete opposite narrative.

"Glad you made it back kid," Drew comments rather casually.

Ryan tightens his grip, "Do you have any idea what happened?"

Drew sighs and lowers his eyes, the faintest hint of regret leaking through, "I do... and I'm sorry. I had no idea it was going to turn out that bad."

Ryan pulls Drew off the wall before slamming him back. Drew just takes it with a brief flash of pain.

"That bad?!" Ryan demands, "You think it was that bad? Why would you even work with that bastard?!"

Blake steps up to try and defuse the situation—as per usual—but he's prevented by Drew holding up a hand to allow their scuffle to resolve on its own. While Ryan finds his actions unavoidable, Drew maintains a completely calm demeanor. 

"I'll admit, my informant let me down, but he's dead now so there's nothing to be done," Drew explains, "Besides, he got me what I needed."

Ryan shakes his head aggressively, "First Steve and now," he takes a forced breath, "and now Jeremy! How many people have to die just to find Heimes."

Drew's eyebrow twitches at the comment, but his front remains unwavering, "I thought I told you to go alone."

Ryan glares at the man and curls his hands deep into his shirt, "And I didn't know you work with scum like Jimmy."

Of all the things that he could say and all the things he could imply, this final comment is what dumbfounds the seasoned veteran. He didn't react to Steve. He hardly shifted at the mention of Jeremy. But Jimmy... Jimmy made him stop. The name alone makes Drew seems to be questioning Ryan's entire argument.

"Wait," Drew says, disassociated from the emotion of the conversation.

Ryan pulls the man backwards and slams him back into the wall, "Don't change the subject."

Drew holds his hands up, "Just so I got this right, my informant was Jimmy."

Reluctantly, Ryan nods with an enraged glare.

With an incredulous look, Drew tilts his head, "Who's Jimmy?"

Suddenly, all the anger, all the hatred, resentment, and scorn that Ryan may have felt, immediately vanishes. Every bone he had to pick with this man suddenly drops from the forefront of his mind.

Jimmy was his informant. He knows it. How did Jimmy seem so confident to give him directions in the stadium? How did he know so much about the situation? Everything points to the conclusion that Jimmy is Drew's informant. It has to be true. It has to. 

But... if Drew doesn't know Jimmy...

Doing a double take, Ryan inspects the man's face for any sign of deception. Surely he'd smile and say that he was testing him and that Jimmy was, in fact, the informant. Surely it's all just some sick joke. Instead of satisfying his desire for resolution, no matter how sadistic, Drew presents only an authentic, unadulterated confusion. The misunderstanding is so clear that not even the best actor in the world could forge the confusion upon this man's face. He truly doesn't know who Jimmy is.

Ryan lets go of Drew and steps back. His mind simultaneously swirling in a catastrophic storm and remaining completely blank. His knees grow weak which requires him to catch himself with the wall. Beside him, his friends all stare in a concerned shock. Not a single one of them understanding the situation. Ryan has never felt more alone while surrounded by people.

"Kid," Drew starts, "Why don't you explain what happened."

While he knows full well what the man said, it doesn't compute in his head. Instead, it becomes some blurred background noise amidst his chaotically silent thoughts. At the same time, Ryan begin walking away down the hall. Some other sporadic noises can be heard far, far away, but that doesn't stop him from trudging forward. The world seems to slow down as he departs from the scene with his feet dragging.

Five grueling minutes pass. No one follows him to his room. No one waits in the halls. Not even the lights are on when he arrives, and he doesn't bother turning them on. He quickly finds himself alone his bed surrounded by ghostly figures all around him. Without even realizing it, his head is in his hands and his eyes stare widely at the ground.

Jimmy. Jimmy Lashing. Is in no way connected to Drew. That means he's either a free agent... or... he knew where the hostages were. He probably knew about that creep. All things considered, he probably knew everything. Everything.

Why does he have to be so stupid? Going alone with a deranged serial killer's instructions? What in the world was he thinking?

Because of his stupid, stupid actions, people are dead—and they're never coming back. Jeremy was finally overcoming himself. His mother could have watched as her introverted son stepped out of his comfort zone and stuck his neck out. After all, Jeremy put himself second to save some random woman. That alone is authentic heroism. The same stubborn boy who got in a fight with a teenage girl about shallow misunderstandings stood up and demanded to put his life on the line for others. It's not just that, Jeremy was showing him something that he was to blind to—progress. And yet, Ryan walked him to that stadium.

Ryan looks to the dark silhouettes of his hands. They each shake softly as he holds them against his knees. That injection inducted a pain he's never experienced before, and it couldn't be bothered to linger long enough for him to understand it. And now his hands... they're shaking... just like when he... he lifted the knife.

He squeezes his eyes closed and holds his head once again. Innocent screaming. Jimmy smiling. The syringe. "We'll laugh about this one day." Blood on his face. The raven mask. A plead for release. Jeremy smiling in the classroom.

It's all—it's all... the Jacobsons, a hanging corpse, a dead dog, mangled teenagers scattered across a hallway, a dead friend, a dead family... Steve. Why does it always come back to Steve?

Once upon a time, he hated the man... so much. He couldn't stand how his narcissistic narrative always pushed others—pushed himself—down. And yet, that poor excuse for a man saved Ryan's life. If it weren't for Steve, he'd be... if it weren't for Steve none of this would have happened. He wouldn't have met back up with the other high schoolers and endured so much pain. He would have been freed from this world... it would have been easier. 

But that's not true. Steve knew what the bottom of a pit felt like and did everything he could to save Ryan from that world. Yet, if he only knew how much this pit hurt, he would have allowed Ryan the easy way out. To watch people die over and over. To reach for some sort of hope but find nothing to cling on to. This is not what he wanted when he decided to drop out of school. This is not at all what he wanted.

What's it going to take to escape this nightmare? What's it going to take to make up for his mistakes? Everything he does just ends in more death. Why won't it end?!

Steve, Bill, Karmen, Charlie, Jeremy, Amber, the Jacob... other teenagers... the birthday girl... That's right... he turned eighteen a few weeks ago. Karmen was going to throw him a party—his first since his mother died. There would be cake, and albeit small, at least there would be three people there who actually invested their time in him. Maybe, if he was lucky, Steve would have even allowed a present to be given. 

His cheeks grow hot.

He brushed off Karmen's offer so casually. Instead of thanking her for her consideration, he just walked up to his room and drowned the world out in some trifling music. If only he had just let the birthday party happen, maybe then he could have felt like a kid again—just one more time. Ever since his mother died, he'd convinced himself that he'd always been alone. It wasn't true. He'd just realized that too late.

Clenching his fist, he can feel something warm land on the tops of his knuckles, though he can't see what it is.

All he ever wanted was to stop hurting. All he wanted was for someone, anyone to save him from a world where he didn't belong. Yet even now, he's stuck... stuck in this same hellhole that's been brutalizing his existence. The only difference now is that he was only hurting himself before, now he's getting innocent people killed. Every single time he's tried to do anything, it's gotten people killed. It's not even his fault, what was he supposed to do?

He pulls out his pistol from its holster and feels its cold surface underneath his rough fingers. 

In all this time, this awful weapon has been the only common denominator. He received a gun in the school and ever since then people have been dying nonstop. Society was normal up until this stupid weapon feel into his lap. If he could go back and shove the gun right back in the janitor's face, he would, without a second thought... but what's done is done... there's no taking it back. 

The pistol slides out of his hand and clanks against the ground. Perhaps if the safety wasn't on, he might even have at risk of getting shot by it... if there was even a round left inside.

That weapon may have brought carnage all around him, but thanks to Steve, it never once damaged him. 

Almost as a reflex, his hand draws his secondary weapon—his knife. The smooth metal is heavy in his shaking hands. His fingertips sliding gently as a mixture of two liquids presses between his hand and the knife. By the time he makes sense of what's occurring in the dark, he finds the tip of the blade a fraction of an inch away from his throat.

All he's good at is surviving. Monsters, horrors, death, despair, he gets through it all. Survival is no longer a question, it has become a given... and that given is costing people around him. He survives a cultist—no one else does. He survives a bloodbath—no one else does. He stares death in the face and walks away alive... but how many people lay dead just because he lived. The pattern is so clear, yet it makes him want to scream. 

His grip tightens on the knife. 

There's still a solution. There's still one more thing he can do.

His eyes close. He breathes in slowly. He waits. Nothing ever comes. 

Then, in the silence of the dark, he opens his eyes at the shadowy snowflakes hanging over him. Their gentle forms reminding him of clouds hanging in the sky in all their white, fluffy radiance. He used to watch the clouds with his mother and point out all the shapes he could spot. 

As the knife clanks against the cement floor, Ryan leans forward with gritting teeth. 

No matter what he tells himself, he just can't do it. His arms simply won't move. This body he's been trapped in for eighteen years has come to the determination that instinct takes precedent. Even if his mind refuses, his body won't.

He was right. He's become too good a surviving. Even far enough to survive himself.

Ryan isn't able to get a wink of sleep as he lies on his back with one foot hanging off the side of the bed. The pitch black room offers his mind room to run wild as nothing dares bother him. This solitude ultimately induces an intense bout of insomnia which cannot be overcome no matter how desperately he closes his eyes. Even if he did finally fall asleep, he knows only a nightmare would be waiting for him. With that in mind, he simply allows his thoughts to simmer as he remains motionless.

Minutes drag long into hours with virtually no change. Various unrelated thoughts bounce in and out of his stream of consciousness, but overall things seem to mellow down to ordinary thoughts like what he'll do tomorrow, and what he should eat for breakfast—he is starving after all. It'd be lie to say the darker things don't creep in every now and again, but after something like three hours of lying on his back, he feels as if he's calmed down for the most part. 

This restless sleep is finally interrupted when footsteps echo down the hallway. He looks up expecting the hall light to flicker on, but that never occurs as a far stranger lighting approached. A dim sort of light, only bright enough to fill a single room, maybe a tidbit more. It's aura of light approaches slowly as the footsteps get louder and louder until the source appears before his door.

Illuminated by the light of a small gas powered hand lantern is the obsidian colored garments of a raven masked man. His prominent bolero procures a perfectly circular shadow on the wall behind him as he nods his head at Ryan.

"Greetings," he says softly, as if to respect the quiet atmosphere, "It's been a while my friend."

Ryan studies him cautiously. On one hand, he should be grateful for everything this mysterious man has done for him, but on the other hand, he can't help but shake the feeling that he's behind some of it. Those conflicting narratives make his head spin and that's the last thing he wants right now.

The raven masked man raises the lantern a bit to get a better view of Ryan's morbid visage, "Hm..."

Ryan doesn't dignify the investigation with a response.

"I apologize for being frank," the man begins, "but I'm not well versed in comforting another. I regrettably cannot repair you this time."

Ryan shakes his head. He doesn't need to be fixed... he just needs some time, "It's alright."

The man lowers the lantern back to a more comfortable position, "At the very least, I do want to commend you for your efforts. You've proven yourself to be a far stronger bo—" he pauses mid-sentence, "far stronger man than anyone could have known."

Feeling as if history is about to repeat itself, Ryan turns away, "Don't try and flatter me, I know what I'm worth... for better or for worse."

"Humble I see," the man musses, "don't let that become false humility, though."

Ryan glances back with a distant confusion, "False humility?"

Chuckling to himself, the man's free hand folds behind his back, "Yes, false humility is neglecting your objective capabilities in light of a facade that demeans your self-worth. You are far from perfect, but expecting perfection would be like a fish expecting to climb a tree."

The philosophical expression makes sense to Ryan, but he can't bring himself to engage with it any further than just hearing it. He has no interest in supporting or defending his position.

"Do you know why I came?" the man asks very simply after a brief lull in the one-sided conversation.

Ryan shakes his head.

"It's because I'd like to give you my name."

Ryan looks up, for the first time, his interest is genuine. What could be so important about a name?

"Wha... why?" he autocorrects.

The man switches the hand holding the lantern, "Names are a powerful thing. They remove abstraction and ground an existence. A wise man once said that every person dies twice: after their last breath, and after their name has been spoken for the last time. By offering you my name, I hope it will lead you to a greater understanding of the world around you."

Ryan looks down at the ground, "With... just a name?"

The man nods his head, "Yes, you may even find my name useful. Perhaps not yet, but in due time."

Looking back up, Ryan shoots the man with a weakly skeptical look, "That won't change anything."

The man shakes his head, "I said when I arrived that I wasn't here to repair you. My name won't change anything for your immediately predicament."

Leaning forward and pressing his arms against his knees, Ryan takes a deep breath. The conversation is simply too high level for him right now, he feels like he's being offered a dollar but doesn't have a wallet to store it.

"What am I even supposed to do then? It's just..." Ryan struggles to articulate, "Steve can't save me this time."

"Ryan," the man says directly and without remorse, "Please look at me."

Begrudgingly, Ryan looks up to find the same, frightening mask with two inky eyes, so dark that he could fit a galaxy in them and still not be able to see to the other side.

"Who are you?" the man asks simply.

Ryan's thoughts waver as the comment catches him in a loop. It's such a simple question—trivial even—that he can't find the words to respond right away. Is he asking for name... or is he asking for something deeper?

When he doesn't respond, the man takes it upon himself to reinforce the question, "I'd like to hear you say it."

"Ryan..." he starts, almost out of habit, before lowering his voice and looking down.

"No," the man responds immediately.

"Ryan Lancer?" Ryan tries, desperate to just end the conversation.

"That's not it," The man folds his arm back behind his back, "I asked who you are, no what you're called."

Looking up, he once again, finds the unwavering gaze of the enigma, "What do you mean? How am I even supposed to know the answer to that?"

Ryan still feels like his response is quite matter-of-factly and struggles to comprehend what this man could be insinuation. That being said, in the back of his mind, he does wonder what those words mean. What does it mean to have a name? Where does the name Lancer even come from? And... who is he? All of these questions seemingly have no paths to a solid answer.

The man leans forward slightly, "Every time we speak, you strike me as someone who constantly asks: What can I do? But never: Who am I? If you, yourself cannot answer it, then what is the relevance of a name? ...because of this, I shall offer you my name, and with it, you may come to understand just who I am."

"Who you are...?" Ryan whispers to himself, more as an echo than an actual thought.

"You will never get back the people you've lost," the man begins, "and it would be the epitome of ignorance to believe you can save everyone. Instead, you can proceed back to the stadium and resolve your failure before it becomes a future tragedy. Your grief may never go away, but you still have the opportunity to preserve your future. That is the choice that you can make."

Ryan stares at him in disbelief, "The... the stadium..."

The man nods, "You've survived the impossible on multiple occasions. So do not ask who will die if you do nothing, instead ask who will survive if you do something. Returning to the stadium is not a command, it is a choice that you must make for yourself... and I believe that you have come to realize the importance of a choice. And through your choices, you may understand who you are."

"So... that means..." Ryan fumbles on his words, not really sure where he's going.

The man takes a step away from the door, "Now, I shall let you be. It will be some time until we meet again, so I wish you the best."

Nothing comes out of Ryan's mouth as he watches the man turn to leave.

Before he departs into the dark hallway, he turns back to Ryan with only his mask, "When we undoubtably meet again, you may refer to me as Vincenzo... Farwell."

With that, the remainder of the night is returned to the ambient creaking of the prison.
