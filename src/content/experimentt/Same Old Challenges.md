The following day starts like any other. Blake and Ryan wake up at the rising sun with only Beth awake before them. The three of them converse briefly while they eat left overs for breakfast and then the two men prepare to set off for the day. Beth warns them about some wolf tracks that Caid found the day before, but they don’t worry too much about it. Ryan continues to carry his knife and pistol while Blake carries a pistol of his own, but that was just basic security these days. Before long, they are out the door and heading towards the town ready to try out this plan of Blake’s.

“So how is this operation supposed to work?” Ryan asks as they wade through a drift of snow.

Blake hops over the peak of the drift and lands on solid concrete on the other side, “I really need to check out the scene again, but if everything works out, we can sneak by and light the place on fire.”

"But what about the combustion problem?"

Blake shakes his finger at Ryan as he agrees with what he said, "That was the problem, but then I remembered something. Remember how we burned that pile in the stadium a few months ago?"

“Of course… wait,” Ryan halts as he questions his friend, “You’re not talking about gasoline, are you?”

“That’s right,” Blake responds as he keeps walking down the road.

Ryan jogs a few steps to keep up pace, “Even if we do have gasoline, we can’t get close to the building to dump it and light it. That's the other problem.”

Blake looks over with a little smirk, “Who ever said we were going to walk up?”

Ryan looks down at the side of the road and notices the frozen carcass of a dead coyote. Its fur is perfectly preserved under a bit of snow and ice. There doesn’t seem to be any open wounds on the animal, so it must have died of hypothermia. The poor creature never had a chance in these freezing temperatures.

“What do you mean?” Ryan finally asks.

Blake glances back at him, “The museum is in the middle of town. We can travel on the rooftops until we get there. Then we light the fire on the roof and spread it.”

“Hm,” Ryan thinks aloud.

Blake gives him an incredulous look, “What? You don’t think it will work?”

“No,” Ryan argues, “I think it could work, but we’ll need a lot of gasoline to get that place lit. The problem is actually getting it since the pumps are all out of order.”

Blake kicks a stick out of his path as he keeps walking, "Then it's a good thing that Max showed us how to siphon gas directly from vehicles. Come on, I saw a gas can up ahead."

As Blake predicted, a red gas can sits next to a semi truck at the only gas station this side of town. Unsurprisingly, it's empty, but that is when Blake pulls out two thin tubes from his backpack. He grabs the gas can and the tubes and walks over to a broken down car. The windows of the car are shattered and signs of a long forgotten struggle are present, but this isn’t the important thing to Blake. He pulls a lever under the steering wheel and the fueling lid pops open. He then unscrews the lid revealing the car’s tank of gas. It’s faint, but he can smell the recognizable scent of gas inside. Blake places both tubes inside the car’s gas tank and then puts the other end of one of the tubes inside the red gas can. After that, he puts a cloth over the hole to the car’s gas can and then blows into the second tube. Almost immediately, gas begins to flow out through the first tube and into the red gas can. The fuel slowly stops flowing through the tube when the can is a little over half full. Blake then pulls out the tubes and cloth and pushes them back into his backpack while picking up the gas can.

"Alright, let's head to the next one."

They end up doing the siphoning thing one more time to fill up the gas can but after that, it's straight to the museum. 

Due to the cold and their very nature, zombies and mutants remain indoors at all times only leaving very briefly if something disturbs them outside. As far as Blake and Ryan are concerned, there is nothing in the city as nothing even bothers to look out a window as they walk down the snowy streets in silence. Sporadically, Ryan glances at a window and sees the afterimage of a strange vase, but it always disappears when he blinks a few times.

People are conditioned to expect streets to be plowed fairly quickly after snow falls, so a snow filled city with a thin layer of snow sitting on every road and sidewalk is almost foreign to Ryan. Traveling is easy enough and the signs remain uncovered with the exception of a few ice sickles, but the strange feeling of a unique landscape persists as they walk down street after street. Eventually, they arrive at their destination.

At the end of a line of buildings is an old, rustic one-story building made entirely out of wood. It’s the type of building that was probably constructed in the late 1800’s but remains standing as a living history museum to detail the town's interesting past during the cowboy era. It can’t be seen from where they are standing, but both men know that a very dense population of zombies and mutants hide inside. They happened to stumble upon it a few weeks ago and nearly died escaping the horde chasing them away. Luckily, they didn’t pursue them very far as their bodies are not conditioned to survive the cold temperatures of the winter. The attackers quickly returned back to their nest shortly after leaving. 

Blake bumps Ryan on the side of the arm with his hand and points at the roof of the buildings close to the museum. The chain of buildings are all one-story buildings of separate small businesses, but Blake specifically points at a particular two-story inn a few buildings down from the museum.

“We’ll get to the roof by climbing out that window,” Blake explains quietly as he begins to head towards it. “We’ll have to clear it as we go through.”

They journey around another snow drift before arriving at the inn just a few buildings away from the museum. The lack of windows hides the sight of any zombies and no ambient noise would indicate a hostile presence but there's no guarantees these days.

Ryan and Blake exchange glances with each other as they both draw their melee weapons to clear the building. Although he only registers Blake's pistol as a weapon, he forgets that the guy carries a survival knife too. Ryan, on the other hand, takes out his much more menacing knife. Their usual method for clearing buildings is for one person to remain in the hallway monitoring their exit route while the other searches a room one at a time eliminating any zombies or otherwise. Most of the time every room is clear, but in the case that something hostile is in the building their strategy ensures that every room is taken care of while they still have the option to run if they stumble upon a mutant they can’t defeat. 

They open the door to the inn and head down the hallway very quietly checking each doors' lock status. Every single one on the bottom floor is locked so they advance up the stairs to the second floor. Usually they would be sure to open every door, but given that they don’t want to make a lot of noise they opt to skip that step and only check ones that could easily open behind them. The situation remains the same upstairs with all locked doors so they eventually arrive at the window at the end of the dimly lit hallway.

The window luckily has the capacity to be opened, but a pesky lock sits on the windowsill. It's not a combination or key lock that he normally finds on fences and doors, instead it's some sort of locking mechanism built into the window itself. He would try to figure out how it works, but he already knows of a particular shortcut to do the work for him. Lining his knife up with the lock, he quickly slides right through it and pulls the window up without further issue.

Without a word, Blake sets the gas can on the floor and climbs onto the windowsill. The drop to the ground below is quite a steep one, a survivable one, but not a pleasant one. He doesn’t waste time icing himself for the jump so he goes ahead and makes the leap to the next building. For a moment, he hangs in the air seemingly floating before gravity does its job and pulls him down, thankfully, right on the roof of the next building. 

Ryan grabs the gas can and tosses it out the window to Blake after he gets his footing and prepares to receive it. He then takes his turn jumping across, easily landing on the roof as his friend did. 

They arrive at the next break in the buildings and use the same maneuver with Blake jumping over first followed by Ryan throwing the gas and then jumping over himself. This process repeats two additional times before they arrive on the roof of the museum itself. 

Upon landing on the roof, they make absolutely certain to remain as quiet as possible so they don’t disturb the zombies below. Despite knowing that a horde of zombies lurks inside, the exterior of the building is eerily quite. In fact, the slight wind provides more pressing audio than anything emanating from the building. 

The roof of the museum is covered in a thin layer of snow but he can feel underfoot that he’s standing upon a wooden structure which is perfect. An unused heating unit sits across the way and a few other utilities scatter the top of the building but the most peculiar thing there is placed near the front of the building. Ryan slowly and carefully approaches the hole in the roof to see a crowd of twitching zombies below. He doesn’t care to get too close, but he can tell clear as day that there are over ten just within a few feet of the door. It seems that the zombies are huddling together like penguins to remain warm. The curious thing is why they would select a building with a hole in it? 

Blake removes the cap of the gas can and begins pouring it out little by little trying to hit dry spots on the roof. Any gabs between dry spots are swept open with his foot as he creates a network of interconnect patches of liquid. Half the can is emptied before Ryan stops him with an idea.

Ryan leans over pushing his mouth right next to Blake’s ear and whispering as softly as possible, “Give me the matches and the rest of the gas. I have an idea.”

Blake is immediately stricken with skepticism, but after a moment of thought, he caves and hands the can over to Ryan. He then reaches into his backpack and pulls out a small box of matches before handing them over to Ryan as well. After that, Blake inspects Ryan hesitantly before taking his leave by hopping back to the roof of the neighboring building.

Now alone on the dangerous roof, Ryan takes his spontaneous plan into action by lighting a match and dropping it on the pools of gas that Blake had just poured out. Immediately the liquid ignites and fire erupts over the pools of gas. The wood doesn’t catch very quickly due to the cold, but it looks like it should work eventually. This now gives Ryan the time to try out something crucially important for them to exterminate the zombies. He heads to the hole in the roof.

Standing close to the gaping hole overlooking an ocean of undead unbeknownst of him. He spots countless individuals who still maintain a most human appearance, but scattered among them are just as many morphed figures with grotesque parts. Some have disfigured faces while others have extra appendages or weapon looking growths along their body. It’s simply horrifying to imagine the pain that those innocent souls had to go through to get to this state. They’re all dead, but that doesn’t change the fact that many of them still have the twisted looks of pure agony and distress solidified upon their crooked visages. 

Ryan takes the can of gas and begins to pour it down on the zombies closest to the front door. The undead corpses below feel the liquid dropping on them and look up to see a fresh, healthy chunk of food waiting to be ripped apart. They begin to rush together even resorting to climbing on each other to attempt to reach up to him. This attempt is all in vain as Ryan reaches the end of his gas and he quickly lights a match and tosses it down. Sadly the match goes out as it falls so he instead pulls out a clump of three matches and lights them together dropping all of them down into the growing pile of rotting flesh. This time the fire takes causing the screams of damned to ring out. It's disheartening to think this sound is not unfamiliar to him, but he tries to perish the thought as he heads over to where Blake is waiting impatiently. 

"Come on!" Blake commands, "Get gotta get out of here."

Dismissively, Ryan nods his head and arrives at the edge of the building. He prepares to jump but then something catches his eyes, something important enough to halt his efforts. In his peripheral vision he spots something moving along the outside museum wall and he instinctively jumps back. Like a flash of lighting a mutant flies right in front of his face and lands a mere inches away from him.

The ugly mutant has a humanoid face with a contorted look of agony like the others. One jarring detail that draws his attention are two blood, gouged out eyes. Beyond that, its mouth hangs open with spread out, very yellow teeth. An elongated tongue hangs out of its mouth with a mind of its own as it waves up and down. With backward-bending limbs the mutant stand on all fours like a spider. Lastly, a scorpion tail curls from the base of the tailbone to just above its morphed head.

Ryan only manages to analyze the creature so thoroughly as it stares at him in silence for a moment.

Drawing his knife, he notices that the tail is dripping in some potent green substance. He braces to act, but the mutant lunges with startling speed. There isn't even time to act as the mutant headbutts him in the abdomen and sends him flying through a line of fire. Thankfully he lands on the other side of the flames instead of the midst of it. Blake shouts out his name but he's unable to respond to it as the mutant vaults over the fire and lands right next to him.

He swings his knife right at the mutant's head, taking the risk of the tail. To his dismay, it misses completely as the mutant rears up on its hind legs and proceeds to shove Ryan backwards. The snow doesn't trip him up as he backpedals a few steps, but the edge of the building does. Before he can catch himself, the mutant lurches forward and seals his fate as he falls backwards off the building.

A one-story fall isn't typically lethal, but if he were to land on his back, it might as well be. Luckily, a small drift of snow cushions his impact as he thumps on the ground. He groans as he can feel weight of the impact forcing all the air out of his lungs. This isn't the first time he's gotten the wind knocked out of him, but that doesn't make it easier. Pair that with the fact that he'll likely have some pretty severe bruising, if not worse, and it figures that he'd begin to question what's to come. 

The pains and anxieties quickly take a back seat as the mutant jumps off the building straight towards him. 

He rolls out of the way which ends up being a clunkier endeavor as the snow blocks his movements. That being said, he narrowly avoids impalement as the mutant's tail pierces into the snow instead of him. This miss allows him just enough time to scramble to his feet.

The flames of the museum burst through a window as a flaming zombie attempts to slip through but gets caught on the glass. Furthermore, a wave of zombies starts banging on the front door but finds that it has been frozen shut. 

Ryan turns back to the mutant, reaching for his pistol.

The mutant doesn't continue its charge, however, as it faces him and proceeds to remain stationary for some inexplainable reason. Suddenly, its head begins to shake violently as it continues to hold position. The convulsing mutant nearly falls over amidst its aggressive movements, but this bout only lasts a few seconds. When the writhing subsides, it's replaced with a calmer, much more controlled demeanor. It begins to move its disturbing mouth with yellowed teeth only for a sound to exit out. Its voice is hoarse at first, but slowly shifts into a much more mature, civil sound with a demonic undertone. The voice still shakes as if struggling to maintain control, yet it still rattles his soul with each passing word. 

"Why are you still alive?"

Ryan takes a few cautious steps down the street away from the museum while still facing the suddenly civil mutant before him.

"No matter how many times I try and kill you. You won't listen. You don't have much longer before it's too late."

Ryan’s body trembles in some strange sense of fear. He nearly forgot about the fact that there were talking mutants besides Heimes. In fact, this is actually the third time he's interacted with one of these things. It would be great if he could stop and have a casual conversation with it, but this is hardly the time nor place. Besides, he can't imagine a future where they actually get alone in a civil manner.

"Who are you?" Ryan demands, "Why do you keep telling me this?"

Ryan takes a few more steps putting even more distance between them. The talking mutant shifts slightly to keep its head facing Ryan. Its back feet take a step to the side to rotate its body making Ryan shiver at the abnormal sight of twisted legs moving.

"You must die before it's too late," the mutant repeats, "Die."

"Why!" Ryan shouts, "Tell me!"

The mutant stops repeating itself and instead falls away to silence. The gouged out eyes seem to stare him down in some crooked, grotesque way.

"Because," it eventually concludes, a dark green liquid dripping out the corners of its mouth, "if you don't. You never will."

Ryan freezes up for a moment under the gravity of what it could be implying. Honestly, he doesn't have the faintest clue what it means, but the hair on the back of his neck says otherwise. Never die? It sounds like a sick joke, yet the mutant doesn't even smirk.

Feeling that he has no other option, he pulls out his pistol and takes a shot right at the mutants head. Immediately the civility in its demeanor vanishes as green blood squirts out of it's eye sockets. To avoid the incoming bullet it perpendicular to him with blazing speed; the bullet doesn't even skim the creature. It then launches itself off the wall rearing, its tail for another impaling assault. Ryan dives out of the way. The mutant's tail misses but it's spider-like arms don't as they pin him to the ground. He tries to slide his knife arm free from underneath him, but it's no use, the monster's arms are blocking all his movements accept his pistol holding arm. 

He only really has one option left at this point with his stomach on the ground, so he bends his arm and attempts to aim his gun behind his back. The mutant can clearly see his futile attempt, but thoughts don't really grace either of them at the moment. He fires the gun and the mutant gracefully dodges its head with green blood being flung onto the snow beside him.

Trying to reposition his gun behind his back becomes a very difficult endeavor as he can't see where the monster's center of mass is, but its all he got. Before he can take his next shot, a stray bullet rings out from somewhere else. This impact lands true into the mutant's torso causing it to rear back just enough for Ryan's knife arm to slip free.

Using all of his strength, he slams the point of the blade into the side of the mutant's head piercing it with ease. He follows it up by ripping the weapon free of the creatures bony visage and slashing its tail which had been meandering just in reach. After both strikes make their mark and the mutant begins gurgling on it's own fluids, Ryan rolls over to push the stunned monster off of him. The creature shakes violently for a few moments before going completely still.

“Thanks for that,” Ryan says to Blake as he gets off the ground slowly and brushes some snow off of him.

The burning museum reeks of death as zombies inside bang on the walls to escape. Thankfully, there are not many windows on the old-timey museum and all that are there are small enough to block the escape of zombies. The front door, however, presents a bit of a problem as the ice seems to be mostly melted and the intense banging on it's wooden surface is reaching it's breaking point.

“No problem,” Blake responds, nudging the dead mutant with his foot, "I've never seen on do that."

The zombie that got stuck on the glass earlier shakes as a group of zombies behind it attempt to struggle out the broken window but can’t fit past the dead zombie impaled by glass and fire. For a moment, Ryan could swear that he hears human chatter in that inferno begging him to let them out, but he doesn’t accept that to be any more than deception. 

"Well it seemed to work," Ryan comments, keeping a close eye on the front door.

Blake nods as he looks back, "Yeah, I only got the idea because I noticed the front door was frozen over. They were basically trapped in there to begin with."

Ryan starts taking a few steps away as his places his hands to his face and blows warm air into them creating a small pocket of heat for his nose, "Let's wait around, just in case any of them try and get out." 

"Agreed," Blake says as he points to a nearby thrift store, "Let's head in there to get out of the cold."

---

“I wish it were summer already,” Ryan complains as he sits under a blanket they found in the thrift store.

They each occasionally glance out the front display windows to see the burning building across the street. So far, nothing has made it out and the banging on the front door has subsided, but they still don't want to take any chances and would rather know ahead of time if anything dangerous escaped.

Blake sits against the opposing wall with only his usual winter clothes on. As he gazes out the window, Ryan finds him lost in a trance.

Ryan can't help but smile a bit. While Chloe is definitely the easiest to talk to, Blake would probably be a moderately close second. He'd normally stay quite at a moment like this, but he finds himself in a strangely talkative mood after such a success, “Yo, you still with me?”

Blake shakes his head and returns his attention to his friend, “Sorry, what’d you say?”

Ryan rolls his eyes and leans back against a pile of clothes he bunched together. “You seem to be doing that a lot these days.”

“Doing what?” Blake responds, completely oblivious to something Ryan finds pretty obvious.

“You stare off into the distance like you’re contemplating life’s meaning.”

Blake shrugs, “Maybe I am.”

Ryan sighs and shifts a bit on his pile of clothes ranging from sweaters to gym shorts. It isn’t much but it's comfortable enough to warrant him leaning on it. If anything, he's more worried that he'll fall asleep on it once he warms up.

“Without that hive, we can take advantage of the situation while it's still cold,” Ryan thinks out loud, “We could probably bring everyone out to a restaurant and bring the backup generator. Max could cook us an actually decent meal… Don’t you think?”

Blake remains uncharacteristically silent, “Are you happy?”

Ryan smiles like it's a joke, but Blake doesn’t even chuckle at the comment and remains dead serious, "What... what do you mean?"

Blake pulls out his survival knife and looks at the sharp blade. A few imperfections can be easily spotted on the blade. Blake’s eyes appear saddened by the sight. 

"I... I thought I'd be happier," Blake looks up at Ryan with a face verging between contemplation and confusion, "Getting rid of this hive was my idea. And we pulled it off perfectly. Sure you got attacked, but we both made it out unscathed. It was a good plan."

Ryan rubs his back, the impact from the fall still causing him a bit of discomfort but he's just glad it wasn't any worse.

“That’s just it,” Blake adds quickly, “Now that we’ve finally done it, I expected to feel fulfilled… like we did something, but all I feel is that same feeling you get when you get home after a long day. It’s just another day. Do you know what I mean?”

Ryan lowers his head and hugs himself to stay a bit warmer, "Yeah, I do actually."

"And..." Blake starts a thought but trails off as he averts his gaze, "Never mind."

Despite the start, Ryan doesn't feel the need to press. Blake can say whatever he needs to say and that's enough. Being here with him after a success if far more than Ryan probably deserves. It's reassuring to have someone around, especially when he's personally trying to grapple with what that sentient mutant was saying. Unfortunately, it's not his first encounter with a talking mutant, but the situation is only made more concerning because the enigmatic entity repeats the same thing verbatim every time: die. It's not even in a threatening way, instead it's almost pleading with him to just give up. The discrepancy makes his stomach churn.

"Uh... Ryan," Blake asks unconfidently, "Can I ask you something?"

Ryan looks up with a nod.

"Do you think... I'm doing a good job? Leading people, I mean."

Ryan mulls over the question carefully as to not misunderstand what is being asked. To him the immediate answer seems obvious, but he wants to sanity check himself in case there's some subtext he's missing. When nothing comes to mind, he just answers truthfully, "Yeah, I trust you."

Blake purses his lips, not quite heartened by the comment, but clearly affected by it. He leans his head back and looks up at the ceiling, "What if I told you that I hate it."

"Hate what?" Ryan says with a quizzical look.

With a drawn out delay, Blake sighs deeply, "My father once told me that responsibility take's precedence over feelings. He was a doctor so I'm not surprised he said that... Anyway, he's the reason I joined the student council to begin with. He's also the reason I stepped up when everything went down."

"And we needed that," Ryan adds, trying his best to sound supportive.

Blake shakes his head slowly, "Maybe you're right. Maybe my role was to take the initiative and keep as many people alive as possible... but... I never once, not even for a second, wanted to do it. I didn't want to be in charge."

Ryan stays quite this time. Even if he wanted to say something, he doesn't have a clue where to begin.

"I'm just tired," Blake continues as he leans forward and places his knuckles against his forehead, "I don't want to do this anymore."

Feeling conflicted, Ryan looks out the window. On one hand he completely gets it, truly. It'd be disingenuous to say he didn't feel exhausted by it all. Heck, he's seen so much death and destruction that he'd be crazy not to feel that way. Twice before he even considered quitting completely. That being said, another part of him felt the exact opposite way. He actually enjoys their simply life, despite the distinct difficulties. He's come to appreciate the people around him, even if there still is, and will frankly always be, a bit of distance between some of them. Before the apocalypse he had no one but himself, so there exists a side of him which is thankful for everything. Even if that gratitude came at a cost he can hardly bear.

As Blake's final comment taints the room with an unsettling atmosphere, he elects to stand up and walk over to the end of their aisle, "I'm sorry... I, err, kind of laid it on heavy there."

Ryan shakes his head, even though Blake's no longer looking at him, "It's fine," his thoughts immediately brush up with the last time he felt the urge to say something and didn't. The aftermath of that still shakes him to the core and demands that he not repeat that mistake, "just know that you're not alone."

Blake glances back slightly, his eyes a bit wider than they were before.

Ryan looks away, "I wish I could have said that a long time ago."

For a moment, his friend looks almost touched by those words. His eyes soften and and his shoulders ease up. Ryan genuinely feels a wave of relief wash over him as it seems his words were actually beneficial to someone he cares about. This feeling doesn't last long, however, as Blake's brief repose is gradually overtaken by a woeful look in his eyes. His eyebrows crease and his head lowers all while his back turns completely on Ryan.

"That's what everyone else said," Blake admits quietly, "then Mr. Heimes got to them."

Ryan watches in silence as his friend departs from the building without another word.
