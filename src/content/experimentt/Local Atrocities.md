When Ryan eventually gets himself off the pile of miscellaneous clothes, he finds the subsequent ruefully awakening. His little bit of warmth developed underneath the blanket and beside the clothes is instead placed with an icy draft that makes him shiver. The soreness in his back only the makes the transition worse.

He steps outside and is thankfully greeted with a silent nod from Blake. Cross armed and leaning against the front of the store, Blake can't bring himself to match Ryan's gaze. Even so, the two of them managed to depart without any exchange of words.

They both pass by the gas station as Ryan looks up at the pretty blue sky. It isn't an especially good day for cloud watching as there are no cumulous clouds and only cirrus clouds—categories he only remembers from a chart he saw three years ago. The former produces the pretty shapes that he finds himself drawn to while the latter are simply wisps with no definite form. He finds himself oddly humored by the unsolicited trivia he just so happens to recall.

“We should probably plan out where to head next before this snow melts,” Blake observes as he gauges the average depth of the snow underfoot. “I know it's dangerous, but I think we should head towards a bigger city. I know we haven't explored much of the town, but I don't like our odds of finding anyone here. A bigger city is more likely to have survivors.”

Ryan nods his head, "Yeah, probably."

Blake is about to say something but then stops himself. He returns to walking as if nothing had ever crossed his mind in the first place.

Ryan looks down at the snow crunching underfoot. Each step leaves an imprint of his passing; a temporary recounting of history. With a brief glance back, he imagines how long this history will remain before it vanishes without a trace. He closes his eyes for a moment and recounts their little voyage from the thrift store, such a trivial endeavor, yet he can't seem to stop thinking about it for some reason.

It’s only then that Ryan realizes Blake is no longer following beside him. He turns to find Blake staring forward in disbelief. Ryan twists around to look at what he was seeing only to find a small human looking figure down the road from them. The person stands probably four feet tall wearing a pink coat with a red stocking cap and pink snow pants. A couple of strands of brown hair stick out from under the hat. It's a kid.

Suddenly the girl makes a break for it through an alleyway between two brick buildings. Blake breaks out into a sprint towards her with Ryan following close behind.

“Wait!” Blake yells as they turn the corner into the alleyway.

They don't find the girl around the corner so they hustle down the claustrophobic space until they exit into a backyard with the barrel of a shotgun pointed straight at them.

A well-built man of about thirty stands holding a large double barreled shotgun with the eyes of an angry momma bear prepared to kill for her cub. His face is covered in a thick black beard and his posture informs them that he's not willing to bluff. Behind the man stands a woman hugging the little girl. A boy about the girls' age stands off to the side of the two and finally another man a few years younger than the first stands by the wayside with a baseball bat in hand. A wool scar covers most of the face of the second man but out of everyone he looks the most surprised by their arrival. He quickly joins the first as Ryan and Blake quietly analyze the situation.

“We’re not here to hurt anyone,” Blake tries with his hands up.

By the look of worry in the woman’s face and the uncertainty of the second man, it’s clear that no one really wants to draw blood here. Sharp ferocity glows around the first man as his eyes narrow. He wouldn't call it bloodlust, but he's the only person in the group who isn't sharing the same fearful expression.

“We’re not going back!” the first man says with the utmost hostility. The little girl finds the man’s tone startling as she squeezes tightly onto the woman.

“I don’t know what you’re talking about,” Blake admits, trying to seem as passive as possible.

The man isn't having it, “You know damn well what’s going on. We’re leaving and you can’t convince us otherwise.”

Blake and Ryan nervously exchange glances before Blake continues to ease some of the tension with honest and sincere words.

“We’re not from around here. We thought everyone in this town was dead so when we saw someone we thought—”

“Go!” the man shouts.

The second man shakes the first's shoulder nervously. "Keep your voice down, otherwise they'll be drawn to us."

The first man doesn’t let his eyes leave the two strangers as he takes a deep breath.

In the background, Ryan notices the two kids wrapped in their mother's arms. Despite the intensity of the situation, he finds the sight rather heartwarming. Then all three people blink in sync and it makes him pause. The girl with the pink coat then stuffs her face into her mother's coat which eases any unfounded suspicion that Ryan may have had.

"I’ll give you the count of three to go back to the hole you came from or else I start shooting."

Ryan clenches his fist. He can't shake the feeling that this man's hostility and paternal instincts remind him of someone—some other father would would do anything to protect his family. Between the quit to anger attitude and sheer malice in his demeanor, it'd lead him to associated him with... Ryan shakes his head. This isn't the time.

“One!” he shouts.

Ryan begins slowly receding back into the alleyway. Blake glances over to him but doesn’t move.

“Two!” he yells.

"Come on," Ryan comments as he waves towards the alleyway.

Blake doesn't budge, "I'm not giving up on this," he then turns back to the shotgun with conviction, "I'm not your enemy."

Immediately the second man shoves the barrel of the gun down. The first man snaps his head over to him and tries to lift the gun again but the second man doesn’t let him.

“What are you doing?!” The first man argues shoving the other man back.

“They’re not from here,” the second responds, gesturing towards them, "Can't you tell? They probably have no idea what's going on."

The first man looks over Ryan and Blake slowly before taking a deep breath of the frigid winter air. The whole group takes a moment of silence while the man collects himself, but eventually he gathers up all his anger and releases it.

“I’m sorry,” he says dryly, more as a matter of fact than a true apology.

Ryan cautiously returns to Blake’s side.

The second man pulls down his scarf revealing short black scruff across his chin and a facial structure that is strikingly similar to the first man’s. He holds out his hand, “I’m Martin and this is my brother Mark.”

They exchange the pleasantry of a handshake while each confirming one another's name.

"Blake…," Blake says while shaking Mark's hand, eyeing the shotgun the whole time, "Do you mind explaining what's going on?"

Martin—the second man—nods his head and looks Ryan and Blake over before glancing back at the family behind, “Okay, but we can’t do it here.”

Moments later, Ryan and Blake find themselves in a small house a few roads down from that little gas station. From the exterior, the house looks completely harmless but upon entering through the front door, a variety of lethal contraptions beg to differ from that assumption. A tripwire lies on the floor in front of the door with a sharp blade hanging above. Just past that, a laser beam across the room connected to a shotgun pointing straight towards the door. Martin disarms both booby traps as they enter the house. The two kids run off down the stairs into the basement while Martin directs Ryan and Blake to the living room. The woman walks into another room while Mark sits down coldly in a rocking chair, his gun still laid out across his lap. Martin stands by the fireplace lighting some logs with a match.

“So you guys are from outside the city?” Martin asks, placing a few thin sticks on the growing fire.

“That’s right,” Blake responds, “We’re in the area until the snow thaws and then we’ll be off.”

Martin nods as he closes the glass door to the fireplace and takes a seat in a chair across from the sofa. He crosses his ankle over his knee as he leans back, "So you don't know anything about what's going on?"

Ryan and Blake both shake their heads.

"They’re a bunch of lousy vermin," Mark grunts clearly riled up just by the thought of the situation.

“In some ways yes,” Martin explains looking at his brother, “But why don't we start from the beginning. We just left the camp of survivors in this city and we have no intention of ever going back.”

Blake's eyes widen, "Survivors! You're saying there's others?"

Martin looks at him incredulously, "Yeah... but I wouldn't recommending seeking them out right now."

Ryan nods his head thoughtfully. He and Blake have only been exploring the city for a few days cumulatively and most of that was limited due to the hive in the museum. With so many zombies congregated in a single location, Blake didn't want to take any chances by getting close or going too far beyond it. In truth, they hadn't even explored most of the city yet, so other survivors were definitely a possibility. Perhaps they may have assumed that there wouldn't be anyone solely based on the fact that every piece of evidence leading up to that point would lead them to conclude that the city was abandoned.

"What's wrong with them?" Blake wonders.

“What’s wrong with em?!” Mark bursts out with a sudden lean forward.

Martin snaps at him quickly, "Mark! They don't know anything so cool it."

“The bad blood must run deep,” Ryan comments under his breath.

Ryan glances across the room and finds two pairs of young eyes staring at him. When he first realizes they're there, he finds them to be completely void of emotion as if shells staring at nothing. Then he blinks and life instantaneously returns to the children's faces as they browse the room with curiosity and fascination. Ryan shakes his head in an attempt to clear his miss leading thoughts.

Martin takes a deep breath as he places both his feet on the floor and leans forward, "This little town of ours used to be a tight knit community. We all trusted each other, even before the world went to shit... but unfortunately, the apocalypse doesn't care how much you trust each other."

Blake leans forward as well, "What do you mean?"

Taking another deep breath, Martin looks to the fireplace, "We've suffered under the brutal nature of these monsters. Although they don't tend to linger outside, any attempt at going just about anywhere resulted in some sort of conflict that got someone killed. We were at our wits end."

"And then the interlopers came," Marks asserts with a grim expression.

Martin nods, "A small group of strangers appeared out of nowhere. We're a rather open minded people so of course we welcomed them—we weren't going to make them kick rocks. Anyway, one of these... people... they made us an offer. They said, with our permission, they could help us with our problem—keep us safe from the monsters."

"And you believed them?" Blake doubts.

Both men shake their heads, Mark with a frown, Martin with an exasperated look.

"No," Martin asserts, "No one did... but then. Someone got hurt—real bad. Even the town physician determined that they were a lost cause... but not the stranger. When no one was looking, the stranger did something to her and she survived. Despite the severity of her injuries, the stranger managed to stabilize her and she ended up surviving."

"That's amazing," Ryan comments, getting an unsettling sense of déjà vu. 

"Right," Martin agrees, "And it's because of that situation that ultimately brought our poor little town to trusting these strangers. That, I think, was our undoing."

Blake and Ryan exchange glances. Blake then looks at Martin hesitantly as if contemplating whether or not the answer will be hard to hear, "What happened then?"

Mark growls and tightens his grip on his gun but doesn’t move otherwise. 

Martin takes a deep breath preparing to fully inform the two young men, "Then, little by little, the atmosphere of our little town started to change. At first it was Old man Dwight laughing more than he usually does. Then the strangers confiscated a building. After that, family friends of ours stopped visiting. And then..."

Martin’s gaze falls to the floor and his hands shake a bit. Mark’s face, on the other hand, is covered by an invisible shadow masking pure hatred. Ryan could feel real bloodlust emanating from him. 

“They sacrificed my wife,” Mark says coldly before returning to silence.

Both teens' eyes fly open and they lean forward in shock. 

“That’s right,” Martin adds coldly, “It was the middle of the night when they came into Mark’s house and kidnapped his wife. We found her impaled on a stake the next day.”

Martin’s sadness grows cold as the thought of a terrifying sight forces him into a disturbing state of mind. Mark remains in his brooding position holding his gun tightly, staring straight at the floor in front of him. 

Eventually Martin takes a deep breath, looks around the room, leans forward, and speaks softly, "Her body... they had done things to her—terrible things. Killing her was one thing, but this..." he averts his eyes, "It's sickening."

Chills roll through Ryan’s body as he imagines the horrifying scene of a loved one dying in such a manner. The idea reminds him of Jeremy’s mother, which makes him clench his fist and close his eyes for a moment.

"Why didn't you kick them out?" Blake points out, a bit rushed, "After something like that, obviously they should be reprimanded."

Martin bites his bottom lip for a moment, “It’s just like a story I once read about a perfect city. There was no pain, no sorrow, and no problems in the city but in order to obtain that perfection, that utopia, they had to condemn a child to unimaginable torture. And... for some reason, the town was okay with this.”

"Darius, Phil, Harrington, all people I considered friends!" Mark shouts, "They just accepted it, said it was necessary. I worked with Phil for fifteen years, and he can't even be bothered to mourn my wife!"

"Calm down," Martin says, hushing his brother, "We can't change what's happened. Whether we like it or not, those strangers have done something to this town that we're not certain about."

"And so you left because of that," Blake concludes.

"Sort of," Martin says, "No one would believe us, not matter what we said."

"I went right up to those bastards and gave them a piece of my mind," Mark recalls with a dark look of hatred, "I couldn't even lay a finger on the imp. He had the strength of ten men. I don't even think he's human."

"Ultimately I don't think there's anything that we could do," Martin summarizes with a deep sigh, "With everyone acting like strangers, we decide to just walk away. Save ourselves while there was still time."

Ryan sighs and leans back against the squishy couch. This whole situation is really… something.

“I think we’ll have to check this place out,” Blake finally decides after a long moment of silence. All eyes dart to him as he presses his hands together before continuing. "Unfortunately this isn't the first time we've dealt with sadistic monsters. Even if it's dangerous, I want to know what we're dealing with."

"I'm not so sure about that," Martin says hesitantly.

Ryan shakes his head, although he's just a skeptical, he's actually on Blake's side for this one. Even if it's dangerous, it's better if they know what's coming rather than waiting for it to come to them. "I think it's important that we see this for ourselves."

Blake nods, "Right, now where can we find everyone?"

"Stop acting tough kid," Mark comments dismissively as he glances out the blinds, "You're better off running."

"You might be right," Blake responds with a considerable amount of conviction, "but how do we know there's not more of these... strangers? For all we know, there could be more in the next town over. At least here, we know what we're getting ourselves into. Now where are they?"

The thought of cultists flashes through Ryan mind and it makes him shiver. Heimes is the only authentic "cultist" that he's dealt with, so his experience is admittedly limited. That being said, anomalous behavior with malicious effects seems to be something right up their alley. If it really is a cultist, perhaps avoiding them all together might actually be the right play. Drew's comment on their very nature still haunts him.

"Actually," Ryan starts, looking particularly at Blake, "We might want to think this through a little more."

"Thank you!" Mark exclaims.

Blake glances back swiftly with a defensive look, "What are you saying?"

With a shrug, Ryan leans a bit further back on the couch, "I'm not disagreeing with you, but we might not want to make such a hasty decision."

Blake purses his lips as he places his clasped hands against his chin. He mulls the options over very carefully in his head before promptly standing up, "We'll need to discuss this with our friends."

Martin nods his head thoughtfully, "You do whatever's best... and if you really must know, everyone lives on the East side of town—opposite of here. The West side had too many monsters."

Blake and Ryan exchange glances while the former makes his way for the door, "Thank you guys for the information, but I think it's about time we take our leave."

Seeing as the conversation has reached its natural conclusion, Ryan leans forward to get off the couch. As he does this, he notices the two children watching eagerly from the corner of the hallway—their youthful vigor easing his worry a bit. Behind them, through a cracked door, he notices the mother. It's hard to make out much given the door is only ajar by a few inches—only the width between one's eyes, but he believes he sees her standing directly in that crack. Her gaze unapologetically assaults him, not with malice, but with an indiscriminate blankness. His meandering sight doesn't dwell, but he does do a double take. On second glance, the mother harbors a warm smile as she inspects her kids instead of him. Equally so, her eyes no longer hold a blank expression but instead have a comfortable, almost nostalgic look to them. 

Ryan swallows hard as he stands up fully and follows Blake out.
